# Epilogue

## II. For a Moment, a Lie Became Truth

<!-- EP-C02-P001 -->
He hurried to the hospital, where Mitya now lay. On the second day after the verdict, Mitya had come down with a nervous fever and been sent to the prisoners’ ward of our town hospital. But at the request of Alyosha and several others—Madame Khokhlakov, Lise, and so on—Dr. Varvinsky had placed Mitya apart from the prisoners, in the very little room where Smerdyakov had lain before. There was, it is true, a sentry at the end of the corridor and bars on the window, so Varvinsky could feel easy about this not entirely lawful indulgence; but he was a kind and compassionate young man. He understood how hard it was for someone like Mitya to step straight into the company of murderers and swindlers, and that he first had to grow used to the idea. Visits from relatives and acquaintances were permitted by the doctor, the superintendent, and even the district police chief, all unofficially. Yet in these days only Alyosha and Grushenka had visited Mitya. Rakitin had already tried twice to see him, but Mitya had earnestly asked Varvinsky not to let him in.

<!-- EP-C02-P002 -->
Alyosha found him sitting on his bed in a hospital gown, slightly feverish, his head wrapped in a towel soaked in vinegar and water. He looked vaguely at Alyosha as he came in, though something like fear flashed through his eyes.

<!-- EP-C02-P003 -->
Ever since the trial, he had been terribly preoccupied. Sometimes he would sit silent for half an hour, apparently thinking something through with great difficulty and pain, forgetting whoever was with him. When he did emerge from his thoughts and begin speaking, he always started abruptly, and invariably spoke of something other than what he really needed to say. Sometimes he looked at his brother with suffering in his eyes. He seemed more at ease with Grushenka than with Alyosha. True, he scarcely spoke to her either, but as soon as she came in his whole face lit up with joy. Alyosha sat down silently beside him on the bed. This time Mitya had been waiting anxiously for Alyosha, but he did not dare ask anything. He thought it inconceivable that Katya would agree to come, and at the same time felt that if she did not come, something utterly impossible would happen. Alyosha understood his feelings.

<!-- EP-C02-P004 -->
“That Trifon,” Mitya began fussily, “that Borisych—they say he’s wrecked his whole inn: he’s lifting floorboards, tearing off planks; they say he’s smashed the whole ‘galdery’ to splinters—all hunting for buried treasure, for that money, the fifteen hundred the prosecutor said I hid there. They say he went straight to work on his rampage as soon as he got home. Serves the swindler right! The attendant here told me yesterday; he’s from there.”

<!-- EP-C02-P005 -->
“Listen,” said Alyosha, “she’ll come, but I don’t know when—perhaps today, perhaps in a few days; I don’t know that, but she will come, she will, that’s certain.”

<!-- EP-C02-P006 -->
Mitya started, was about to say something, but remained silent. The news affected him terribly. It was plain that he longed painfully to know the details of their conversation, but was again afraid to ask just now: anything cruel or contemptuous from Katya would have struck him like a knife at that moment.

<!-- EP-C02-P007 -->
“One thing she said was that I absolutely must set your conscience at rest about escaping. If Ivan hasn’t recovered by then, she’ll take it on herself.”

<!-- EP-C02-P008 -->
“You’ve told me that already,” Mitya remarked thoughtfully.

<!-- EP-C02-P009 -->
“And you’ve already told Grusha,” Alyosha observed.

<!-- EP-C02-P010 -->
“Yes,” Mitya admitted. “She won’t come this morning.” He looked timidly at his brother. “She’ll only come this evening. Yesterday, as soon as I told her Katya was making the arrangements, she said nothing; her lips twisted. She only whispered, ‘Let her!’ She understood it was important. I didn’t dare question her further. Surely she understands now, I think, that Katya loves Ivan, not me?”

<!-- EP-C02-P011 -->
“Does she?” Alyosha blurted out.

<!-- EP-C02-P012 -->
“Perhaps she doesn’t. Only she won’t come this morning,” Mitya hastened to make clear once more. “I gave her an errand... Listen, our brother Ivan will surpass us all. He’s the one who ought to live, not us. He’ll get well.”

<!-- EP-C02-P013 -->
“Would you believe it, though Katya’s frightened for him, she scarcely doubts that he’ll recover,” said Alyosha.

<!-- EP-C02-P014 -->
“That means she’s convinced he’ll die. It’s fear that makes her sure he’ll recover.”

<!-- EP-C02-P015 -->
“Our brother has a strong constitution. I too have great hopes that he’ll recover,” Alyosha said anxiously.

<!-- EP-C02-P016 -->
“Yes, he’ll recover. But she’s convinced he’ll die. She has a great deal of sorrow...”

<!-- EP-C02-P017 -->
There was a silence. Something very important was tormenting Mitya.

<!-- EP-C02-P018 -->
“Alyosha, I love Grusha terribly,” he suddenly said in a trembling voice full of tears.

<!-- EP-C02-P019 -->
“They won’t let her join you there,” Alyosha took him up at once.

<!-- EP-C02-P020 -->
“And here’s something else I wanted to tell you,” Mitya continued, his voice suddenly ringing out. “If they start beating me on the journey or there, I won’t submit; I’ll kill someone, and they’ll shoot me. And it’s twenty years! They’ve already started using the familiar ‘you’ with me here. The attendants call me that. I lay here all last night judging myself: I’m not ready! I haven’t the strength to accept it! I wanted to sing a ‘hymn,’ and I can’t even bear an attendant’s familiarity! For Grusha I’d endure anything, anything... except beatings, though... But they won’t let her go there.”

<!-- EP-C02-P021 -->
Alyosha smiled gently.

<!-- EP-C02-P022 -->
“Listen, brother, once and for all,” he said. “Here is what I think about it. And you know I won’t lie to you. Listen: you’re not ready, and a cross like that is not for you. More than that: since you aren’t ready, you have no need of a martyr’s cross like that. If you’d killed our father, I would regret your rejecting your cross. But you’re innocent, and such a cross is too much for you. You wanted to bring a different man to life within you through suffering; I think you need only remember that other man always, all your life, wherever you escape to—and that will be enough for you. Not having accepted that great suffering on the cross will only make you feel an even greater duty within you; and this unceasing sense of duty, from now on and all your life, may help your rebirth more than going there would. Because there you wouldn’t endure it, you’d rebel, and perhaps in the end you really would say, ‘I’ve paid my debt.’ The lawyer spoke the truth about that. Heavy burdens aren’t for everyone; for some they’re impossible... Those are my thoughts, if you need them so much. If others were left to answer for your escape—officers, soldiers—I ‘wouldn’t allow’ you to escape,” Alyosha smiled. “But they say, and assure us—the official at that staging post told Ivan himself—that if it’s managed properly, there may be no very severe punishment, and they can get off with something trifling. Of course bribery is dishonorable even in this case, but I absolutely refuse to judge that, precisely because if, for instance, Ivan and Katya asked me to make the arrangements for you, I know I’d go and pay the bribes; I have to tell you the whole truth. And so I’m no judge of what you do yourself. But know that I’ll never condemn you either. It would be strange, after all—how could I be your judge in this matter? Well, now I think I’ve considered everything.”

<!-- EP-C02-P023 -->
“But I’ll condemn myself!” Mitya exclaimed. “I’ll escape—that was decided even without you: could Mitka Karamazov possibly not escape? But I’ll condemn myself, and over there I’ll pray for forgiveness of my sin forever! That’s how the Jesuits talk, isn’t it? The way you and I are talking now, eh?”

<!-- EP-C02-P024 -->
“It is,” Alyosha said with a gentle smile.

<!-- EP-C02-P025 -->
“I love you because you always tell the whole, entire truth and hide nothing!” Mitya exclaimed with a joyful laugh. “So I’ve caught my Alyoshka being a Jesuit! I ought to smother you in kisses for that, that’s what! Well, now listen to the rest; I’ll lay open the other half of my soul to you. Here’s what I’ve thought up and decided: if I do escape, even with money and a passport, and even to America, I’m still heartened by the thought that I won’t be escaping to joy or happiness, but truly to another penal servitude, perhaps no less than this one! No less, Alexei, I tell you truly, no less! I already hate that America, damn it. Grusha will be with me, but look at her: is she an American? She’s Russian, Russian to her very bones; she’ll pine for her native mother earth, and every hour I’ll see that she’s pining because of me, that she’s taken such a cross upon herself for me—and what has she done wrong? And could I bear those louts over there, even if every last one of them may be better than I am? I hate that America already! Even if every last one of them is some sort of prodigious machinist or whatever—damn them, they’re not my people, they don’t share my soul! I love Russia, Alexei, I love the Russian God, though I’m a scoundrel myself! Why, I’ll die over there!” he suddenly cried, his eyes flashing. His voice trembled with tears.

<!-- EP-C02-P026 -->
“Well, here’s what I’ve decided, Alexei, listen!” he began again, mastering his emotion. “Grusha and I will get there—and straight to plowing and working, among the wild bears, in solitude, somewhere far away. Surely even there we’ll find somewhere far away! They say there are still redskins there, somewhere on the edge of their horizon—well, we’ll go to that edge, to the last of the Mohicans. And straight to our grammar, Grusha and I. Work and grammar, for about three years. In those three years we’ll learn English like the English themselves. And once we’ve learned it—goodbye, America! We’ll flee back here, to Russia, as American citizens. Don’t worry, we won’t turn up here in this little town. We’ll hide somewhere far away, in the north or the south. I’ll have changed by then, and so will she; over there in America a doctor will make me some sort of false wart—they’re mechanics for a reason. Failing that, I’ll put out one eye, grow a gray beard an arshin long—I’ll go gray with longing for Russia—and perhaps they won’t recognize me. If they do, let them send me into exile; it’s all the same, it means it wasn’t meant to be! Here too we’ll plow the land somewhere in the wilderness, and I’ll pretend to be an American all my life. But we’ll die on our native soil. That’s my plan, and it is immutable. Do you approve?”

<!-- EP-C02-P027 -->
“I do,” said Alyosha, not wanting to contradict him.

<!-- EP-C02-P028 -->
Mitya fell silent for a moment, then suddenly said:

<!-- EP-C02-P029 -->
“And the way they did me in at the trial! The way they did me in!”

<!-- EP-C02-P030 -->
“Even if they hadn’t, you’d still have been convicted,” Alyosha said with a sigh.

<!-- EP-C02-P031 -->
“Yes, the people here were sick of me! God be with them, but it’s hard!” Mitya groaned in anguish.

<!-- EP-C02-P032 -->
They fell silent again for a moment.

<!-- EP-C02-P033 -->
“Alyosha, put a knife in me now!” he suddenly exclaimed. “Is she coming now or not? Tell me! What did she say? How did she say it?”

<!-- EP-C02-P034 -->
“She said she would come, but I don’t know whether it’ll be today. It’s hard for her, you know!” Alyosha looked timidly at his brother.

<!-- EP-C02-P035 -->
“Of course it is, of course it’s hard! Alyosha, this will drive me mad. Grusha keeps looking at me. She understands. Lord God, humble me: what am I demanding? I’m demanding Katya! Do I understand what I’m demanding? Godless Karamazov recklessness! No, I’m incapable of suffering! A scoundrel, that says it all!”

<!-- EP-C02-P036 -->
“Here she is!” Alyosha exclaimed.

<!-- EP-C02-P037 -->
At that moment Katya suddenly appeared in the doorway. She paused for an instant, looking at Mitya with a lost expression. He sprang to his feet, his face showing fear; he turned pale, but at once a timid, pleading smile flickered on his lips, and suddenly, unable to help himself, he stretched out both hands to Katya. At the sight she rushed to him. She took his hands and almost forced him to sit on the bed, sat beside him herself, and, never letting go of his hands, squeezed them tightly, convulsively. Several times they both tried to say something, but stopped and again gazed silently and intently at each other, as though riveted there, with strange smiles; some two minutes passed like this.

<!-- EP-C02-P038 -->
“Have you forgiven me or not?” Mitya finally faltered, and at that very instant, turning to Alyosha with a face distorted by joy, he cried out to him:

<!-- EP-C02-P039 -->
“Do you hear what I’m asking? Do you hear?”

<!-- EP-C02-P040 -->
“That’s why I loved you, because you have a generous heart!” Katya suddenly burst out. “And you don’t need my forgiveness; I need yours. Whether you forgive me or not, you’ll remain a wound in my soul all my life, and I in yours—that’s how it should be...” She paused for breath.

<!-- EP-C02-P041 -->
“Why have I come?” she began again, hurriedly, in a frenzy. “To embrace your feet, to squeeze your hands like this, till it hurts—remember how I squeezed them in Moscow?—to tell you again that you’re my god, my joy, to tell you that I love you madly,” she almost moaned in anguish, and suddenly pressed her lips hungrily to his hand. Tears poured from her eyes.

<!-- EP-C02-P042 -->
Alyosha stood silent and disconcerted; he had not expected anything like what he was seeing.

<!-- EP-C02-P043 -->
“Love has passed, Mitya!” Katya began again. “But what has passed is so dear to me it hurts. Know that forever. But now, for just one little moment, let what might have been come true,” she faltered with a twisted smile, again looking joyfully into his eyes. “You love someone else now, and I love someone else, but I’ll still love you forever, and you’ll love me—did you know that? Do you hear? Love me, love me all your life!” she exclaimed, with an almost threatening tremor in her voice.

<!-- EP-C02-P044 -->
“I will love you, and... you know, Katya,” Mitya too began, catching his breath at every word, “you know, I loved you five days ago, that evening... When you fell and they carried you away... All my life! That’s how it will be, how it will be forever...”

<!-- EP-C02-P045 -->
So they babbled to each other, saying things almost senseless and frenzied, perhaps even untrue; but at that moment it was all true, and they believed themselves with all their hearts.

<!-- EP-C02-P046 -->
“Katya,” Mitya suddenly exclaimed, “do you believe I killed him? I know you don’t now, but then... when you testified... Did you really, really believe it?”

<!-- EP-C02-P047 -->
“I didn’t believe it then either! I never believed it! I hated you, and suddenly I convinced myself, just for that moment... When I testified... I convinced myself and believed it... and when I’d finished testifying, I stopped believing it again at once. You must know all of that. I’d forgotten I came here to punish myself!” she said, suddenly with an entirely new expression, quite unlike her babbling of love just a moment before.

<!-- EP-C02-P048 -->
“How you suffer, woman!” suddenly burst from Mitya, quite beyond his control.

<!-- EP-C02-P049 -->
“Let me go,” she whispered. “I’ll come again; it’s hard now!..”

<!-- EP-C02-P050 -->
She had begun to rise, but suddenly cried out loudly and drew back. Grushenka had entered the room unexpectedly, though quite quietly. No one was expecting her. Katya strode toward the door, but as she drew level with Grushenka she suddenly stopped, turned white as chalk, and quietly, almost in a whisper, moaned to her:

<!-- EP-C02-P051 -->
“Forgive me!”

<!-- EP-C02-P052 -->
Grushenka looked her straight in the face and, after a moment’s pause, replied in a venomous voice, poisoned with spite:

<!-- EP-C02-P053 -->
“We’re wicked, my dear, you and I! Both wicked! How could either of us forgive? Save him, and I’ll worship you all my life.”

<!-- EP-C02-P054 -->
“But you won’t forgive her!” Mitya shouted at Grushenka in a frenzy of reproach.

<!-- EP-C02-P055 -->
“Don’t worry, I’ll save him for you!” Katya whispered quickly, and ran from the room.

<!-- EP-C02-P056 -->
“How could you not forgive her, after she herself said to you, ‘Forgive me’?” Mitya again exclaimed bitterly.

<!-- EP-C02-P057 -->
“Mitya, don’t you dare reproach her, you have no right!” Alyosha shouted hotly at his brother.

<!-- EP-C02-P058 -->
“It was her proud lips speaking, not her heart,” Grushenka said with a kind of loathing. “If she frees you, I’ll forgive everything...”

<!-- EP-C02-P059 -->
She fell silent, as though suppressing something within her. She had not yet recovered herself. It turned out later that she had come in quite by chance, suspecting nothing at all and not expecting to find what she had found.

<!-- EP-C02-P060 -->
“Alyosha, run after her!” Mitya turned impulsively to his brother. “Tell her... I don’t know what... don’t let her go away like that!”

<!-- EP-C02-P061 -->
“I’ll come back before evening!” Alyosha called, and ran after Katya. He caught up with her outside the hospital grounds. She was walking fast, hurrying, but as soon as Alyosha reached her she said quickly:

<!-- EP-C02-P062 -->
“No, I can’t punish myself before that woman! I said ‘Forgive me’ because I wanted to punish myself to the very end. She didn’t forgive me... I love her for it!” Katya added in a distorted voice, her eyes flashing with savage spite.

<!-- EP-C02-P063 -->
“My brother wasn’t expecting her at all,” Alyosha began to murmur. “He was sure she wouldn’t come...”

<!-- EP-C02-P064 -->
“No doubt. Let’s leave it,” she cut him short. “Listen: I can’t go with you to the funeral now. I’ve sent them flowers for the little coffin. I think they still have money. If they need anything, tell them I’ll never abandon them in future... Well, leave me now, leave me, please. You’re late already; the bells are ringing for the late liturgy... Leave me, please!”
