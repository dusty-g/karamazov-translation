# Epilogue

## I. Plans to Save Mitya

<!-- EP-C01-P001 -->
On the fifth day after Mitya’s trial, very early in the morning, before nine, Alyosha came to Katerina Ivanovna’s to settle the details of a matter important to them both; he also had a message for her. She sat talking to him in the very room where she had once received Grushenka; in the next room Ivan Fyodorovich lay feverish and unconscious. Immediately after that scene in court, Katerina Ivanovna had ordered the sick, unconscious Ivan Fyodorovich brought to her house, disregarding all the inevitable gossip and condemnation that would follow. Of the two female relatives who lived with her, one had left for Moscow immediately after the scene in court; the other had stayed. But even if both had left, Katerina Ivanovna would not have changed her mind: she would have stayed to nurse the sick man, sitting with him day and night. Varvinsky and Herzenstube were treating him; the Moscow doctor had returned to Moscow, declining to offer a prognosis. The remaining doctors tried to encourage Katerina Ivanovna and Alyosha, but it was clear that they could not yet give them any firm hope. Alyosha visited his sick brother twice a day. This time, however, he had a particular and extremely troublesome errand, and he sensed how difficult it would be to broach it; meanwhile he was in a great hurry, for he had another urgent engagement elsewhere that same morning and had to make haste. They had been talking for about a quarter of an hour. Katerina Ivanovna was pale and very tired, yet at the same time in a state of intense, painful agitation: she sensed one of the reasons Alyosha had come to see her now.

<!-- EP-C01-P002 -->
“Don’t worry about his decision,” she told Alyosha with firm insistence. “One way or another, he’ll come to this in the end: he must escape! That unfortunate man, that hero of honor and conscience—not him, not Dmitri Fyodorovich, but the man lying behind that door, who sacrificed himself for his brother,” Katya added, her eyes flashing, “told me his whole escape plan long ago. You know, he’d already made contact with people... I’ve told you something about it already... You see, it will most likely happen at the third staging post from here, when the convoy of exiles is being taken to Siberia. Oh, that’s still a long way off. Ivan Fyodorovich has already been to see the man in charge of the third staging post. The only thing we don’t know is who will command the convoy, and you can’t find that out so far in advance. Perhaps tomorrow I’ll show you the whole detailed plan Ivan Fyodorovich left with me the day before the trial, in case anything happened... It was that very evening when you found us quarreling, remember? He was going down the stairs, and when I saw you I made him come back—do you remember? Do you know what we’d quarreled about?”

<!-- EP-C01-P003 -->
“No, I don’t,” said Alyosha.

<!-- EP-C01-P004 -->
“Of course he kept it from you then: it was this very escape plan. He’d told me all the main points three days before—that was when we began quarreling, and we quarreled for the whole three days. We quarreled because, when he told me that if Dmitri Fyodorovich was convicted he would escape abroad with that creature, I suddenly grew angry—I won’t tell you why; I don’t know why myself... Oh, of course it was the creature, that creature I was angry about, precisely because she too was going abroad with Dmitri!” Katerina Ivanovna suddenly exclaimed, her lips trembling with anger. “The instant Ivan Fyodorovich saw how angry I was about that creature, he thought I was jealous of her over Dmitri and therefore still loved Dmitri. That was our first quarrel. I refused to explain, I couldn’t ask forgiveness; it hurt me that a man like that could suspect me of still loving that... And this when I myself had told him plainly, long before, that I didn’t love Dmitri, that I loved him alone! I was angry with him only because I was angry with that creature! Three days later, that evening when you came in, he brought me a sealed envelope, which I was to open at once if anything happened to him. Oh, he foresaw his illness! He told me the envelope contained the details of the escape, and that if he died or became dangerously ill, I was to save Mitya on my own. At the same time he left me money, almost ten thousand—the very money the prosecutor mentioned in his speech, after someone told him Ivan Fyodorovich had sent it to be changed. I was suddenly terribly struck by the fact that, although Ivan Fyodorovich was still jealous and still convinced I loved Mitya, he hadn’t abandoned the idea of saving his brother, and was entrusting the rescue to me, to me myself! Oh, that was a sacrifice! No, Alexei Fyodorovich, you cannot fully understand such self-sacrifice! I was about to fall at his feet in reverence, but suddenly I thought he would take it simply as my joy at Mitya’s being saved—and he certainly would have thought that! The mere possibility of such an unjust thought on his part angered me so much that I flared up again and, instead of kissing his feet, made another scene! Oh, I’m so unhappy! That’s my nature—a terrible, miserable nature! Oh, you’ll see: I’ll do it, I’ll drive him too to leave me for someone easier to live with, as Dmitri did, but then... no, then I won’t be able to bear it, I’ll kill myself! And that time when you came in, when I called to you and told him to come back, as he came in with you I was so overcome with anger at the hateful, contemptuous look he suddenly gave me that—remember?—I shouted at you that it was he, he alone who’d convinced me his brother Dmitri was a murderer! I deliberately slandered him, to hurt him once more, but he never, never tried to convince me that his brother was a murderer; on the contrary, I, I myself tried to convince him! Oh, my fury is to blame for everything, everything! It was I, I who prepared the way for that accursed scene in court! He wanted to prove to me that he was noble and that, even if I loved his brother, he still wouldn’t destroy him out of revenge and jealousy. So he came forward in court... I caused it all, I alone am to blame!”

<!-- EP-C01-P005 -->
Katya had never made such confessions to Alyosha before, and he felt that she had now reached that pitch of unbearable suffering at which even the proudest heart painfully breaks its own pride and falls, overcome by grief. Oh, Alyosha knew another terrible cause of her present torment, however carefully she had concealed it from him in all these days since Mitya’s conviction; but for some reason it would have hurt him too much if she had resolved to prostrate herself so completely as to speak to him of that cause herself, here and now. She was suffering over her “betrayal” in court, and Alyosha sensed that her conscience was driving her to confess, to him in particular, to Alyosha, with tears and shrieks and hysterics, pounding on the floor. But he dreaded that moment and wanted to spare the suffering woman. This made the errand he had come on still harder. He spoke of Mitya again.

<!-- EP-C01-P006 -->
“It’s all right, it’s all right, don’t be afraid for him!” Katya began again, sharply and obstinately. “It’s all just a passing mood with him; I know him, I know that heart all too well. You can be sure he’ll agree to escape. And above all, it won’t be now; he’ll still have time to decide. Ivan Fyodorovich will be well by then and will manage everything himself, so there’ll be nothing for me to do. Don’t worry, he’ll agree to escape. Why, he’s agreed already: could he leave his creature behind? They won’t let her into penal servitude with him, so how can he not escape? It’s you he’s chiefly afraid of, afraid you won’t approve of the escape on moral grounds, but you must magnanimously allow it, if your sanction is so indispensable,” Katya added venomously. She paused and gave a mocking smile.

<!-- EP-C01-P007 -->
“He talks about some sort of hymns,” she began again, “and the cross he has to bear, some sort of duty—I remember Ivan Fyodorovich telling me a great deal about it then, and if you only knew how he spoke!” Katya suddenly exclaimed with irrepressible feeling. “If you only knew how he loved that unfortunate man as he told me about him, and how he hated him, perhaps at that very same moment! And I—oh, I listened to his account and his tears with a proud little smile! Oh, creature! I’m the creature, I am! I’m the one who brought on his fever! But the other one, the convicted one—is he ready to suffer?” Katya finished irritably. “Is suffering for a man like that? People like him never suffer!”

<!-- EP-C01-P008 -->
Something very like hatred and disgusted contempt sounded in those words. And yet she herself had betrayed him. “Well, perhaps it’s because she feels so guilty toward him that she hates him at times,” Alyosha thought. He wanted it to be only “at times.” He heard a challenge in Katya’s last words, but did not take it up.

<!-- EP-C01-P009 -->
“That’s why I sent for you today: I want you to promise you’ll persuade him yourself. Or do you too think it would be dishonorable to escape, unheroic, or whatever it is... un-Christian, perhaps?” Katya added, more challengingly still.

<!-- EP-C01-P010 -->
“No, it’s all right. I’ll tell him everything...” Alyosha murmured. “He’s asking you to come and see him today,” he suddenly blurted out, looking her steadily in the eyes. She shuddered all over and drew a little away from him on the sofa.

<!-- EP-C01-P011 -->
“Me... can that be possible?” she faltered, turning pale.

<!-- EP-C01-P012 -->
“It’s possible, and you must!” Alyosha began insistently, suddenly animated. “He needs you very much, just now. I wouldn’t have brought this up and tormented you before the time had come if it weren’t necessary. He’s ill, he’s like a man out of his mind, he keeps asking for you. He isn’t asking you to come and make peace with him; only to come and appear in the doorway. A great deal has happened to him since that day. He understands how immeasurably he has wronged you. He doesn’t want your forgiveness: ‘I can’t be forgiven,’ he says himself. He only wants you to appear in the doorway...”

<!-- EP-C01-P013 -->
“You’ve taken me so suddenly...” Katya faltered. “All these days I’ve had a feeling you would come with this... I knew he would send for me!.. It’s impossible!”

<!-- EP-C01-P014 -->
“Even if it’s impossible, do it. Remember, for the first time he’s been struck by how much he has insulted you—the first time in his life; he has never understood it so fully before! He says that if you refuse to come, ‘I’ll be unhappy now for the rest of my life.’ Do you hear? A man sentenced to twenty years of penal servitude still means to be happy—isn’t that pitiful? Think of it: you’ll be visiting an innocent man who’s been ruined,” Alyosha burst out defiantly. “His hands are clean, there’s no blood on them! For the sake of all the immeasurable suffering ahead of him, visit him now! Come, see him off into the darkness... stand in the doorway, that’s all... You must, you must do this!” Alyosha concluded, stressing the word “must” with extraordinary force.

<!-- EP-C01-P015 -->
“I must, but... I can’t,” Katya all but moaned. “He’ll look at me... I can’t.”

<!-- EP-C01-P016 -->
“Your eyes must meet. How will you live out your life if you don’t make up your mind now?”

<!-- EP-C01-P017 -->
“I’d rather suffer all my life.”

<!-- EP-C01-P018 -->
“You must come, you must come,” Alyosha repeated with the same relentless emphasis.

<!-- EP-C01-P019 -->
“But why today, why now?.. I can’t leave a sick man...”

<!-- EP-C01-P020 -->
“You can for a minute; it’s only a minute. If you don’t come, he’ll be down with a fever by tonight. I wouldn’t lie to you—have pity!”

<!-- EP-C01-P021 -->
“Have pity on me,” Katya reproached him bitterly, and began to cry.

<!-- EP-C01-P022 -->
“Then you’ll come!” Alyosha said firmly when he saw her tears. “I’ll go and tell him you’re coming at once.”

<!-- EP-C01-P023 -->
“No, don’t tell him, whatever you do!” Katya cried in alarm. “I’ll come, but don’t tell him beforehand, because I’ll come, but perhaps I won’t go in... I don’t know yet...”

<!-- EP-C01-P024 -->
Her voice broke off. She was breathing with difficulty. Alyosha rose to leave.

<!-- EP-C01-P025 -->
“What if I meet someone?” she suddenly said in a low voice, turning pale again.

<!-- EP-C01-P026 -->
“That’s why it has to be now, so you won’t meet anyone there. No one will be there, I promise you. We’ll be waiting,” he concluded insistently, and left the room.
