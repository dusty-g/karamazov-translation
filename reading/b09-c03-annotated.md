# Book IX: The Preliminary Investigation
## III. The Soul’s Journey Through Ordeals. The First Ordeal[^b09-c03-n001]

<!-- B09-C03-P001 -->
And so Mitya sat there, staring wildly around at everyone, unable to understand what they were saying to him. Suddenly he rose, flung up his arms, and shouted:

<!-- B09-C03-P002 -->
“I’m not guilty! I’m not guilty of this bloodshed! I’m not guilty of my father’s blood... I wanted to kill him, but I’m not guilty! It wasn’t me!”

<!-- B09-C03-P003 -->
He had scarcely shouted this when Grushenka rushed out from behind the curtains and fell straight at the police chief’s feet.

<!-- B09-C03-P004 -->
“It’s me, me, the wretch, I’m to blame!” she cried in a heartrending shriek, all in tears, reaching out her hands to them all. “He killed him because of me!.. I tormented him and drove him to it! I tormented that poor dead old man too, out of spite, and brought him to this! I’m to blame, I’m the first, I’m the chief one, I’m to blame!”

<!-- B09-C03-P005 -->
“Yes, you’re to blame! You’re the chief criminal! You wild creature, you debauched woman, you’re chiefly to blame!” the police chief shouted, shaking his hand at her threateningly, but now they quickly and firmly silenced him. The prosecutor even put his arms around him.

<!-- B09-C03-P006 -->
“This will be complete disorder, Mikhail Makarovich!” he cried. “You’re positively obstructing the investigation... ruining the case...” He was almost breathless.

<!-- B09-C03-P007 -->
“Take measures, take measures, take measures!” Nikolai Parfenovich, too, grew terribly heated. “Otherwise it’s absolutely impossible!..”

<!-- B09-C03-P008 -->
“Try us together!” Grushenka went on crying frantically, still on her knees. “Punish us together, I’ll go with him now even to execution!”

<!-- B09-C03-P009 -->
“Grusha, my life, my blood, my sacred treasure!” Mitya threw himself on his knees beside her and clasped her tightly in his arms. “Don’t believe her!” he cried. “She’s innocent of everything, of any bloodshed, of everything!”

<!-- B09-C03-P010 -->
He remembered afterward that several men had dragged him away from her by force, that she had suddenly been led away, and that when he came to himself he was already sitting at the table. Men with badges stood beside and behind him. Across the table, on the sofa, sat Nikolai Parfenovich, the judicial investigator, who kept urging him to drink a little water from the glass on the table. “It will refresh you, it will calm you; don’t be afraid, don’t worry,” he added with extreme politeness. Suddenly, Mitya remembered, he became terribly curious about the investigator’s large rings, one an amethyst, the other some bright yellow, transparent stone with such a beautiful sparkle. Long afterward he recalled with amazement that these rings irresistibly drew his eyes even throughout those terrible hours of questioning, so that for some reason he could not tear himself away from them and forget them, although they had so little to do with his situation. To Mitya’s left, in the place where Maximov had sat at the beginning of the evening, the prosecutor had now seated himself; at Mitya’s right, where Grushenka had been, sat a rosy-cheeked young man in a very worn jacket of some sort, rather like a hunting jacket, with an inkwell and paper before him. He proved to be the investigator’s clerk, whom he had brought with him. The police chief now stood by the window at the other end of the room, beside Kalganov, who had also sat down on a chair by that window.

<!-- B09-C03-P011 -->
“Have some water!” the investigator repeated gently for the tenth time.

<!-- B09-C03-P012 -->
“I’ve had some, gentlemen, I’ve had some... but... well, gentlemen, crush me, execute me, decide my fate!” Mitya exclaimed, fixing the investigator with a dreadful, motionless, bulging stare.

<!-- B09-C03-P013 -->
“Then you positively assert that you are not guilty of the death of your father, Fyodor Pavlovich?” the investigator asked gently but insistently.

<!-- B09-C03-P014 -->
“Not guilty! I’m guilty of other bloodshed, another old man’s blood, but not my father’s. And I mourn it! I killed, I killed the old man, killed him and struck him down... But it’s hard to answer for that blood with other blood, terrible blood I’m not guilty of... A terrible accusation, gentlemen, like a blow straight to the forehead! But who killed my father, who killed him? Who could have killed him if I didn’t? A miracle, an absurdity, an impossibility!..”

<!-- B09-C03-P015 -->
“Yes, precisely, who could have killed him...” the investigator began, but the prosecutor, Ippolit Kirillovich (the assistant prosecutor, though for brevity we too shall call him the prosecutor), exchanged a glance with the investigator and said to Mitya:

<!-- B09-C03-P016 -->
“You need not be anxious about the old servant Grigory Vasilyev. Let me inform you that he is alive, has regained consciousness, and, despite the grievous injuries you inflicted on him, according to his testimony and now your own, it appears certain he will survive—at least in the doctor’s opinion.”

<!-- B09-C03-P017 -->
“Alive? Then he’s alive!” Mitya suddenly cried, throwing up his hands. His whole face shone. “Lord, I thank you for the great miracle you have worked for me, sinner and evildoer that I am, in answer to my prayer!.. Yes, yes, in answer to my prayer, I prayed all night!..” And he crossed himself three times. He was almost breathless.

<!-- B09-C03-P018 -->
“Well, it is from this very Grigory that we have received such significant testimony concerning you that...” the prosecutor began to continue, but Mitya suddenly sprang from his chair.

<!-- B09-C03-P019 -->
“One minute, gentlemen, for God’s sake, just one minute; I’ll run to her...”

<!-- B09-C03-P020 -->
“Excuse me! That’s quite impossible just now!” Nikolai Parfenovich almost shrieked, jumping to his feet too. The men with badges on their chests seized Mitya, though he sat down again of his own accord...

<!-- B09-C03-P021 -->
“Gentlemen, what a pity! I wanted to go to her for just a moment... to tell her that this blood, which has been sucking at my heart all night, is washed away, has vanished, and that I’m no longer a murderer! Gentlemen, she’s my fiancée!” he suddenly said in rapture and reverence, looking around at them all. “Oh, thank you, gentlemen! Oh, how you’ve given me new life, how you’ve raised me from the dead in a single instant!.. That old man—why, he carried me in his arms, gentlemen, washed me in a tub when everyone abandoned me, a three-year-old child. He was a real father to me!”

<!-- B09-C03-P022 -->
“Then you...” the investigator began.

<!-- B09-C03-P023 -->
“One moment, gentlemen, give me just another minute,” Mitya interrupted, setting both elbows on the table and covering his face with his palms. “Let me gather myself a little, let me catch my breath, gentlemen. It’s all terribly shattering, terrible—a man isn’t a drumskin, gentlemen!”

<!-- B09-C03-P024 -->
“Perhaps a little more water...” Nikolai Parfenovich murmured.

<!-- B09-C03-P025 -->
Mitya took his hands from his face and laughed. His eyes were bright; he seemed completely changed in a single instant. His whole tone had changed too: here he sat again as the equal of all these people, all these old acquaintances, just as though they had all met somewhere in society yesterday, before anything had happened. Let us note in passing, however, that when Mitya first arrived among us he had been warmly received at the police chief’s, but later, particularly in the last month, he had scarcely visited him; when they met, in the street for instance, the police chief would frown heavily and acknowledge him merely out of politeness, something Mitya had noticed very well. He knew the prosecutor still more slightly, but sometimes paid visits—of the most respectful kind—to the prosecutor’s wife, a nervous lady full of fancies, without altogether understanding himself why he went to see her; she always received him kindly, and for some reason had continued to take an interest in him until very recently. He had not yet had time to become acquainted with the investigator, but had met him too and even spoken with him once or twice, on both occasions about the female sex.

<!-- B09-C03-P026 -->
“You’re a most skillful investigator, Nikolai Parfenych, I can see that,” Mitya suddenly laughed cheerfully, “but now I’ll help you myself. Oh, gentlemen, I’ve come back to life... and don’t take offense at my speaking to you so freely and directly. Besides, I’m a little drunk, I’ll tell you that frankly. I believe I’ve had the honor... the honor and pleasure of meeting you, Nikolai Parfenych, at my relative Miusov’s... Gentlemen, gentlemen, I’m not claiming equality; I do understand what I am as I sit before you now. I’m under... if Grigory has indeed testified against me... then I’m under—oh, of course I’m under—terrible suspicion! Horrible, horrible—I understand that! But to business, gentlemen, I’m ready, and we’ll finish this in a moment now, because, listen, listen, gentlemen. If I know I’m innocent, surely we’ll finish it in a moment! Won’t we? Won’t we?”

<!-- B09-C03-P027 -->
Mitya spoke rapidly and at length, nervously and expansively, as though quite determined to take his listeners for his best friends.

<!-- B09-C03-P028 -->
“For the present, then, we shall record that you categorically deny the accusation brought against you,” Nikolai Parfenovich said impressively, and, turning to the clerk, dictated in a low voice what he should write.

<!-- B09-C03-P029 -->
“Record it? You want to write it down? Well, write it down, I agree, you have my full consent, gentlemen... Only, you see... Wait, wait, write this: ‘He is guilty of violence, guilty of grievous injuries inflicted on the poor old man.’ Well, and there’s something else I’m guilty of in my own eyes, inwardly, in the depths of my heart—but you needn’t write that down,” he suddenly turned to the clerk. “That’s my private life, gentlemen, that’s no concern of yours, those depths of the heart, I mean... But I’m not guilty of murdering my old father! It’s a wild idea! An utterly wild idea!.. I’ll prove it to you and you’ll be convinced at once. You’ll laugh, gentlemen, you yourselves will roar with laughter at your suspicions!..”

<!-- B09-C03-P030 -->
“Calm yourself, Dmitri Fyodorovich,” the investigator reminded him, apparently hoping to subdue the frantic man with his own composure. “Before we continue the questioning, I should like, if you will agree to answer, to hear you confirm the fact that you apparently did not love the late Fyodor Pavlovich and were engaged in some sort of continual quarrel with him... Here, at least, a quarter of an hour ago, you seem to have stated that you even wanted to kill him: ‘I didn’t kill him,’ you exclaimed, ‘but I wanted to!’”

<!-- B09-C03-P031 -->
“Did I exclaim that? Oh, very possibly, gentlemen! Yes, unfortunately I wanted to kill him, wanted to many times... unfortunately, unfortunately!”

<!-- B09-C03-P032 -->
“You wanted to. Would you be willing to explain what principles, precisely, guided you in this hatred of your father’s person?”

<!-- B09-C03-P033 -->
“What is there to explain, gentlemen?” Mitya gave a gloomy shrug and lowered his eyes. “I never hid my feelings; the whole town knows, everyone in the tavern knows. Only recently I declared them in the monastery, in the elder Zosima’s cell... That same evening I beat my father and almost killed him, and swore I’d come back and kill him, before witnesses... Oh, a thousand witnesses! I’ve been shouting it all month, everyone’s a witness!.. The fact is there, the fact speaks, it cries out—but feelings, gentlemen, feelings are another matter. You see, gentlemen,” Mitya frowned, “I don’t think you have the right to ask me about my feelings. You’re invested with authority, I understand that, but this is my business, my inner business, something intimate, but... since I haven’t hidden my feelings before... in the tavern, for instance, and have told anyone and everyone, then... then I won’t make a secret of it now either. You see, gentlemen, I do understand that in this case the evidence against me is terrible: I told everyone I’d kill him, and suddenly he’s been killed—how could it be anyone but me, then? Ha-ha! I forgive you, gentlemen, I forgive you entirely. Why, I’m astonished down to my very epidermis myself, because who did kill him, after all, in that case, if it wasn’t me? Isn’t that so? If not me, then who, who? Gentlemen,” he suddenly exclaimed, “I want to know, I even demand that you tell me: where was he killed? How was he killed, with what, and how? Tell me,” he asked quickly, looking from the prosecutor to the investigator.

<!-- B09-C03-P034 -->
“We found him lying on his back on the floor of his study, with his skull smashed in,” the prosecutor said.

<!-- B09-C03-P035 -->
“That’s terrible, gentlemen!” Mitya suddenly shuddered and, leaning his elbow on the table, covered his face with his right hand.

<!-- B09-C03-P036 -->
“We shall continue,” Nikolai Parfenovich interrupted. “What, then, prompted these feelings of hatred? You appear to have declared publicly that it was jealousy?”

<!-- B09-C03-P037 -->
“Well, yes, jealousy, and not only jealousy.”

<!-- B09-C03-P038 -->
“Disputes over money?”

<!-- B09-C03-P039 -->
“Yes, over money too.”

<!-- B09-C03-P040 -->
“The dispute concerned, I believe, three thousand allegedly withheld from your inheritance.”

<!-- B09-C03-P041 -->
“Three! More, more!” Mitya flared up. “More than six, perhaps more than ten. I told everyone, shouted it to everyone! But I’d decided, so be it, to settle for three thousand. I desperately needed that three thousand... so that I considered that packet of three thousand, which I knew he had under his pillow, ready for Grushenka, as good as stolen from me, that’s what, gentlemen: I considered it mine, just as much my own property...”

<!-- B09-C03-P042 -->
The prosecutor exchanged a significant glance with the investigator and managed to wink at him unnoticed.

<!-- B09-C03-P043 -->
“We shall return to that subject,” the investigator said at once. “For now, allow us to note and record this particular little point: that you regarded the money in that envelope as, so to speak, your own property.”

<!-- B09-C03-P044 -->
“Write it down, gentlemen, I understand that this too is evidence against me, but I’m not afraid of evidence and I’m speaking against myself. Do you hear? Of my own accord! You see, gentlemen, I think you take me for an entirely different man from the one I am,” he suddenly added gloomily and sadly. “You’re speaking to an honorable man, a most honorable person; above all—don’t lose sight of this—a man who has done countless vile things but has always been and remained a most honorable being, as a being, inwardly, at bottom, well, in short, I can’t express it... That’s precisely what has tormented me all my life: I thirsted for honor, was, so to speak, a sufferer for honor and sought it with a lantern, with Diogenes’ lantern[^b09-c03-n003], yet all my life I’ve done nothing but filthy things, like all of us, gentlemen... that is, like me alone, gentlemen, not all of us, just me, I misspoke, me alone, alone!.. Gentlemen, my head hurts.” He grimaced in pain. “You see, gentlemen, I didn’t like his appearance, something dishonorable, the boasting and trampling on everything holy, the mockery and unbelief, loathsome, loathsome! But now that he’s dead I think differently.”

<!-- B09-C03-P045 -->
“In what way differently?”

<!-- B09-C03-P046 -->
“Not differently, but I’m sorry I hated him so much.”

<!-- B09-C03-P047 -->
“You feel remorse?”

<!-- B09-C03-P048 -->
“No, not exactly remorse, don’t write that down. I’m no good myself, gentlemen, that’s the point, I’m not so beautiful myself, and so I had no right to consider him repulsive, that’s it! You may write that down, if you like.”

<!-- B09-C03-P049 -->
After saying this, Mitya suddenly became extremely sad. For some time, as he answered the investigator’s questions, he had been gradually growing darker and darker. And suddenly, at this very moment, another unexpected scene broke out. Although Grushenka had been taken away earlier, she had not been taken very far, only to the third room along from the blue room where the questioning was now taking place. It was a small room with one window, just beyond the large room in which the dancing and the lavish revels had gone on during the night. There she sat, with only Maximov for company just then, terribly shaken and terribly frightened, clinging to her as though seeking safety beside her. Some peasant with a badge on his chest stood at their door. Grushenka was crying, and suddenly, when grief pressed too hard upon her soul, she sprang up, flung out her hands, and with a loud cry of “My grief, my grief!” rushed from the room toward him, toward her Mitya, so unexpectedly that no one had time to stop her. Hearing her cry, Mitya shook all over, sprang up, shouted, and rushed headlong to meet her, as though beside himself. But again they were not allowed to reach each other, although they had already caught sight of one another. They seized his arms firmly: he fought and struggled, and it took three or four men to hold him. They seized her too, and he saw her stretching her arms toward him and crying out as they led her away. When the scene was over he found himself once more in his former place at the table, opposite the investigator, shouting at them:

<!-- B09-C03-P050 -->
“What do you want with her? Why are you tormenting her? She’s innocent, innocent!..”

<!-- B09-C03-P051 -->
The prosecutor and investigator tried to calm him. Some time passed like this, about ten minutes; at last Mikhail Makarovich, who had gone out, hurried into the room and said loudly and excitedly to the prosecutor:

<!-- B09-C03-P052 -->
“She’s been moved, she’s downstairs. Would you allow me, gentlemen, just one word with this unhappy man? In your presence, gentlemen, in your presence!”

<!-- B09-C03-P053 -->
“By all means, Mikhail Makarovich,” the investigator answered. “Under the present circumstances we have no objection.”

<!-- B09-C03-P054 -->
“Dmitri Fyodorovich, listen, my dear fellow,” Mikhail Makarovich began, turning to Mitya, his whole agitated face expressing warm, almost fatherly compassion for the unfortunate man. “I’ve taken your Agrafena Alexandrovna downstairs myself and put her in the care of the innkeeper’s daughters; that old man Maximov is with her now and won’t leave her, and I’ve talked her round, do you hear? Talked her round and calmed her, made her understand that you have to clear yourself, so she mustn’t interfere, mustn’t distress you, or you might get confused and give an inaccurate account of yourself, do you understand? In short, I spoke to her and she understood. She’s a sensible girl, my friend, she’s good; she tried to kiss an old man’s hands, my hands, pleaded for you. She sent me here herself to tell you not to worry about her, and now, my dear fellow, I must, I must go and tell her that you’re calm and have been reassured about her. So calm yourself, understand that. I’ve wronged her; she’s a Christian soul, yes, gentlemen, a gentle soul, guilty of nothing. What shall I tell her, then, Dmitri Fyodorovich: will you sit quietly or not?”

<!-- B09-C03-P055 -->
The good man said a great deal more than necessary, but Grushenka’s grief, human grief, had entered his kind soul, and there were even tears in his eyes. Mitya sprang up and rushed toward him.

<!-- B09-C03-P056 -->
“Forgive me, gentlemen, allow me, oh, allow me!” he cried. “You’re an angelic, angelic soul, Mikhail Makarovich; thank you for her! I will, I’ll be calm, I’ll be cheerful. In your boundless kindness tell her I’m cheerful, cheerful, I’ll even start laughing now, knowing she has a guardian angel like you. I’ll finish everything at once, and as soon as I’m free I’ll go straight to her; she’ll see, let her wait! Gentlemen,” he suddenly turned to the prosecutor and investigator, “now I’ll open my whole soul to you, pour it all out, we’ll finish this in a moment, finish cheerfully—by the end we’ll be laughing, won’t we? But, gentlemen, that woman is the queen of my soul! Oh, let me say this, this much I will reveal to you... I can see I’m with most honorable people: she’s my light, my sacred treasure, and if you only knew! You heard her cries: ‘With you even to execution!’ And what have I given her, I, a beggar, a pauper? Why does she love me so, do I deserve it, a clumsy, shameful creature with a shameful face, such love that she’d go to penal servitude with me? For my sake she lay at your feet just now, she, so proud and innocent of everything! How can I help adoring her, crying out, rushing toward her as I did just now? Oh, gentlemen, forgive me! But now, now I’m comforted!”

<!-- B09-C03-P057 -->
He dropped onto his chair and, covering his face with both palms, burst into sobs. But these were happy tears now. In an instant he recovered himself. The old police chief was very pleased, and so, it seemed, were the lawyers: they felt that the questioning was about to enter a new phase. Having seen the police chief off, Mitya positively brightened.

<!-- B09-C03-P058 -->
“Well, gentlemen, now I’m yours, entirely yours. And... if it weren’t for all these little details, we’d come to an understanding at once. There I am talking about details again. I’m yours, gentlemen, but I swear we need mutual trust—yours in me and mine in you—or we’ll never get to the end of it. I’m saying this for your own sake. To business, gentlemen, to business, and above all, don’t dig about in my soul like this, don’t torment it with trifles; just ask about the case and the facts, and I’ll satisfy you at once. To hell with the details!”

<!-- B09-C03-P059 -->
So Mitya exclaimed. The questioning began again.

[^b09-c03-n001]: The title invokes a Christian tradition in which the soul meets spirits that accuse it of sins on its way upward after death.


[^b09-c03-n003]: In the traditional story, the Greek philosopher Diogenes carried a lit lantern in daylight, saying he was looking for a human being.
