# Book XI: Brother Ivan Fyodorovich
## III. A Little Devil

<!-- B11-C03-P001 -->
Entering Lise’s room, he found her reclining in her old chair, the one in which she had been wheeled about when she still could not walk. She did not move to greet him, but her sharp, watchful eyes fastened on him. There was something feverish in her gaze; her face was pale and yellow. Alyosha was astonished at how she had changed in three days; she had even grown thinner. She did not hold out her hand. He touched her slender, long fingers himself where they lay motionless on her dress, then sat down opposite her without a word.

<!-- B11-C03-P002 -->
“I know you’re hurrying to the prison,” Lise said abruptly, “and Mama’s kept you for two hours. She’s just told you about me and Yulia.”

<!-- B11-C03-P003 -->
“How did you know?” Alyosha asked.

<!-- B11-C03-P004 -->
“I was listening at the door. What are you staring at? I want to listen, so I listen; there’s nothing wrong with it. I’m not asking forgiveness.”

<!-- B11-C03-P005 -->
“Has something upset you?”

<!-- B11-C03-P006 -->
“Quite the opposite, I’m very glad. Just now I was thinking again, for the thirtieth time, how good it is that I turned you down and won’t be your wife. You’re no good as a husband. I’d marry you, and then give you a note to take to the man I fell in love with after you, and you’d take it and be sure to deliver it, and even bring back an answer. You’d be forty, and still carrying those notes of mine.”

<!-- B11-C03-P007 -->
She suddenly laughed.

<!-- B11-C03-P008 -->
“There’s something spiteful in you, and at the same time something innocent,” Alyosha said, smiling at her.

<!-- B11-C03-P009 -->
“The innocent part is that I’m not ashamed in front of you. Not only am I not ashamed, I don’t want to be ashamed, in front of you in particular, you of all people. Alyosha, why don’t I respect you? I love you very much, but I don’t respect you. If I respected you, I couldn’t speak without being ashamed, could I?”

<!-- B11-C03-P010 -->
“Yes.”

<!-- B11-C03-P011 -->
“And do you believe I’m not ashamed in front of you?”

<!-- B11-C03-P012 -->
“No, I don’t.”

<!-- B11-C03-P013 -->
Lise laughed nervously again; she spoke quickly, hurriedly.

<!-- B11-C03-P014 -->
“I sent sweets to your brother Dmitri Fyodorovich in prison. Alyosha, do you know how pretty you are! I’m going to love you terribly for letting me stop loving you so quickly.”

<!-- B11-C03-P015 -->
“Why did you send for me today, Lise?”

<!-- B11-C03-P016 -->
“I wanted to tell you something I wish for. I want someone to torment me, marry me and then torment me, deceive me, leave me, and go away. I don’t want to be happy!”

<!-- B11-C03-P017 -->
“Have you grown fond of disorder?”

<!-- B11-C03-P018 -->
“Oh, I want disorder. I keep wanting to set fire to the house. I imagine going up and setting it alight secretly—it has to be secretly. They try to put it out, and it keeps burning. And I know, but I say nothing. Oh, nonsense! How boring it all is!”

<!-- B11-C03-P019 -->
She waved her hand in disgust.

<!-- B11-C03-P020 -->
“You live in such comfort,” Alyosha said quietly.

<!-- B11-C03-P021 -->
“Would it be better to be poor?”

<!-- B11-C03-P022 -->
“Yes.”

<!-- B11-C03-P023 -->
“Your dead monk told you that. It’s not true. Let me be rich and everyone else poor; I’ll eat sweets and drink cream, and I won’t give any to anyone. Oh, don’t speak, don’t say anything!” She waved her hand, although Alyosha had not opened his mouth. “You’ve told me all that before; I know it by heart. It’s boring. If I’m poor, I’ll kill someone—and if I’m rich, perhaps I’ll kill someone too. Why just sit here! You know, I want to reap, to reap rye. I’ll marry you, and you become a peasant, a real peasant, and we’ll have a little foal. Shall we? Do you know Kalganov?”

<!-- B11-C03-P024 -->
“Yes.”

<!-- B11-C03-P025 -->
“He goes about dreaming all the time. He says: why live for real? Better to dream. You can dream up the most delightful things, but living is a bore. And yet he’ll get married soon himself; he’s already declared his love to me too. Can you spin a whipping top?”

<!-- B11-C03-P026 -->
“Yes.”

<!-- B11-C03-P027 -->
“That’s what he’s like, a top: wind him up and let him go, and whip, whip, whip him with the little whip. I’ll marry him and keep him spinning all his life. Aren’t you ashamed to sit here with me?”

<!-- B11-C03-P028 -->
“No.”

<!-- B11-C03-P029 -->
“You’re terribly angry because I’m not talking about holy things. I don’t want to be holy. What do they do to you in the next world for the greatest sin of all? You ought to know that exactly.”

<!-- B11-C03-P030 -->
“God will judge.” Alyosha looked searchingly at her.

<!-- B11-C03-P031 -->
“That’s just what I want. I’d arrive and they’d condemn me, and then I’d suddenly laugh in all their faces. I want terribly to set fire to the house, Alyosha, our house. You still don’t believe me?”

<!-- B11-C03-P032 -->
“Why not? There are even children, about twelve years old, who badly want to set something on fire, and they do. It’s a kind of illness.”

<!-- B11-C03-P033 -->
“Not true, not true. There may be children like that, but that isn’t what I mean.”

<!-- B11-C03-P034 -->
“You’re taking evil for good. It’s a passing crisis; your earlier illness may be responsible.”

<!-- B11-C03-P035 -->
“So you do despise me! I simply don’t want to do good, I want to do evil, and there’s no illness about it.”

<!-- B11-C03-P036 -->
“Why do evil?”

<!-- B11-C03-P037 -->
“So there’ll be nothing left anywhere. Oh, how good it would be if nothing were left! You know, Alyosha, sometimes I think of doing a terrible lot of evil and all sorts of foul things. I’ll do them secretly for a long time, and suddenly everyone will find out. They’ll all surround me and point their fingers at me, and I’ll look at them all. That’s very pleasant. Why is it so pleasant, Alyosha?”

<!-- B11-C03-P038 -->
“Yes. The need to crush something good, or to set fire to something, as you said. That happens too.”

<!-- B11-C03-P039 -->
“I’m not just saying it, you know. I’ll do it.”

<!-- B11-C03-P040 -->
“I believe you.”

<!-- B11-C03-P041 -->
“Oh, how I love you for saying, ‘I believe you.’ And you’re not lying, not at all. Or perhaps you think I’m saying all this on purpose to tease you?”

<!-- B11-C03-P042 -->
“No, I don’t… though perhaps there’s a little of that need in it too.”

<!-- B11-C03-P043 -->
“A little. I’ll never lie to you,” she said, her eyes flashing with a strange fire.

<!-- B11-C03-P044 -->
What struck Alyosha most was her seriousness: there was not a trace of laughter or playfulness in her face now, although before, gaiety and playfulness had never deserted her even in her most “serious” moments.

<!-- B11-C03-P045 -->
“There are moments when people love crime,” Alyosha said thoughtfully.

<!-- B11-C03-P046 -->
“Yes, yes! You’ve said what I was thinking. They love it, everyone loves it, and always, not just at ‘moments.’ You know, it’s as though everyone once agreed to lie about it, and they’ve all been lying ever since. Everyone says they hate evil, but secretly they all love it.”

<!-- B11-C03-P047 -->
“Are you still reading bad books?”

<!-- B11-C03-P048 -->
“Yes. Mama reads them and hides them under her pillow, and I steal them.”

<!-- B11-C03-P049 -->
“Aren’t you ashamed to ruin yourself?”

<!-- B11-C03-P050 -->
“I want to ruin myself. There’s a boy here who lay under the rails while the train went over him. Lucky boy! Listen, they’re trying your brother now for killing his father, and everyone loves the fact that he killed his father.”

<!-- B11-C03-P051 -->
“They love the fact that he killed his father?”

<!-- B11-C03-P052 -->
“Loves it, everyone loves it! Everyone says it’s terrible, but secretly they love it terribly. I’m the first to love it.”

<!-- B11-C03-P053 -->
“There’s some truth in what you say about everyone,” Alyosha said quietly.

<!-- B11-C03-P054 -->
“Oh, what thoughts you have!” Lise squealed in delight. “And you a monk! You wouldn’t believe how I respect you, Alyosha, for never lying. Oh, I’ll tell you a funny dream I have. Sometimes I dream about devils: it’s night, I’m in my room with a candle, and suddenly there are devils everywhere, in every corner and under the table, and they open the door, and there’s a crowd of them outside it, wanting to come in and seize me. They’re coming closer, they’re already seizing me. Suddenly I cross myself, and they all draw back, frightened; only they don’t go away altogether, they stand by the door and in the corners, waiting. And suddenly I feel a terrible desire to start cursing God out loud, so I begin cursing him, and all at once they crowd toward me again, so delighted, and they’re already seizing me again, and suddenly I cross myself again—and they all draw back. It’s terribly exciting; it takes my breath away.”

<!-- B11-C03-P055 -->
“I’ve had that very dream too,” Alyosha said suddenly.

<!-- B11-C03-P056 -->
“Really?” Lise cried in surprise. “Listen, Alyosha, don’t laugh, this is terribly important: can two different people have the same dream?”

<!-- B11-C03-P057 -->
“Apparently they can.”

<!-- B11-C03-P058 -->
“Alyosha, I tell you, this is terribly important,” Lise continued in something like excessive astonishment. “The dream isn’t important, but the fact that you could have the very same dream as me. You never lie to me; don’t lie now. Is it true? You’re not laughing?”

<!-- B11-C03-P059 -->
“It’s true.”

<!-- B11-C03-P060 -->
Something had struck Lise profoundly, and she fell silent for half a minute.

<!-- B11-C03-P061 -->
“Alyosha, come to see me, come more often,” she suddenly said in a pleading voice.

<!-- B11-C03-P062 -->
“I’ll always come to see you, all my life,” Alyosha answered firmly.

<!-- B11-C03-P063 -->
“You’re the only one I tell,” Lise began again. “I tell myself, and you. Only you in the whole world. And I’d rather tell you than myself. I’m not ashamed in front of you at all. Alyosha, why am I not ashamed in front of you at all, at all? Alyosha, is it true that the Yids steal children at Easter and cut their throats?”[^b11-c03-n001]

<!-- B11-C03-P064 -->
“I don’t know.”

<!-- B11-C03-P065 -->
“I have a book here. I read about some trial somewhere, and how a Yid first cut all the fingers off both hands of a four-year-old boy, then crucified him on a wall, nailed him up and crucified him, and afterward said at the trial that the boy died quickly, in four hours. Some quickly! He said the boy moaned, kept moaning, while he stood there admiring him. That’s good!”

<!-- B11-C03-P066 -->
“Good?”

<!-- B11-C03-P067 -->
“Good. Sometimes I imagine I crucified him myself. He hangs there moaning, and I sit down opposite him and eat pineapple compote. I love pineapple compote. Do you?”

<!-- B11-C03-P068 -->
Alyosha said nothing and looked at her. Her pale yellow face suddenly twisted, and her eyes blazed.

<!-- B11-C03-P069 -->
“You know, when I read about that Yid, I shook with tears all night. I imagine the little child crying and moaning—four-year-old boys do understand—and that thought about the compote won’t leave me alone. In the morning I sent a letter to someone, telling him he must come to see me. He came, and suddenly I told him about the boy and the compote, told him everything, everything, and said, ‘That’s good.’ He suddenly laughed and said it really was good. Then he got up and left. He’d only been here five minutes. Did he despise me, despise me? Tell me, tell me, Alyosha, did he despise me or not?” She straightened up on the couch, her eyes flashing.

<!-- B11-C03-P070 -->
“Tell me,” Alyosha said in agitation, “did you send for him yourself, this person?”

<!-- B11-C03-P071 -->
“Myself.”

<!-- B11-C03-P072 -->
“You sent him a letter?”

<!-- B11-C03-P073 -->
“A letter.”

<!-- B11-C03-P074 -->
“Specifically to ask about this, about the child?”

<!-- B11-C03-P075 -->
“No, not about that at all, not at all. But as soon as he came in, I asked him about it. He answered, laughed, got up, and left.”

<!-- B11-C03-P076 -->
“This person was honest with you,” Alyosha said quietly.

<!-- B11-C03-P077 -->
“But did he despise me? Was he laughing at me?”

<!-- B11-C03-P078 -->
“No, because perhaps he believes in the pineapple compote himself. He’s very ill now too, Lise.”

<!-- B11-C03-P079 -->
“Yes, he believes in it!” Lise’s eyes flashed.

<!-- B11-C03-P080 -->
“He despises no one,” Alyosha went on. “He just believes in no one. And if he doesn’t believe in them, then of course he despises them.”

<!-- B11-C03-P081 -->
“So me too? Me?”

<!-- B11-C03-P082 -->
“You too.”

<!-- B11-C03-P083 -->
“That’s good,” Lise said with a sort of grinding sound. “When he went out and laughed, I felt it was good to be despised. And the boy with his fingers cut off is good, and being despised is good…”

<!-- B11-C03-P084 -->
She laughed straight into Alyosha’s face, spitefully and feverishly.

<!-- B11-C03-P085 -->
“You know, Alyosha, you know, I’d like… Alyosha, save me!” She suddenly sprang from the couch, rushed to him, and threw her arms tightly around him. “Save me,” she almost moaned. “Could I tell anyone else in the world what I’ve told you? And I was telling the truth, the truth, the truth! I’ll kill myself because everything disgusts me! I don’t want to live because everything disgusts me! Everything disgusts me, everything! Alyosha, why don’t you love me at all, at all!” she finished in a frenzy.

<!-- B11-C03-P086 -->
“But I do love you!” Alyosha answered fervently.

<!-- B11-C03-P087 -->
“And will you cry for me, will you?”

<!-- B11-C03-P088 -->
“Yes.”

<!-- B11-C03-P089 -->
“Not because I wouldn’t be your wife, but just cry for me, just for me?”

<!-- B11-C03-P090 -->
“Yes.”

<!-- B11-C03-P091 -->
“Thank you! Your tears are all I need. Let everyone else put me to death and crush me underfoot, everyone, everyone, without a single exception! Because I don’t love anyone. Do you hear? No one! I hate them instead! Go, Alyosha, it’s time to see your brother!” She suddenly tore herself away from him.

<!-- B11-C03-P092 -->
“How can I leave you like this?” Alyosha said, almost frightened.

<!-- B11-C03-P093 -->
“Go to your brother, they’ll lock the prison. Go, here’s your hat! Kiss Mitya, go, go!”

<!-- B11-C03-P094 -->
She almost shoved Alyosha forcibly through the doorway. He was looking at her in sorrowful bewilderment when suddenly he felt a letter in his right hand, a little letter, firmly folded and sealed. He looked down and instantly read the address: Ivan Fyodorovich Karamazov. He glanced quickly at Lise. Her face had become almost threatening.

<!-- B11-C03-P095 -->
“Give it to him, you must give it to him!” she commanded in a frenzy, shaking all over. “Today, this minute! Or I’ll poison myself! That’s why I sent for you!”

<!-- B11-C03-P096 -->
She quickly slammed the door. The bolt clicked. Alyosha put the letter in his pocket and went straight to the stairs without stopping at Madame Khokhlakov’s; he had even forgotten her. As soon as Alyosha had gone, Lise immediately drew back the bolt, opened the door a tiny crack, put her finger in the gap, and slammed the door, crushing it with all her strength. After about ten seconds she freed her hand and walked quietly, slowly to her chair, sat down quite straight, and began staring at her blackened little finger and the blood that had forced its way out from under the nail. Her lips trembled, and quickly, quickly she whispered to herself:

<!-- B11-C03-P097 -->
“Vile, vile, vile, vile!”

[^b11-c03-n001]: Lise repeats the blood libel, a false antisemitic accusation that Jews murder Christian children for ritual purposes. It is her reported claim, not a fact.
