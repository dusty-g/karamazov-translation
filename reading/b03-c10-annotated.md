# Book III: The Lustful
## X. The Two Together

<!-- B03-C10-P001 -->
Alyosha left his father's house more crushed and dispirited than he had been when he entered it earlier. His mind, too, seemed shattered and scattered, and at the same time he felt afraid to gather the pieces and find a single meaning in all the painful contradictions he had lived through that day. Something in him bordered on despair, a feeling Alyosha's heart had never known before. Above everything, like a mountain, loomed the chief question, fateful and insoluble: how would it end between his father and his brother Dmitri, with that terrible woman between them? Now he had witnessed it himself. He had been there and seen them face each other. Yet it was only his brother Dmitri who could end up unhappy, utterly and terribly unhappy: certain disaster lay in wait for him. Other people, too, had turned out to be involved in all this, perhaps far more deeply than Alyosha could have imagined before. There was even something mysterious about it. His brother Ivan had taken a step toward him, as Alyosha had wished for so long, and now, for some reason, he felt frightened by that move toward closeness. And those women? Strangely enough, earlier he had set out for Katerina Ivanovna's in great embarrassment; now he felt none at all. On the contrary, he was hurrying to her of his own accord, as though expecting to find guidance there. And yet delivering his message was plainly harder now than it had been earlier: the question of the three thousand had been settled once and for all, and his brother Dmitri, now feeling himself dishonored and utterly without hope, would surely shrink from no further degradation. Besides, he had told him to report the scene that had just taken place at their father's.

<!-- B03-C10-P002 -->
It was already seven o'clock and growing dark when Alyosha went to Katerina Ivanovna's. She occupied a very spacious, comfortable house on Bolshaya Street. Alyosha knew she lived with two aunts. One, however, was an aunt only to her sister Agafya Ivanovna: she was the silent woman in her father's house who had cared for her there, along with her sister, when she came to them from boarding school. The other was a fashionable, dignified Moscow lady, though poor. It was said that both deferred to Katerina Ivanovna in everything and stayed with her solely for propriety's sake. Katerina Ivanovna herself deferred only to her benefactress, the general's wife, who had remained in Moscow because of illness and to whom she was obliged to send two letters every week with detailed news of herself.

<!-- B03-C10-P003 -->
When Alyosha entered the hall and asked the maid who had opened the door to announce him, those in the drawing room evidently already knew he had arrived—perhaps they had spotted him through the window. He suddenly heard a commotion, the sound of women's running footsteps and rustling dresses: perhaps two or three women had run out. Alyosha thought it strange that his arrival could cause such a stir. He was shown into the drawing room at once, however. It was a large room, lavishly furnished with elegant pieces, quite unlike anything provincial. There were many sofas and couches, settees, large and small tables; pictures hung on the walls, vases and lamps stood on the tables, there were flowers everywhere and even an aquarium by the window. The twilight left the room rather dim. Alyosha made out a silk mantilla thrown onto a sofa where someone had evidently just been sitting. On the table in front of it stood two half-finished cups of chocolate, biscuits, a crystal plate of dark raisins and another of sweets. Someone had been entertained. Alyosha realized he had arrived during a visit and winced. At that moment, though, the curtain over the doorway lifted and Katerina Ivanovna hurried in with quick steps, holding out both hands to him with a joyful, delighted smile. At the same moment a maid brought in two lighted candles and set them on the table.

<!-- B03-C10-P004 -->
“Thank God, you're here at last! All day I've been praying to God for you alone! Sit down.”

<!-- B03-C10-P005 -->
Katerina Ivanovna's beauty had struck Alyosha before, when his brother Dmitri first brought him to meet her about three weeks earlier, at her own very earnest wish. They had not really fallen into conversation on that visit, however. Assuming Alyosha was very embarrassed, Katerina Ivanovna seemed to spare him and spent the whole visit talking to Dmitri Fyodorovich. Alyosha said nothing, but took in a great deal. He was struck by the haughty girl's commanding manner, her proud ease, her self-assurance. All of it was unmistakable. Alyosha felt he was not exaggerating. He found her large, dark, glowing eyes beautiful and especially suited to her pale, even slightly sallow, elongated face. But in those eyes, and in the shape of her lovely lips, there was something his brother could certainly fall desperately in love with, yet perhaps could not love for long. He had almost said as much to Dmitri when, after the visit, his brother pressed him and begged him not to conceal his impression of his fiancée.

<!-- B03-C10-P006 -->
“You'll be happy with her, but perhaps… not peacefully happy.”

<!-- B03-C10-P007 -->
“That's just it, brother, people like that stay as they are; they don't bow to fate. So you think I won't love her forever?”

<!-- B03-C10-P008 -->
“No, perhaps you will love her forever, but perhaps you won't always be happy with her…”

<!-- B03-C10-P009 -->
Alyosha had given his opinion blushing and annoyed with himself for yielding to his brother's pleas and expressing such “stupid” thoughts. For his own opinion seemed terribly stupid to him the moment he had uttered it. He was ashamed, too, of pronouncing so authoritatively on a woman. All the more astonishing, then, was the feeling he had now, at his first glimpse of Katerina Ivanovna hurrying toward him, that perhaps he had been very wrong. This time her face shone with genuine, unassuming kindness, with frank and ardent sincerity. Of all the former “pride and haughtiness” that had so struck Alyosha, only a bold, noble energy remained, and a kind of clear, powerful faith in herself. At his first glance, from her first words, Alyosha understood that the full tragedy of her position with the man she loved so much was no secret to her; perhaps she already knew everything, absolutely everything. And yet, despite it all, there was so much light in her face, so much faith in the future. Alyosha suddenly felt he had gravely and deliberately wronged her. He was won over and drawn to her at once. Beyond all this, he noticed from her very first words that she was in a state of great excitement, perhaps quite unusual for her—an excitement almost like rapture.

<!-- B03-C10-P010 -->
“I've been waiting so anxiously because you're the only person I can learn the whole truth from now—no one else!”

<!-- B03-C10-P011 -->
“I came…” Alyosha faltered, confused. “I… he sent me…”

<!-- B03-C10-P012 -->
“Ah, he sent you. Just as I felt he would. Now I know everything, everything!” Katerina Ivanovna cried, her eyes suddenly flashing. “Wait, Alexei Fyodorovich, first I'll tell you why I was waiting so anxiously. You see, perhaps I know much more than even you do; it isn't news I need from you. This is what I need: I must know your own, personal, latest impression of him. I need you to tell me in the most direct, unvarnished, even brutal way—oh, as brutal as you like!—how you yourself see him now, and his situation, after your meeting with him today. Perhaps that will be better than talking to him face to face myself, when he won't come to see me anymore. Do you understand what I want from you? Now, what message did he send you with? I knew he would send you! Speak plainly, tell me his very last word!”

<!-- B03-C10-P013 -->
“He asked me to give you… his regards, and to say he would never come again… and sends his regards.”

<!-- B03-C10-P014 -->
“His regards? Did he say that, use those very words?”

<!-- B03-C10-P015 -->
“Yes.”

<!-- B03-C10-P016 -->
“Perhaps in passing, by accident—he misspoke, used the wrong word?”

<!-- B03-C10-P017 -->
“No, he specifically told me to pass on those words: ‘my regards.’ He asked me three times not to forget.”

<!-- B03-C10-P018 -->
Katerina Ivanovna flushed.

<!-- B03-C10-P019 -->
“Help me now, Alexei Fyodorovich. This is where I need your help. I'll tell you what I think, and you need only tell me whether I'm right. Listen: if he'd sent me his regards in passing, without insisting on those words, without stressing them, that would have been everything… It would have been the end! But if he laid particular stress on those words, if he specially charged you not to forget to give me his regards, then he must have been agitated, beside himself, perhaps? He made up his mind and frightened himself with his decision! He didn't walk away from me with a firm step; he hurtled down a mountain. That stress on those words may be nothing but bravado…”

<!-- B03-C10-P020 -->
“Yes, yes!” Alyosha agreed warmly. “That's what I think now too.”

<!-- B03-C10-P021 -->
“And if so, he isn't lost yet! He's only in despair, and I can still save him. Wait: did he tell you anything about the money, the three thousand?”

<!-- B03-C10-P022 -->
“He didn't just mention it; perhaps it was tormenting him more than anything. He said he had no honor left, and now nothing mattered,” Alyosha answered fervently, feeling with his whole heart the hope pouring into it, feeling that there might indeed be a way out, a way to save his brother. “But do you… know about that money?” he added, then suddenly stopped short.

<!-- B03-C10-P023 -->
“I've known a long time, and I know for certain. I sent a telegram to Moscow to ask, and I've known for a long time that the money never arrived. He didn't send it, but I said nothing. In this past week I've learned how badly he needed more money, and still does… I had just one aim in all this: that he should know whom to come back to and who his truest friend was. No, he won't believe I'm his truest friend, he wouldn't get to know me, he sees me only as a woman. All week one terrible worry has tormented me: how could I keep him from being ashamed before me of squandering those three thousand? Let him feel ashamed before everyone else, even himself, but not before me. After all, he tells God everything without shame. Why does he still not know how much I can bear for him? Why, why doesn't he know me, how dare he not know me after everything that's happened? I want to save him forever. Let him forget me as his fiancée! And now he's afraid for his honor in my eyes! He wasn't afraid to open his heart to you, Alexei Fyodorovich, was he? Why haven't I earned the same by now?”

<!-- B03-C10-P024 -->
She spoke the last words in tears; they sprang from her eyes.

<!-- B03-C10-P025 -->
“I must tell you,” said Alyosha, his voice trembling too, “what just happened between him and Father.” And he described the whole scene, how he'd been sent for the money, how Dmitri had burst in and beaten their father, and afterward had again, with particular insistence, told him, Alyosha, to go and give her “his regards.” “He went to that woman…” Alyosha added quietly.

<!-- B03-C10-P026 -->
“And do you think I can't bear that woman? Does he think I can't bear it? But he won't marry her.” She suddenly gave a nervous laugh. “Can a Karamazov burn with a passion like that forever? It's passion, not love. He won't marry her because she won't have him…” Katerina Ivanovna suddenly gave another strange smile.

<!-- B03-C10-P027 -->
“He may marry her,” Alyosha said sadly, lowering his eyes.

<!-- B03-C10-P028 -->
“He won't marry her, I'm telling you! That girl is an angel, do you know that? Do you!” Katerina Ivanovna suddenly cried with extraordinary fervor. “She's the most extraordinary of extraordinary creatures! I know how seductive she is, but I also know how kind, how resolute, how noble. Why are you looking at me like that, Alexei Fyodorovich? Perhaps you're surprised by what I'm saying, perhaps you don't believe me? Agrafena Alexandrovna, my angel!” she suddenly called to someone, looking into the next room. “Come and join us. This is a dear fellow, this is Alyosha, he knows all about our affairs. Let him see you!”

<!-- B03-C10-P029 -->
“I've just been waiting behind the curtain for you to call,” said a gentle, even somewhat sugary, female voice.

<!-- B03-C10-P030 -->
The curtain lifted, and… Grushenka herself approached the table, laughing and full of delight. Something seemed to shudder inside Alyosha. His gaze fixed on her; he could not look away. There she was, that terrible woman—the “beast,” as his brother Ivan had called her only half an hour before. And yet the creature standing before him seemed the most ordinary and unassuming imaginable—a kind, pleasant woman, beautiful, admittedly, but so like all the other beautiful yet “ordinary” women! True, she was very, very good-looking—a Russian beauty of the kind so many love to the point of passion. She was fairly tall, though a little shorter than Katerina Ivanovna, who was very tall indeed, and plump, with soft, seemingly soundless movements, as though these too had been pampered into a special sugary affectation, like her voice. She did not approach with Katerina Ivanovna's strong, brisk stride, but noiselessly. Her feet made no sound at all on the floor. She sank softly into an armchair, with a soft rustle of her sumptuous black silk dress, delicately wrapping a costly black woolen shawl around her full, foam-white neck and broad shoulders. She was twenty-two, and her face expressed precisely that age. Her complexion was very fair, with a pale pink blush high on her cheeks. The outline of her face seemed rather too broad, and her lower jaw even projected a little. Her upper lip was thin, while the lower, slightly protruding, was twice as full and seemed swollen. But her magnificent, abundant dark blond hair, her dark sable brows, and her lovely gray-blue eyes with their long lashes would surely have made even the most indifferent and distracted person suddenly stop before that face, even in a crowd, on a promenade, in a crush, and remember it for a long time. What struck Alyosha most was the childlike, innocent expression of her face. She looked like a child, delighted in something like a child; she had indeed approached the table “full of delight,” as though expecting something at that very moment with the most childlike, impatient, trusting curiosity. Her gaze gladdened the soul—Alyosha felt that. There was something else in her that he could not, or would not have known how to, account for, but that perhaps made itself felt in him unconsciously: once again, the softness, the gentleness of her movements, their catlike silence. And yet hers was a powerful, ample body. Beneath the shawl one could make out her broad, full shoulders and high, still entirely youthful breasts. Her body perhaps promised the form of the Venus de Milo, though even now the proportions were certainly somewhat exaggerated—one could sense it. Connoisseurs of Russian female beauty could have predicted unerringly, looking at Grushenka, that by thirty this fresh, still youthful beauty would lose its harmony and spread, the face itself would grow puffy, little wrinkles would appear extraordinarily quickly around the eyes and on the forehead, and the complexion would coarsen, perhaps turn ruddy—in a word, a beauty of the moment, a fleeting beauty of the kind so often found in Russian women. Alyosha, of course, was not thinking of this; but though enchanted, he asked himself with a feeling of discomfort, almost of regret: why did she draw her words out like that, why couldn't she speak naturally? Evidently she found beauty in this drawl and in the exaggeratedly sugary shading of her syllables and sounds. It was, of course, merely a bad habit, a lapse of taste that testified to a poor upbringing, to a vulgar idea of propriety absorbed in childhood. And yet her pronunciation and intonation seemed to Alyosha an almost impossible contradiction of that childlike, innocent, joyful face, that quiet, happy, infantlike radiance in her eyes! Katerina Ivanovna promptly settled her into the armchair opposite Alyosha and ecstatically kissed her laughing lips several times. She seemed positively in love with her.

<!-- B03-C10-P031 -->
“This is the first time we've met, Alexei Fyodorovich,” she said in rapture. “I wanted to know her, to see her; I meant to go to her, but at the first word of my wish she came herself. I knew we would settle everything together, everything! My heart told me… They begged me not to take this step, but I sensed how it would turn out, and I wasn't wrong. Grushenka has explained everything to me, all her intentions. She flew down here like a good angel and brought peace and joy…”

<!-- B03-C10-P032 -->
“You didn't scorn me, my dear, worthy young lady,” Grushenka drawled in a singsong voice, with the same sweet, joyful smile.

<!-- B03-C10-P033 -->
“Don't you dare say such things to me, you enchantress, you sorceress! Scorn you? Here, I'll kiss your lower lip again. It does look swollen, so let's make it swell a little more, and again, again… Look how she's laughing, Alexei Fyodorovich. The heart rejoices just looking at this angel…” Alyosha was blushing and trembling with a slight, imperceptible tremor.

<!-- B03-C10-P034 -->
“You're spoiling me, dear young lady, and perhaps I don't deserve your kindness at all.”

<!-- B03-C10-P035 -->
“Doesn't deserve it! She doesn't deserve it!” Katerina Ivanovna exclaimed, as fervently as before. “You must know, Alexei Fyodorovich, that we have a fanciful little head and a willful but very, very proud little heart! We're noble, Alexei Fyodorovich, we're generous, did you know that? We've only been unhappy. We were far too quick to sacrifice everything for a man who may have been unworthy, or thoughtless. There was someone, an officer too; we loved him, we gave him everything. It was long ago, five years ago, and he forgot us, he married. Now he's a widower, he's written, he's coming here—and you must know that he's the only man we still love, the only one we've loved all our life! He'll come, and Grushenka will be happy again, but for these whole five years she's been unhappy. Yet who can reproach her, who can boast of her favors! Only that old merchant who could not use his legs—but he was more a father to us, our friend, our protector. He found us in despair, in torment, abandoned by the man we loved so much… Why, she meant to drown herself, and that old man saved her, saved her!”

<!-- B03-C10-P036 -->
“You're defending me far too much, dear young lady, you're in such a hurry about everything,” Grushenka drawled again.

<!-- B03-C10-P037 -->
“Defending you? Who are we to defend you, how would we even dare? Grushenka, angel, give me your hand. Look at this plump, tiny, lovely hand, Alexei Fyodorovich. Do you see it? It has brought me happiness and brought me back to life, and now I'm going to kiss it, on the back and on the palm, there, there, and there!” And in a kind of rapture she kissed Grushenka's genuinely lovely, perhaps rather too plump little hand three times. Grushenka held it out and watched the “dear young lady” with a nervous, ringing, lovely little laugh; clearly she enjoyed having her hand kissed like this. “Perhaps a little too much rapture,” flashed through Alyosha's mind. He blushed. All the while his heart had been strangely uneasy.

<!-- B03-C10-P038 -->
“You won't make me ashamed, will you, dear young lady, for kissing my hand like that in front of Alexei Fyodorovich?”

<!-- B03-C10-P039 -->
“Did you think I meant to shame you by it?” Katerina Ivanovna said, a little surprised. “Oh, my dear, how badly you misunderstand me!”

<!-- B03-C10-P040 -->
“But perhaps you don't quite understand me either, dear young lady. Perhaps I'm much worse than I seem to you. I have a wicked heart, I'm willful. I only captured poor Dmitri Fyodorovich then for the fun of mocking him.”

<!-- B03-C10-P041 -->
“But now you're going to save him. You gave your word. You'll bring him to his senses, you'll tell him you love someone else, have loved him for a long time, and now he's offering you his hand…”

<!-- B03-C10-P042 -->
“Oh no, I never gave you my word about that. You were saying all that to me yourself, but I never gave it.”

<!-- B03-C10-P043 -->
“Then I misunderstood you,” Katerina Ivanovna said quietly, seeming to turn a little pale. “You promised…”

<!-- B03-C10-P044 -->
“Oh no, angel of a young lady, I didn't promise you anything,” Grushenka interrupted softly and evenly, with the same cheerful, innocent expression. “Now you see, worthy young lady, how nasty and self-willed I am compared with you. I'll do whatever takes my fancy. Perhaps I promised you something earlier, but now I'm thinking again: what if I take a liking to him again, to Mitya? I did like him very much once, I liked him for almost a whole hour. Perhaps I'll go and tell him right now to stay with me from this very day… That's how changeable I am…”

<!-- B03-C10-P045 -->
“Earlier you said… something quite different…” Katerina Ivanovna could barely get the words out.

<!-- B03-C10-P046 -->
“Oh, earlier! But I have a tender, foolish heart. Just think what he's been through because of me! What if I get home and feel sorry for him—what then?”

<!-- B03-C10-P047 -->
“I didn't expect…”

<!-- B03-C10-P048 -->
“Oh, young lady, how kind and noble you turn out to be beside me. Perhaps you'll stop loving a fool like me now, because of my nature. Give me your dear hand, angel of a young lady,” she asked tenderly, taking Katerina Ivanovna's hand almost reverently. “Here, my dear young lady, I'll take your hand and kiss it just as you kissed mine. You kissed mine three times, and I ought to kiss yours three hundred times to pay you back. Let that be how it is, then, and afterward whatever God sends; perhaps I'll be your complete slave and wish to please you slavishly in everything. Let it be as God ordains, with no agreements or promises between us. Your hand, your dear little hand! My dear young lady, my impossibly beautiful darling!”

<!-- B03-C10-P049 -->
She gently brought the hand toward her lips, admittedly with the strange intention of “paying back” the kisses. Katerina Ivanovna did not pull her hand away. With timid hope she had listened to Grushenka's latest promise—also expressed very strangely—to please her “slavishly”; she looked intently into her eyes and saw in them the same innocent, trusting expression, the same clear cheerfulness… “Perhaps she's simply too naive!” The thought flashed hopefully through Katerina Ivanovna's heart. Meanwhile Grushenka, as though enchanted by the “dear little hand,” slowly raised it to her lips. But just short of them she suddenly held it still for two or three moments, as though considering something.

<!-- B03-C10-P050 -->
“And do you know what, angel of a young lady,” she suddenly drawled in the gentlest, most sugary little voice of all, “do you know what, I think I won't kiss your hand after all.” And she gave a merry little laugh.

<!-- B03-C10-P051 -->
“As you like… What's the matter with you?” Katerina Ivanovna suddenly started.

<!-- B03-C10-P052 -->
“So that's what you'll have to remember: you kissed my hand, but I didn't kiss yours.” Something suddenly flashed in her eyes. She was staring at Katerina Ivanovna with terrible intensity.

<!-- B03-C10-P053 -->
“You insolent woman!” Katerina Ivanovna suddenly said, as though she had understood something all at once. She flushed all over and sprang up. Grushenka rose too, unhurriedly.

<!-- B03-C10-P054 -->
“And now I'll tell Mitya how you kissed my hand, and I didn't kiss yours at all. Oh, how he'll laugh!”

<!-- B03-C10-P055 -->
“You vile creature, get out!”

<!-- B03-C10-P056 -->
“Oh, shame on you, young lady, shame on you. Those words don't become you at all, my dear young lady.”

<!-- B03-C10-P057 -->
“Get out, you creature for sale!” Katerina Ivanovna screamed. Every feature of her utterly distorted face was trembling.

<!-- B03-C10-P058 -->
“For sale, am I? You yourself went to gentlemen at dusk for money, an unmarried girl, bringing your beauty to sell. I know all about it.”

<!-- B03-C10-P059 -->
Katerina Ivanovna gave a cry and started to fling herself at her, but Alyosha held her back with all his strength:

<!-- B03-C10-P060 -->
“Not a step, not a word! Don't say anything, don't answer her. She's leaving, she'll leave at once!”

<!-- B03-C10-P061 -->
At that moment both of Katerina Ivanovna's relatives ran into the room at the cry, and the maid ran in too. They all rushed to her.

<!-- B03-C10-P062 -->
“And so I will,” said Grushenka, snatching her mantilla from the sofa. “Alyosha, my dear, walk with me!”

<!-- B03-C10-P063 -->
“Go, please go quickly!” Alyosha clasped his hands before her in appeal.

<!-- B03-C10-P064 -->
“Dear Alyoshenka, walk with me! I'll tell you something very, very sweet on the way! I put on that scene for you, Alyoshenka. Come along, darling, you'll be glad afterward.”

<!-- B03-C10-P065 -->
Alyosha turned away, wringing his hands. Grushenka ran out of the house with a ringing laugh.

<!-- B03-C10-P066 -->
Katerina Ivanovna went into a fit. She sobbed, choking with spasms. Everyone fussed around her.

<!-- B03-C10-P067 -->
“I warned you,” the elder aunt told her. “I tried to stop you taking this step… You're too impetuous… How could you decide to do such a thing! You don't know these creatures, and they say this one's the worst of all… No, you're far too willful!”

<!-- B03-C10-P068 -->
“She's a tiger!” Katerina Ivanovna screamed. “Why did you hold me back, Alexei Fyodorovich? I'd have beaten her, beaten her!”

<!-- B03-C10-P069 -->
She could not restrain herself in front of Alyosha; perhaps she did not want to.

<!-- B03-C10-P070 -->
“She ought to be whipped, on the scaffold, by the executioner, in front of everyone!”

<!-- B03-C10-P071 -->
Alyosha backed toward the door.

<!-- B03-C10-P072 -->
“But God!” Katerina Ivanovna suddenly cried, throwing up her hands. “Him! How could he be so dishonorable, so inhuman! He told that creature what happened there, on that fateful, forever accursed, accursed day! ‘You came to sell your beauty, dear young lady!’ She knows! Your brother is a scoundrel, Alexei Fyodorovich!”

<!-- B03-C10-P073 -->
Alyosha wanted to say something, but could not find a single word. His heart tightened with pain.

<!-- B03-C10-P074 -->
“Go, Alexei Fyodorovich! I'm ashamed, I'm horrified! Tomorrow… I beg you on my knees, come tomorrow. Don't judge me, forgive me, I don't know what I may yet do to myself!”

<!-- B03-C10-P075 -->
Alyosha went out into the street almost staggering. He wanted to cry too, just as she did. Suddenly the maid caught up with him.

<!-- B03-C10-P076 -->
“The young lady forgot to give you this little letter from Madame Khokhlakov. She's had it since dinner.”

<!-- B03-C10-P077 -->
Alyosha mechanically accepted the little pink envelope and slipped it into his pocket almost without knowing it.
