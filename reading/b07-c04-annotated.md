# Book VII: Alyosha

## IV. Cana of Galilee

<!-- B07-C04-P001 -->
It was already very late by monastic standards when Alyosha reached the hermitage; the gatekeeper let him in by a special route. Nine had already struck—the hour for everyone to rest and be at peace after a day so troubling to them all. Alyosha timidly opened the door and entered the elder’s cell, where his coffin now stood. No one was there except Father Paisius, reading the Gospel alone over the coffin, and the young novice Porfiry, who, worn out by the previous night’s conversation and the bustle of the day, lay on the floor in the other room, sleeping the sound sleep of youth. Father Paisius heard Alyosha come in but did not even look his way. Alyosha turned right from the door into the corner, knelt, and began to pray. His soul was overflowing, but somehow indistinctly: no single feeling stood out with any force; instead, one displaced another in a quiet, steady circling. Yet there was sweetness in his heart, and strangely, Alyosha was not surprised by it. Again he saw before him this coffin, this dead man so dear to him, covered on all sides, but his soul held none of the tearful, aching, agonizing pity of that morning. On entering, he had fallen before the coffin as before something sacred, yet joy, joy shone in his mind and heart.

One window of the cell stood open; the air was fresh and rather cold. “The smell must have grown stronger, then, if they’ve decided to open a window,” Alyosha thought. But even this thought of the odor of corruption, which only a little earlier had seemed so terrible and shameful, did not stir his former anguish or indignation now.

He began praying quietly, but soon felt that he was praying almost mechanically. Fragments of thought flashed through his soul, flared like little stars, and immediately went out, replaced by others; yet something whole, firm, and soothing reigned in his soul, and he knew it himself. At times he would begin praying fervently; he so longed to give thanks and to love... But having begun his prayer, he would suddenly turn to something else, fall into thought, and forget both the prayer and what had interrupted it. He tried listening to what Father Paisius was reading, but he was very tired and little by little began to doze...

<!-- B07-C04-P002 -->
“And on the third day there was a wedding in Cana of Galilee,” Father Paisius read, “and the mother of Jesus was there. And Jesus and his disciples were invited to the wedding.”

<!-- B07-C04-P003 -->
“A wedding? What’s this... a wedding...” swept through Alyosha’s mind like a whirlwind. “She has happiness too... she’s gone to a feast... No, she didn’t take a knife, didn’t take a knife... Those were only ‘pitiful’ words... Well... pitiful words must be forgiven, they must. Pitiful words comfort the soul... without them people’s grief would be too hard to bear. Rakitin turned into a side street. As long as Rakitin keeps thinking about his grievances, he’ll always be turning into a side street... But the road... the road is wide, straight, bright, crystal clear, and the sun is at its end... Eh?.. What are they reading?”

<!-- B07-C04-P004 -->
“...And when the wine ran out, the mother of Jesus said to him: They have no wine...” Alyosha heard.

<!-- B07-C04-P005 -->
“Oh yes, I missed some, and I didn’t want to miss any. I love this passage: it’s Cana of Galilee, the first miracle... Oh, that miracle, oh, that dear miracle! It was human joy that Christ visited when he worked his first miracle, rather than sorrow; he helped human joy... ‘Whoever loves people loves their joy too...’ The dead man was always repeating that; it was one of his most important thoughts... You can’t live without joy, Mitya says... Yes, Mitya... Everything true and beautiful is always full of forgiveness—that was something he said too...”

<!-- B07-C04-P006 -->
“...Jesus said to her: Woman, what is this to me and to you? My hour has not yet come. His mother said to the servants: Whatever he tells you, do.”

<!-- B07-C04-P007 -->
“Do... The joy, the joy of some poor, very poor people... They must have been poor, if there wasn’t even enough wine for a wedding... Historians write that around Lake Gennesaret and in all those places the people living there then were the poorest imaginable... And that other great heart, belonging to the other great being who was there, his mother, knew that he had not come down only for his great and terrible undertaking, that his heart was open too to the simple, artless merriment of some unlettered, unlettered and simple souls who had kindly invited him to their humble wedding. ‘My hour has not yet come,’ he says with a quiet smile—he must have smiled gently at her... Really, had he come down to earth to multiply wine at poor people’s weddings? And yet he went and did it at her request... Oh, he’s reading again.”

<!-- B07-C04-P008 -->
“...Jesus said to them: Fill the water jars with water. And they filled them to the brim.

<!-- B07-C04-P009 -->
“And he said to them: Draw some out now and bring it to the master of the feast. And they brought it.

<!-- B07-C04-P010 -->
“When the master of the feast tasted the wine that had been water, and did not know where it came from—but the servants who had drawn the water knew—the master of the feast called the bridegroom.

<!-- B07-C04-P011 -->
“And he said to him: Every man serves the good wine first, and when the guests are drunk, then the worse; but you have kept the good wine until now.”

<!-- B07-C04-P012 -->
“But what is this, what is this? Why is the room growing wider... Oh yes... it’s a wedding, a marriage feast... yes, of course. There are the guests, there sit the bride and groom, and the joyful crowd, and... where is the all-wise master of the feast? But who is that? Who? The room has grown wider again... Who is getting up from behind the big table? What... Is he here too? But he’s in the coffin... And yet he’s here too... he’s stood up, seen me, he’s coming this way... Lord!..”

<!-- B07-C04-P013 -->
Yes, he had come up to him, to him: a wizened little old man, his face lined with fine wrinkles, joyful and laughing softly. The coffin is gone now, and he is wearing the same clothes as yesterday when he sat with them, when the guests gathered around him. His whole face is uncovered, his eyes shine. How can it be? So he too is at the feast, he too has been invited to the wedding at Cana of Galilee...

<!-- B07-C04-P014 -->
“Yes, my dear, I too was invited, invited and called,” a quiet voice sounds above him. “Why have you hidden yourself here where no one can see you... Come and join us.”

<!-- B07-C04-P015 -->
His voice, the voice of the elder Zosima... And how could it be anyone else, if he is calling? The elder helped Alyosha up with his hand, and Alyosha rose from his knees.

<!-- B07-C04-P016 -->
“We are rejoicing,” the wizened little old man continues. “We are drinking new wine, the wine of a new, great joy. Do you see how many guests there are? There are the bridegroom and the bride, and there is the all-wise master of the feast, tasting the new wine. Why are you looking at me in such surprise? I gave a little onion, and here I am. And many here gave only a little onion, just one little onion... What are our deeds worth? And you, my quiet one, you, my gentle boy, you too managed to give a little onion today to a woman who was hungry. Begin, my dear, begin your work, my gentle one!.. And do you see our sun, do you see him?”

<!-- B07-C04-P017 -->
“I’m afraid... I don’t dare look...” Alyosha whispered.

<!-- B07-C04-P018 -->
“Do not be afraid of him. His majesty fills us with fear, his exalted height with awe, but he is infinitely merciful; out of love he became like us and rejoices with us, turns water into wine so that the guests’ joy may not end. He is waiting for new guests, ceaselessly calling new ones, now and forever and ever. There, they are bringing new wine, do you see, they are bringing the vessels...”

<!-- B07-C04-P019 -->
Something was burning in Alyosha’s heart, something suddenly filled it to the point of pain; tears of rapture were bursting from his soul... He stretched out his arms, cried out, and woke...

<!-- B07-C04-P020 -->
Again the coffin, the open window, and the quiet, solemn, measured reading of the Gospel. But Alyosha was no longer listening to what was being read. Strangely, he had fallen asleep on his knees, yet now he was standing, and suddenly, as though breaking free of the spot, he took three firm, quick steps and came right up to the coffin. His shoulder even brushed Father Paisius without his noticing. For a moment Father Paisius looked up at him from the book, but at once lowered his eyes again, understanding that something strange had happened to the young man. For half a minute Alyosha gazed at the coffin, at the covered, motionless dead man stretched out inside it, an icon on his chest and a monastic hood bearing an eight-pointed cross on his head. Only a moment ago he had heard his voice, and that voice was still sounding in his ears. He was still listening, still waiting for more sounds... but suddenly he turned sharply and left the cell.

<!-- B07-C04-P021 -->
He did not stop on the little porch either, but hurried down the steps. His soul, full of rapture, thirsted for freedom, for room, for open space. Above him the dome of heaven spread wide and boundless, full of quiet, shining stars. From the zenith to the horizon the Milky Way, still indistinct, appeared double. The fresh night, quiet to the point of stillness, enfolded the earth. The white towers and golden domes of the cathedral shone against the sapphire sky. The luxuriant autumn flowers in the beds beside the house had gone to sleep until morning. The silence of earth seemed to merge with the silence of heaven; the mystery of earth touched the mystery of the stars... Alyosha stood looking, and suddenly threw himself to the ground as though his legs had been cut from under him.

<!-- B07-C04-P022 -->
He did not know why he was embracing it; he could not account for his irresistible desire to kiss it, to kiss all of it, but he kissed it, weeping, sobbing, drenching it with his tears, and in ecstasy swore to love it, to love it forever and ever. “Wet the earth with the tears of your joy and love these tears of yours...” rang out in his soul. What was he weeping for? Oh, in his rapture he wept even for those stars shining upon him out of the abyss, and “was not ashamed of this ecstasy.” It was as though threads from all these countless worlds of God had come together at once in his soul, and all of it trembled, “in contact with other worlds.” He wanted to forgive everyone for everything and to ask forgiveness—oh, not for himself, but for everyone, for everything and everyone, while “others are asking for me,” rang out in his soul again. But with every moment he felt clearly, almost tangibly, something firm and unshakable as this vault of heaven descending into his soul. Something like an idea was establishing its rule in his mind—for the whole of his life now, forever and ever. He had fallen to the earth a weak young man and risen a fighter, steadfast for the rest of his life, and he knew and felt this suddenly, in that very moment of rapture. And never, never in all his life afterward could Alyosha forget that moment. “Someone visited my soul in that hour,” he would say later, with firm faith in his own words...

<!-- B07-C04-P023 -->
Three days later he left the monastery, in keeping with the words of his departed elder, who had instructed him to “live in the world.”
