# Book II, Chapter IV
## A Lady of Little Faith

*Part I · Book II: An Inappropriate Gathering*

<!-- B02-C04-P001 -->
The visiting landowner shed quiet tears as she watched the whole scene—the conversation with the common people and the blessing he gave them—and dabbed them away with her handkerchief. She was a sensitive society lady, and many of her impulses were sincerely kind. When the elder finally approached her too, she greeted him with rapture:

<!-- B02-C04-P002 -->
“I've been through so much, so much, watching this whole moving scene…” Emotion kept her from finishing. “Oh, I understand why the people love you. I love the people myself, I want to love them, and how could anyone help loving the people—our beautiful Russian people, so simplehearted in their greatness!”

<!-- B02-C04-P003 -->
“How is your daughter's health? Did you wish to speak with me again?”

<!-- B02-C04-P004 -->
“Oh, I begged so insistently, I pleaded. I was ready to go down on my knees and stay there under your windows for three whole days, if that was what it took for you to let me in. We've come to you, great healer, to express all our rapturous gratitude. You have healed my Liza, healed her completely, and how? By praying over her on Thursday and laying your hands on her. We hurried here to kiss those hands, to pour out our feelings and our reverence!”

<!-- B02-C04-P005 -->
“What do you mean, healed her? She's still lying in her chair, isn't she?”

<!-- B02-C04-P006 -->
“But the nighttime fevers have vanished completely, for two days now, ever since Thursday,” the lady hurried on nervously. “And that's not all: her legs have grown stronger. She got up this morning feeling well, she slept the whole night. Look at the color in her cheeks, at her shining eyes. Before, she was always crying, and now she laughs, she's cheerful, she's happy. This morning she insisted on being put on her feet, and she stood for a whole minute by herself, without any support at all. She's betting me that in two weeks she'll be dancing the quadrille. I called in the local doctor, Herzenstube. He shrugs and says, ‘I'm amazed, I can't understand it.’ And you expect us not to trouble you, to keep from flying here to thank you? Lise, do thank him, thank him!”

<!-- B02-C04-P007 -->
Lise's sweet, laughing face suddenly grew serious. She raised herself in her chair as far as she could and, looking at the elder, clasped her hands before him, but she couldn't help herself and suddenly laughed…

<!-- B02-C04-P008 -->
“It's him I'm laughing at, him!” she said, pointing to Alyosha, annoyed with herself like a child for not being able to hold back her laughter. Anyone looking at Alyosha, who stood a step behind the elder, would have seen a swift flush flood his cheeks. His eyes flashed and fell.

<!-- B02-C04-P009 -->
“She has an errand for you, Alexei Fyodorovich… How are you?” her mother went on, turning suddenly to Alyosha and holding out her prettily gloved hand. The elder looked around and suddenly studied Alyosha. Alyosha came up to Liza and, smiling rather strangely and awkwardly, offered her his hand too. Lise put on a solemn face.

<!-- B02-C04-P010 -->
“Katerina Ivanovna sent you this through me.” She handed him a little letter. “She especially asks you to come and see her, and soon, very soon, and not to let her down but to be sure to come.”

<!-- B02-C04-P011 -->
“She's asking me to come? Me, to her… What for?” Alyosha murmured in profound surprise. His face suddenly grew very troubled.

<!-- B02-C04-P012 -->
“Oh, it's all about Dmitri Fyodorovich and… all these recent happenings,” her mother explained hurriedly. “Katerina Ivanovna has now decided on a course of action… but for that she absolutely must see you… Why? Of course I don't know, but she asked you to come as soon as possible. And you will, I'm sure you will, even Christian feeling requires it.”

<!-- B02-C04-P013 -->
“I've only seen her once,” Alyosha continued, still just as bewildered.

<!-- B02-C04-P014 -->
“Oh, she's such a lofty, such an unattainable creature!… Her suffering alone… Consider what she's endured, what she's enduring now, consider what lies ahead of her… It's all terrible, terrible!”

<!-- B02-C04-P015 -->
“All right, I'll come,” Alyosha decided, after glancing through the short, mysterious note, which contained no explanation beyond an urgent request that he come.

<!-- B02-C04-P016 -->
“Oh, how kind and wonderful that will be of you!” Lise cried, suddenly all animation. “And I've been telling Mama: he won't go for anything, he's busy saving his soul. How wonderful, how wonderful you are! I've always thought you were wonderful, and I'm so glad to tell you now!”

<!-- B02-C04-P017 -->
“Lise!” her mother said sternly, though she smiled at once.

<!-- B02-C04-P018 -->
“You've forgotten us too, Alexei Fyodorovich. You don't want to visit us at all, and yet Lise has told me twice that she's only happy when she's with you.” Alyosha raised his lowered eyes, suddenly blushed again, and suddenly smiled again, without knowing why. The elder, however, was no longer watching him. He had begun talking to the visiting monk who, as we have already said, had been waiting beside Lise's chair for him to appear. He seemed to be one of the simplest of monks, that is, a man of common birth, with a narrow, unshakable outlook, but devout and, in his own way, obstinate. He said he came from somewhere in the far north, from Obdorsk, from St. Sylvester's, a poor monastery with only nine monks. The elder blessed him and invited him to visit his cell whenever he wished.

<!-- B02-C04-P019 -->
“How do you dare to do such things?” the monk suddenly asked in a grave, imposing tone, pointing at Lise. He was referring to her “healing.”

<!-- B02-C04-P020 -->
“It's certainly too early to speak of that. An improvement is not yet a complete recovery and could have come about for other reasons. But if anything has happened, it was through no one's power, only by God's will. Everything comes from God. Come and see me, Father,” he added to the monk, “for I can't receive visitors at all times: I'm ill, and I know my days are numbered.”

<!-- B02-C04-P021 -->
“Oh no, no, God won't take you away from us, you'll live a long, long time yet,” the mother cried. “And what is wrong with you? You look so healthy, so cheerful, so happy.”

<!-- B02-C04-P022 -->
“I feel so much better today, but I know it's only for a moment. By now I know my illness too well to be mistaken. But if I seem so cheerful to you, nothing you could ever have said or done would have given me greater joy than that remark. For people were created for happiness, and anyone who is wholly happy may rightly say to himself: ‘I have fulfilled God's commandment on this earth.’ All the righteous, all the saints, all the holy martyrs were happy, every one of them.”

<!-- B02-C04-P023 -->
“Oh, the way you speak, what bold and exalted words!” the mother cried. “You speak and it's as though you pierce right through me. And yet happiness, happiness—where is it? Who can say they're happy? Oh, since you've been kind enough to let us see you once more today, listen to everything I left unsaid last time, everything I didn't dare say, everything that makes me suffer so much, and has for so long, so long! I'm suffering, forgive me, I'm suffering…” And in a sudden rush of feeling she clasped her hands before him.

<!-- B02-C04-P024 -->
“What troubles you most?”

<!-- B02-C04-P025 -->
“I suffer from… a lack of faith…”

<!-- B02-C04-P026 -->
“A lack of faith in God?”

<!-- B02-C04-P027 -->
“Oh no, no, I wouldn't dare even think of that, but the life to come—it's such a mystery! And no one, absolutely no one has an answer! Listen, you're a healer, you understand the human soul. Of course I wouldn't presume to expect you to believe me completely, but I give you my most solemn word that I'm not speaking frivolously now, that the thought of that future life beyond the grave troubles me to the point of anguish, of terror and fear… And I don't know whom to turn to, I've never dared to in all my life… And now I'm daring to turn to you… Oh God, what must you think of me now!” She threw up her hands.

<!-- B02-C04-P028 -->
“Don't worry about my opinion,” the elder replied. “I fully believe in the sincerity of your anguish.”

<!-- B02-C04-P029 -->
“Oh, how grateful I am to you! You see, I close my eyes and think: if everyone believes, where did it come from? And then people insist that it all began in fear of the terrifying forces of nature, and that none of it exists. Well, I think, I've believed all my life—I'll die, and suddenly there's nothing, only ‘burdock growing on my grave,’ as I read in a certain writer.[^b02-c04-n001] It's terrible! How, how can I get my faith back? Though I only believed when I was a little child, mechanically, without thinking about anything… How, then, how can it be proved? I've come now to throw myself at your feet and beg you for this. Because if I let this chance slip away too, no one will ever give me an answer for the rest of my life. How can it be proved, how can I be sure? Oh, how unhappy I am! I stand here and look around and see that no one cares, almost no one, no one bothers about it now, and I'm the only one who can't bear it. It's killing me, killing me!”

<!-- B02-C04-P030 -->
“No doubt it is. But nothing can be proved here, though it is possible to become convinced.”

<!-- B02-C04-P031 -->
“How? By what means?”

<!-- B02-C04-P032 -->
“Through the experience of active love. Try to love your neighbors actively and tirelessly. As you make progress in love, you will also become convinced of God's existence and the immortality of your own soul. If you reach complete selflessness in your love for your neighbor, then you will believe beyond any doubt, and no doubt will even be able to enter your soul. This has been tried; this is certain.”

<!-- B02-C04-P033 -->
“Active love? Now there's another question, and what a question, what a question! You see, I love humanity so much that, would you believe it, I sometimes dream of giving up everything, everything I have, leaving Lise and becoming a sister of mercy. I close my eyes, think and dream, and in those moments I feel an irresistible strength within me. No wounds, no festering sores could frighten me. I'd bandage and wash them with my own hands, I'd nurse those suffering people, I'm ready to kiss their sores…”

<!-- B02-C04-P034 -->
“It's already a great deal, and a good thing, that your mind dreams of that and not of something else. Every now and then, quite without meaning to, you will actually do a good deed.”

<!-- B02-C04-P035 -->
“Yes, but how long could I survive such a life?” the lady continued hotly, almost frantically. “That's the chief question! The most tormenting of all my questions. I close my eyes and ask myself: how long could you keep going on that path? And if the sick man whose sores you're washing doesn't immediately thank you, but instead begins to torment you with his whims, neither appreciating nor even noticing your devoted service to humanity, begins shouting at you, making rude demands, even complaining to someone in authority—as often happens with people who suffer terribly—what then? Will your love last or won't it? And now—imagine it, I've already reached my answer, with a shudder: if there's anything that could instantly cool my ‘active’ love for humanity, it's ingratitude alone. In short, I work for pay. I demand to be paid at once, that is, I want praise, and love in payment for love. Otherwise I'm incapable of loving anyone!”

<!-- B02-C04-P036 -->
She was in a fit of the most sincere self-flagellation, and when she finished she looked at the elder with defiant resolve.

<!-- B02-C04-P037 -->
“That's exactly what a doctor once told me, though it was a long time ago,” the elder remarked. “He was already an older man, and unquestionably intelligent. He spoke as frankly as you do, though he was joking, but sadly. ‘I love humanity,’ he said, ‘but I puzzle myself: the more I love humanity in general, the less I love people in particular, that is, separately, as individuals. In my dreams,’ he said, ‘I have often reached the point of passionately imagining how I would serve humanity, and perhaps I really would have gone to the cross for people if it had somehow suddenly been necessary. And yet I can't spend two days in the same room with anyone, as I know from experience. The moment he's near me, his personality weighs on my pride and cramps my freedom. In a single day I can come to hate even the best of men: one because he takes too long over his dinner, another because he has a cold and keeps blowing his nose. I become people's enemy,’ he said, ‘as soon as they come into the slightest contact with me. And yet it has always happened that the more I hated people in particular, the more ardent my love for humanity in general became.’”

<!-- B02-C04-P038 -->
“But what is one to do? What is one to do in that case? Must one despair?”

<!-- B02-C04-P039 -->
“No, for it's enough that you grieve over it. Do what you can, and it will be counted in your favor. You've already done a great deal, since you've been able to see into yourself so deeply and sincerely! But if you spoke so sincerely to me now simply to be praised for your truthfulness, as I have just praised you, then of course you will get nowhere in the work of active love. It will all remain in your dreams, and your whole life will flash by like a phantom. Naturally, you'll forget about the life to come as well, and in the end you'll somehow settle down of your own accord.”

<!-- B02-C04-P040 -->
“You've crushed me! Only now, this very moment, as you were speaking, have I understood that I really was just waiting for you to praise my sincerity when I told you I couldn't bear ingratitude. You've shown me myself, you've caught me out and explained me to myself!”

<!-- B02-C04-P041 -->
“Do you mean that? Well, now, after such an admission, I believe you're sincere and goodhearted. If you don't reach happiness, always remember that you're on the right road, and try not to stray from it. Above all, flee from lies, every kind of lie, especially lies to yourself. Watch for your own lies and examine them every hour, every minute. Flee from disgust too, both toward others and toward yourself: what seems foul within you is already being cleansed by the very fact that you've noticed it. Flee from fear as well, though fear is only a consequence of lying. Never be frightened by your own faintheartedness as you strive toward love; don't be too frightened even by the bad things you do along the way. I'm sorry I can't tell you anything more comforting, for active love, compared with love in dreams, is a harsh and frightening thing. Love in dreams craves a heroic deed that can be done quickly, brings quick satisfaction, and has everyone looking on. It really can go so far that people even give their lives, provided it doesn't take too long, provided it's over quickly, as though onstage, with everyone watching and praising. But active love is work and endurance, and for some people, perhaps, a whole science. Yet I predict that at the very moment when you look with horror at how, despite all your efforts, you have not only failed to come closer to your goal but seem to have moved farther away—at that very moment, I predict this to you, you will suddenly reach your goal and see clearly above you the wonder-working power of the Lord, who has loved you all along and mysteriously guided you all along. Forgive me for not being able to stay with you longer. They are waiting for me. Goodbye.”

<!-- B02-C04-P042 -->
The lady was crying.

<!-- B02-C04-P043 -->
“Lise, Lise, do bless her, bless her!” she cried, suddenly all in a flutter.

<!-- B02-C04-P044 -->
“She isn't even worth loving. I saw her being naughty the whole time,” the elder said playfully. “Why were you laughing at Alexei all this time?”

<!-- B02-C04-P045 -->
And Lise had indeed been playing this trick the whole time. She had noticed long before, on their previous visit, that Alyosha was embarrassed by her and tried not to look at her, and this had begun to amuse her terribly. She watched intently, waiting to catch his eye. Unable to endure her fixed stare, every now and then Alyosha would suddenly look at her himself, involuntarily, drawn by an irresistible force, and at once she would smile triumphantly straight into his eyes. Alyosha grew still more embarrassed and annoyed. At last he turned away altogether and hid behind the elder's back. After several minutes, drawn by that same irresistible force, he turned again to see whether she was looking at him, and saw that Lise, almost hanging out of her chair, was peering at him from the side, waiting with all her might for him to look. When she caught his eye, she burst out laughing so hard that even the elder could no longer hold back:

<!-- B02-C04-P046 -->
“Why are you embarrassing him like that, you naughty girl?”

<!-- B02-C04-P047 -->
All at once, quite unexpectedly, Lise blushed, her eyes flashed, and her face became terribly serious. Suddenly she began to speak quickly and nervously, in a hot, indignant complaint:

<!-- B02-C04-P048 -->
“But why has he forgotten everything? He used to carry me in his arms when I was little, we played together. He used to come and teach me to read, did you know that? When he said goodbye two years ago, he said he'd never forget, that we were friends forever, forever, forever! And now he's suddenly afraid of me. Am I going to eat him or something? Why won't he come over, why won't he talk? Why won't he come and see us? Is it you who won't let him? We know he goes everywhere. It isn't proper for me to invite him. He should have been the first to remember, if he hasn't forgotten. Oh no, he's saving his soul now! Why did you put that long cassock on him?… If he runs, he'll fall…”

<!-- B02-C04-P049 -->
And suddenly, unable to help herself, she covered her face with her hand and laughed terribly, uncontrollably, in her long, nervous, shaking, soundless laugh. The elder had listened to her with a smile and blessed her tenderly. But as she began to kiss his hand, she suddenly pressed it to her eyes and burst into tears:

<!-- B02-C04-P050 -->
“Don't be angry with me, I'm a fool, I'm worthless… and perhaps Alyosha's right, quite right, not to want to visit someone so ridiculous.”

<!-- B02-C04-P051 -->
“I'll certainly send him,” the elder said firmly.

[^b02-c04-n001]: She is recalling Bazarov’s words in Chapter XXI of Ivan Turgenev’s *Fathers and Sons*: he imagines burdock growing from his dead body.
