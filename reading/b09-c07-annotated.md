# Book IX: The Preliminary Investigation
## VII. Mitya’s Great Secret. Hissed Down

<!-- B09-C07-P001 -->
“Gentlemen,” he began, still just as agitated, “this money... I want to confess everything... this money was mine.”

<!-- B09-C07-P002 -->
The prosecutor’s and the investigator’s faces lengthened; this was not at all what they had expected.

<!-- B09-C07-P003 -->
“How could it be yours?” Nikolai Parfenovich faltered. “When as late as five that afternoon, by your own admission...”

<!-- B09-C07-P004 -->
“Oh, to hell with five that afternoon and my own admission! That’s not the point now! The money was mine, mine—that is, my stolen money... Not mine, I mean, but stolen, stolen by me, and there was fifteen hundred, and I had it on me, on me all the time...”

<!-- B09-C07-P005 -->
“But where did you get it?”

<!-- B09-C07-P006 -->
“Off my neck, gentlemen, off my neck, this very neck of mine... I had it here round my neck, sewn into a rag and hanging round my neck, for a long time now, a whole month I’d been wearing it round my neck in shame and disgrace!”

<!-- B09-C07-P007 -->
“But whose money did you... appropriate?”

<!-- B09-C07-P008 -->
“You mean ‘steal’? Say things plainly now. Yes, I consider it as good as stealing, though if you prefer, I did indeed ‘appropriate’ it. To my mind, though, I stole it. And last night I stole it outright.”

<!-- B09-C07-P009 -->
“Last night? But you just said it was a month ago that you... obtained it!”

<!-- B09-C07-P010 -->
“Yes, but not from my father, not from my father, don’t worry, I didn’t steal it from my father, but from her. Let me tell you and don’t interrupt. This is hard, you know. You see, a month ago Katerina Ivanovna Verkhovtseva, my former fiancée, sent for me... Do you know her?”

<!-- B09-C07-P011 -->
“Of course we do, certainly.”

<!-- B09-C07-P012 -->
“I know you know her. The noblest soul, the noblest of the noble, but she’d hated me for a long time already—oh, a long, long time... and deservedly, deservedly hated me!”

<!-- B09-C07-P013 -->
“Katerina Ivanovna?” the investigator repeated in surprise. The prosecutor, too, stared at him in utter astonishment.

<!-- B09-C07-P014 -->
“Oh, don’t take her name in vain! I’m a scoundrel to bring her into this. Yes, I saw she hated me... long ago... from the very first time, from that time in my lodgings back there... But enough, enough, you’re not even worthy to know about that, there’s no need for it at all... All you need is that a month ago she sent for me, gave me three thousand to send to her sister and another relation in Moscow—as if she couldn’t send it herself!—and I... it was at that fatal hour of my life when I... well, in a word, when I’d just fallen in love with another woman, with her, the present one, she’s sitting downstairs with you now, Grushenka... I carried her off here to Mokroye and in two days I squandered half those cursed three thousand on a spree, fifteen hundred, that is, and kept the other half on me. Well, those fifteen hundred I kept, I carried round my neck in place of a little devotional pouch, and yesterday I broke it open and squandered them. The eight hundred rubles left over are in your hands now, Nikolai Parfenovich, the change from yesterday’s fifteen hundred.”

<!-- B09-C07-P015 -->
“Wait, how can that be? You spent three thousand here a month ago, not fifteen hundred. Everyone knows that.”

<!-- B09-C07-P016 -->
“Who knows it? Who counted it? Who did I let count it?”

<!-- B09-C07-P017 -->
“But you yourself told everyone you’d spent exactly three thousand then.”

<!-- B09-C07-P018 -->
“True, I did, I told the whole town, and the whole town said it, and everyone thought so, and here in Mokroye everyone thought so too, that it was three thousand. All the same, I spent fifteen hundred, not three thousand, and sewed the other fifteen hundred into a little pouch. That’s how it was, gentlemen, that’s where yesterday’s money came from...”

<!-- B09-C07-P019 -->
“This is almost miraculous...” Nikolai Parfenovich faltered.

<!-- B09-C07-P020 -->
“May I ask,” the prosecutor said at last, “did you tell anyone at all about this before... that is, that you kept those fifteen hundred back at the time, a month ago?”

<!-- B09-C07-P021 -->
“I told no one.”

<!-- B09-C07-P022 -->
“That’s strange. Really no one at all?”

<!-- B09-C07-P023 -->
“No one at all. No one and no one.”

<!-- B09-C07-P024 -->
“But why keep quiet about it? What made you keep it such a secret? Let me be more precise: you’ve finally revealed your secret to us, so ‘disgraceful,’ as you describe it, though essentially—that is, relatively speaking, of course—this action, the appropriation of someone else’s three thousand rubles, and undoubtedly only a temporary appropriation, is in my view at least merely an extremely thoughtless action, but not so disgraceful, taking your character into account as well... Well, let us even say a highly reprehensible action, I agree, but reprehensible, still not disgraceful... That is, my point really is that many people had already guessed this past month about the three thousand from Madame Verkhovtseva you’d squandered, without your confession. I’d heard the story myself... Mikhail Makarovich, for instance, had heard it too. So that by now it’s hardly a story at all, but the gossip of the entire town. Besides, there are indications that you yourself, if I’m not mistaken, confessed to someone that this money came from Madame Verkhovtseva... And that’s why it astonishes me so much that until now, until this very moment, you should have made such an extraordinary secret of those fifteen hundred you say you set aside, associating a kind of horror with this secret of yours... It’s incredible that a secret like that should have cost you such agonies to confess... You were actually shouting just now that you’d rather go to penal servitude than confess...”

<!-- B09-C07-P025 -->
The prosecutor stopped. He had grown heated. Making no effort to hide his annoyance, almost his anger, he had poured out everything that had accumulated, without troubling about elegance of expression—that is, disjointedly and almost confusedly.

<!-- B09-C07-P026 -->
“The disgrace wasn’t in the fifteen hundred, but in my separating those fifteen hundred from the three thousand,” Mitya said firmly.

<!-- B09-C07-P027 -->
“But what,” the prosecutor asked with an irritated smile, “what exactly is disgraceful about your taking three thousand reprehensibly—or disgracefully, if you prefer—and then setting half aside as you thought fit? What matters is that you appropriated three thousand, not what you did with it afterward. By the way, why did you do that, set half aside? What for, with what purpose? Can you explain that to us?”

<!-- B09-C07-P028 -->
“Oh, gentlemen, the purpose is the whole point!” Mitya exclaimed. “I set it aside out of baseness, out of calculation, that is, because in this case calculation is baseness... And that baseness went on for a whole month!”

<!-- B09-C07-P029 -->
“I don’t understand.”

<!-- B09-C07-P030 -->
“You amaze me. Still, I’ll explain again, perhaps it really is hard to understand. Look, follow me: I appropriate three thousand entrusted to my honor, go on a spree with it, squander it all, and come to her the next morning and say, ‘Katya, I’ve done wrong, I’ve squandered your three thousand’—well, is that good? No, it’s not good—dishonorable and weak, a brute, a man with so little self-control he’s no better than a beast, isn’t that so, isn’t it? But still not a thief? Not an outright thief, surely, not outright, you must agree! Squandered it, but didn’t steal it! Now a second, still more favorable case, follow me or I may get muddled again—my head’s spinning somehow—so, the second case: I spend only fifteen hundred here out of the three thousand, half, that is. The next day I go to her and bring that half: ‘Katya, take this half from me, a wretch and a thoughtless scoundrel, because I’ve squandered one half, so I’ll squander this one too—better put it beyond temptation!’ Well, what about that case? Anything you please, a brute and a scoundrel, but no longer a thief, definitely not a thief, because if I were a thief I certainly wouldn’t have brought back half the money, I’d have pocketed that too. And she can see that since I’ve brought back half, I’ll bring the rest as well, what I’ve squandered: I’ll spend my whole life looking for it, working for it, but I’ll find it and pay it back. So a scoundrel, but not a thief, not a thief, whatever you say, not a thief!”

<!-- B09-C07-P031 -->
“Let us allow that there is a certain difference,” the prosecutor said with a cold smile. “Still, it’s strange that you find the difference so momentous.”

<!-- B09-C07-P032 -->
“Yes, I do find it so momentous! Anyone can be a scoundrel, perhaps everyone is, but not everyone can be a thief, only the scoundrel of scoundrels. Well, I’m no good at these fine distinctions... Only a thief is worse than a scoundrel, that’s my conviction. Listen: I carry the money on me for a whole month, I can make up my mind to return it as soon as tomorrow, and then I’m no longer a scoundrel, but I can’t make up my mind, that’s the thing, although every day I resolve to, although every day I goad myself: ‘Decide, decide, you scoundrel!’ And for a whole month I can’t decide, that’s it! Is that good, do you think, good?”

<!-- B09-C07-P033 -->
“Let us say it’s not so good, I can understand that perfectly well and I don’t dispute it,” the prosecutor answered with restraint. “And in any case, let’s put aside all argument about these niceties and distinctions, and perhaps you would return to the matter at hand. The matter is that you still haven’t explained, although we asked, why you divided the three thousand in the first place, squandering one half and hiding the other. What exactly did you hide it for, what did you actually intend to do with those fifteen hundred you set aside? I insist on an answer to this question, Dmitri Fyodorovich.”

<!-- B09-C07-P034 -->
“Oh yes, of course!” Mitya cried, striking his forehead. “Forgive me, I’m tormenting you and not explaining the main thing, or you’d understand in a flash, because the disgrace lies in the purpose, in that purpose! You see, it was all the old man, the dead man, he kept tempting Agrafena Alexandrovna, and I was jealous. I thought then that she was wavering between him and me, and every day I thought: what if she suddenly decides, what if she gets tired of tormenting me and suddenly says, ‘I love you, not him, take me to the ends of the earth.’ And all I have is two twenty-kopeck pieces; what could I take her away on, what would I do then? I’d be done for. I didn’t know her or understand her then, I thought she wanted money and wouldn’t forgive my poverty. And so, like a snake, I count out half the three thousand and cold-bloodedly sew it up with a needle, sew it up calculatingly, sew it up before the drinking, and then, once it’s sewn up, go off drinking on the other half! No, that’s baseness! Do you understand now?”

<!-- B09-C07-P035 -->
The prosecutor laughed out loud; so did the investigator.

<!-- B09-C07-P036 -->
“To my mind, it was even prudent and moral to restrain yourself and not squander it all,” Nikolai Parfenovich giggled. “What’s so wrong with that?”

<!-- B09-C07-P037 -->
“That I stole it, that’s what! Oh God, your failure to understand horrifies me! All the time I carried those fifteen hundred sewn up against my chest, every day and every hour I told myself, ‘You’re a thief, you’re a thief!’ That’s why I was so savage all month, why I fought in the tavern, why I beat my father, because I felt I was a thief! I couldn’t bring myself, didn’t dare, to tell even Alyosha, my brother, about those fifteen hundred: that’s how deeply I felt myself a scoundrel and a crook! But understand that while I carried them, at the same time, every day and every hour of my life I told myself, ‘No, Dmitri Fyodorovich, perhaps you’re not a thief yet.’ Why? Precisely because tomorrow you can go and give those fifteen hundred back to Katya. And it was only yesterday that I decided to tear my pouch off my neck, as I was walking from Fenya’s to Perkhotin’s; until that moment I couldn’t bring myself to, and the instant I tore it off I became definitively and indisputably a thief, a thief and a dishonorable man for the rest of my life. Why? Because along with the pouch I tore apart my dream of going to Katya and saying, ‘I’m a scoundrel, but not a thief’! Do you understand now, do you understand?”

<!-- B09-C07-P038 -->
“Why did you decide to do it yesterday evening in particular?” Nikolai Parfenovich tried to interrupt.

<!-- B09-C07-P039 -->
“Why? A ridiculous question: because I’d sentenced myself to die at five in the morning, here at daybreak: ‘After all,’ I thought, ‘what difference does it make whether I die a scoundrel or an honorable man!’ Well, it turned out it did make a difference! Would you believe it, gentlemen, what tormented me most last night wasn’t, wasn’t having killed the old servant and facing Siberia—and when? Just when my love had been fulfilled and heaven had opened to me again! Oh, that tormented me, but not so much: still not so much as the cursed knowledge that I’d finally torn that cursed money off my chest and squandered it, and so now I was definitively a thief! Oh, gentlemen, I tell you again, with my heart’s blood: I learned a great deal last night! I learned that it’s impossible not only to live a scoundrel, but to die a scoundrel too... No, gentlemen, one must die honorably!..”

<!-- B09-C07-P040 -->
Mitya was pale. His face looked worn and exhausted, despite his extreme excitement.

<!-- B09-C07-P041 -->
“I’m beginning to understand you, Dmitri Fyodorovich,” the prosecutor said slowly, gently, almost compassionately, “but all this, if you’ll forgive me, is only nerves, in my opinion... your overwrought nerves, that’s what it is. Why, for example, to spare yourself so much torment for almost a whole month, couldn’t you have gone and returned those fifteen hundred to the person who entrusted them to you, and, after explaining yourself to her, why couldn’t you, given your situation at the time, so dreadful as you describe it, have tried the arrangement that so naturally suggests itself? That is, after nobly confessing your errors to her, why not ask that same lady for the sum you needed for your expenses? With her generous heart, and seeing your distress, she surely wouldn’t have refused, especially against a written undertaking, or at least against the same security you offered the merchant Samsonov and Madame Khokhlakov. Even now, after all, you consider that security valuable?”

<!-- B09-C07-P042 -->
Mitya suddenly flushed.

<!-- B09-C07-P043 -->
“Do you really consider me a scoundrel to that extent? You can’t be serious!..” he said indignantly, looking the prosecutor in the eyes, as if unable to believe what he had heard.

<!-- B09-C07-P044 -->
“I assure you I’m serious... Why do you think I’m not?” the prosecutor asked, surprised in his turn.

<!-- B09-C07-P045 -->
“Oh, how vile that would be! Gentlemen, do you know you’re tormenting me! Very well, I’ll tell you everything, so be it, now I’ll confess all my infernal nature, but to shame you, and you yourselves will be astonished at the baseness a combination of human feelings can reach. You should know that I’d already thought of this combination myself, this very one you’ve just described, prosecutor! Yes, gentlemen, I too had that thought during this cursed month, and almost decided to go to Katya, that’s how base I was! But to go to her, tell her of my betrayal, and for that very betrayal, to carry out that betrayal, for the coming expenses of that betrayal, ask her herself, ask Katya for money—ask, do you hear, ask!—and immediately run away from her with another woman, her rival, a woman who hates and insults her—good God, prosecutor, you must be mad!”

<!-- B09-C07-P046 -->
“Mad or not, of course in the heat of the moment I overlooked... this question of feminine jealousy... if there really could have been jealousy, as you maintain... yes, perhaps there is something of the kind here,” the prosecutor said with a smile.

<!-- B09-C07-P047 -->
“But it would be such filth!” Mitya struck the table savagely with his fist. “It would stink so badly I don’t know what! Do you realize she might have given me the money—would have, certainly would have—she’d have given it to take revenge on me, for the pleasure of revenge, she’d have given it out of contempt for me, because she too is an infernal soul, a woman of tremendous wrath! And I’d have taken the money, oh, I’d have taken it, taken it, and then all my life... Oh God! Forgive me, gentlemen, I’m shouting like this because I had that thought so recently, only the day before yesterday, precisely when I was struggling with Pointer that night, and then yesterday, yes, yesterday too, all day yesterday, I remember, right up to this incident...”

<!-- B09-C07-P048 -->
“What incident?” Nikolai Parfenovich put in curiously, but Mitya did not hear.

<!-- B09-C07-P049 -->
“I’ve made you a terrible confession,” he concluded gloomily. “Appreciate it, gentlemen. No, that’s not enough. Don’t merely assess it—treasure it, and if not, if even this passes your souls by, then you simply have no respect for me, gentlemen, that’s what I’m telling you, and I’ll die of shame for having confessed to men like you! Oh, I’ll shoot myself! I can see it already, I can see you don’t believe me! What, you want to write this down too?” he cried, alarmed now.

<!-- B09-C07-P050 -->
“Yes, what you’ve just said,” Nikolai Parfenovich looked at him in surprise, “that until the very last hour you still intended to go to Madame Verkhovtseva to ask her for that sum... I assure you, this is very important testimony for us, Dmitri Fyodorovich, I mean this whole incident... and especially for you, especially important for you.”

<!-- B09-C07-P051 -->
“Have mercy, gentlemen!” Mitya flung up his hands. “At least don’t write that, have some shame! I’ve torn my soul in two before you, so to speak, and you’ve taken advantage of it to poke your fingers into the torn place in both halves... Oh God!”

<!-- B09-C07-P052 -->
He covered his face with his hands in despair.

<!-- B09-C07-P053 -->
“Don’t distress yourself so, Dmitri Fyodorovich,” the prosecutor concluded. “Everything we’ve written will be read back to you, and whatever you disagree with we’ll change in accordance with your words. But now let me repeat one little question for the third time: did no one, really no one at all, hear from you about this money you’d sewn into a pouch? I must tell you, that’s almost impossible to imagine.”

<!-- B09-C07-P054 -->
“No one, no one, I told you, otherwise you’ve understood nothing! Leave me alone.”

<!-- B09-C07-P055 -->
“Very well. The matter must be cleared up, and there’s still a great deal to do before then, but consider for now: we may have dozens of statements that it was you yourself who spread the word, even shouted everywhere, about the three thousand you’d spent, three, not fifteen hundred; and this time too, when yesterday’s money appeared, you managed to inform a number of people that once again you’d brought three thousand with you...”

<!-- B09-C07-P056 -->
“You don’t have dozens of statements, you have hundreds, two hundred statements, two hundred people heard it, a thousand heard it!” Mitya exclaimed.

<!-- B09-C07-P057 -->
“Well, you see, everyone, everyone testifies to it. Surely the word ‘everyone’ means something?”

<!-- B09-C07-P058 -->
“It means nothing. I lied, and everyone started lying after me.”

<!-- B09-C07-P059 -->
“But why did you feel such a need to ‘lie,’ as you put it?”

<!-- B09-C07-P060 -->
“The devil knows. To boast, perhaps... just... about how much money I’d squandered... Perhaps to forget the money I’d sewn up... yes, that’s exactly why... damn... how many times have you asked me that question? Well, I lied, and that’s that, once I’d lied I didn’t want to correct it. Why does a man lie sometimes?”

<!-- B09-C07-P061 -->
“That’s very difficult to determine, Dmitri Fyodorovich, why a man lies,” the prosecutor said gravely. “Tell me, though, was this pouch, as you call it, round your neck a large one?”

<!-- B09-C07-P062 -->
“No, not large.”

<!-- B09-C07-P063 -->
“About what size?”

<!-- B09-C07-P064 -->
“Fold a hundred-ruble note in half—that size.”

<!-- B09-C07-P065 -->
“Better still, why not show us the scraps? You must have them somewhere on you.”

<!-- B09-C07-P066 -->
“Oh, damn... what nonsense... I don’t know where they are.”

<!-- B09-C07-P067 -->
“But wait: where and when did you take it off your neck? By your own statement, you didn’t go home?”

<!-- B09-C07-P068 -->
“When I left Fenya’s and was walking to Perkhotin’s, I tore it off my neck on the way and took out the money.”

<!-- B09-C07-P069 -->
“In the dark?”

<!-- B09-C07-P070 -->
“What would I need a candle for? I did it with my finger in a moment.”

<!-- B09-C07-P071 -->
“Without scissors, in the street?”

<!-- B09-C07-P072 -->
“In the square, I think; why scissors? An old rag, it tore at once.”

<!-- B09-C07-P073 -->
“What did you do with it afterward?”

<!-- B09-C07-P074 -->
“Threw it away there.”

<!-- B09-C07-P075 -->
“Where exactly?”

<!-- B09-C07-P076 -->
“In the square, just somewhere in the square! The devil knows where in the square. Why do you need to know?”

<!-- B09-C07-P077 -->
“It’s extremely important, Dmitri Fyodorovich: material evidence in your own favor. Why won’t you understand that? Who helped you sew it up a month ago?”

<!-- B09-C07-P078 -->
“No one helped, I sewed it myself.”

<!-- B09-C07-P079 -->
“You know how to sew?”

<!-- B09-C07-P080 -->
“A soldier has to know how to sew, and this took no skill at all.”

“Where did you get the material, the rag you sewed it into, that is?”

<!-- B09-C07-P082 -->
“Are you really not joking?”

“Certainly not, and we’re in no mood for jokes, Dmitri Fyodorovich.”

<!-- B09-C07-P084 -->
“I don’t remember where I got the rag, somewhere or other.”

“One would think you’d remember that, at least?”

“I swear to God I don’t remember, perhaps I tore up some linen.”

“That’s very interesting: tomorrow we might find that article in your lodgings, perhaps a shirt you tore a piece from. What was the rag made of: canvas, linen?”

<!-- B09-C07-P088 -->
“The devil knows what. Wait... I don’t think I tore it off anything. It was calico... I think I sewed it into my landlady’s cap.”

<!-- B09-C07-P089 -->
“Your landlady’s cap?”

<!-- B09-C07-P090 -->
“Yes, I pinched it from her.”

<!-- B09-C07-P091 -->
“What do you mean, pinched it?”

<!-- B09-C07-P092 -->
“You see, I do remember pinching a cap once for rags, or perhaps to wipe my pen. I took it on the sly because it was a worthless rag, and the scraps were lying about my room, and then there were those fifteen hundred, so I took them and sewed them up... I think it was those very rags I sewed them into. Some old calico rubbish, washed a thousand times.”

<!-- B09-C07-P093 -->
“And now you remember that definitely?”

<!-- B09-C07-P094 -->
“I don’t know about definitely. I think it was a cap. Oh, who cares!”

<!-- B09-C07-P095 -->
“In that case, your landlady might at least remember that this item went missing?”

<!-- B09-C07-P096 -->
“Not at all. She never missed it. An old rag, I tell you, an old rag, not worth a penny.”

<!-- B09-C07-P097 -->
“And where did you get the needle and thread?”

<!-- B09-C07-P098 -->
“I’m stopping, I won’t say any more. Enough!” Mitya finally lost his temper.

<!-- B09-C07-P099 -->
“And again, it’s strange that you should have completely forgotten the exact spot in the square where you threw this... pouch away.”

<!-- B09-C07-P100 -->
“Well, have the square swept tomorrow, perhaps you’ll find it,” Mitya said with a wry smile. “Enough, gentlemen, enough,” he declared in an exhausted voice. “I see it clearly: you haven’t believed me! Not one thing, not a penny’s worth! My fault, not yours, I shouldn’t have ventured it. Why, why did I make myself loathsome by confessing my secret! And it’s a joke to you, I can see it in your eyes. You drove me to it, prosecutor! Sing a hymn now, if you can... Damn you, you torturers!”

<!-- B09-C07-P101 -->
He lowered his head and covered his face with his hands. The prosecutor and investigator were silent. A minute later he raised his head and looked at them blankly. His face expressed a despair already complete, already irreversible, and he fell strangely quiet, sitting as though unconscious of himself. Meanwhile the business had to be finished: they must proceed without delay to question the witnesses. It was already about eight in the morning. The candles had long since been put out. Mikhail Makarovich and Kalganov, who had been coming and going throughout the interrogation, had both gone out again. The prosecutor and investigator also looked extremely tired. The morning had dawned foul, the whole sky was overcast, and rain was pouring down in buckets. Mitya stared blankly at the windows.

<!-- B09-C07-P102 -->
“May I look out the window?” he suddenly asked Nikolai Parfenovich.

<!-- B09-C07-P103 -->
“Oh, as much as you like,” he answered.

<!-- B09-C07-P104 -->
Mitya got up and went to the window. The rain lashed the small greenish panes. Directly below the window he could see the muddy road, and farther off, in the rainy haze, black, poor, unlovely rows of huts, seeming still blacker and poorer in the rain. Mitya remembered “golden-haired Phoebus” and how he had meant to shoot himself at his first ray. “Perhaps a morning like this would be better,” he thought with a wry smile, and suddenly, flinging his hand down, he turned to his “torturers.”

<!-- B09-C07-P105 -->
“Gentlemen!” he exclaimed. “I can see I’m lost. But she? Tell me about her, I beg you, is she to be lost along with me? She’s innocent, she was beside herself last night when she cried out that it was ‘all her fault.’ She’s guilty of nothing, nothing! I’ve been grieving all night, sitting here with you... Couldn’t you, can’t you tell me what you’re going to do with her now?”

<!-- B09-C07-P106 -->
“Set your mind entirely at rest on that point, Dmitri Fyodorovich,” the prosecutor answered at once, with visible haste. “At present we have no substantial grounds for troubling in any way the lady in whom you take such an interest. I hope the same will hold as the case proceeds... On the contrary, we’ll do everything in that respect that lies in our power. Be quite at ease.”

<!-- B09-C07-P107 -->
“Gentlemen, thank you, I knew all along you were honest and just men after all, in spite of everything. You’ve lifted a burden from my soul... Well, what do we do now? I’m ready.”

<!-- B09-C07-P108 -->
“Well, we ought to hurry. We must proceed without delay to question the witnesses. All of it must take place in your presence, and therefore...”

<!-- B09-C07-P109 -->
“What about a little tea first?” Nikolai Parfenovich interrupted. “Surely we’ve earned it by now!”

<!-- B09-C07-P110 -->
They decided that if there was tea ready downstairs—since Mikhail Makarovich had surely gone off “to have his tea”—they would each drink a glass and then “go on and on.” A proper tea and “a little something to eat” would wait for a less busy hour. There was indeed tea downstairs, and it was soon brought up. At first Mitya refused the glass Nikolai Parfenovich courteously offered him, but then asked for it himself and drank it eagerly. Altogether, he looked quite extraordinarily exhausted. With his prodigious strength, what could one night of revelry and even the most intense emotions have mattered? Yet he himself felt he could scarcely sit upright, and at times everything before his eyes began, as it were, to move and spin. “A little more and I may start raving,” he thought to himself.
