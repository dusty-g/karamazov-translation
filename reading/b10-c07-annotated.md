# Book X: Boys
## VII. Ilyusha

<!-- B10-C07-P001 -->
The doctor came out of the room already wrapped in his fur coat again, his cap on his head. His face was almost angry, full of distaste, as though he were constantly afraid of brushing against something dirty. He cast a quick glance round the entryway, giving Alyosha and Kolya a stern look as he did so. From the doorway Alyosha waved to the coachman, and the carriage that had brought the doctor drew up to the outer door. The staff captain rushed out after the doctor and stopped him for a final word, bending, almost writhing before him. The poor man's face was stricken, his eyes frightened.

<!-- B10-C07-P002 -->
“Your Excellency, Your Excellency... can it really be?..” he began, but could not finish; he merely flung up his hands in despair, still looking at the doctor with a last plea, as though one word from him now really could change the sentence passed on the poor boy.

<!-- B10-C07-P003 -->
“What can be done? I'm not God,” the doctor replied carelessly, though with his habitual air of authority.

<!-- B10-C07-P004 -->
“Doctor... Your Excellency... will it be soon, soon?”

<!-- B10-C07-P005 -->
“Pre-pare your-self for any-thing,” the doctor pronounced, stressing each syllable. Lowering his eyes, he himself prepared to step across the threshold toward the carriage.

<!-- B10-C07-P006 -->
“Your Excellency, for Christ's sake!” the staff captain stopped him once more in terror. “Your Excellency!.. Is there nothing, really nothing, nothing at all that can save him now?..”

<!-- B10-C07-P007 -->
“It no longer de-pends on me,” the doctor said impatiently. “And yet, hm...” He suddenly paused. “If, for example, you could... dis-patch... your patient... immediately and without the slightest delay”—the doctor said “immediately and without the slightest delay” not merely sternly but almost angrily, so that the staff captain even flinched—“to Syr-a-cuse, then... as a result of the new and fa-vor-a-ble cli-mat-ic conditions... it might, perhaps, be possible...”

<!-- B10-C07-P008 -->
“To Syracuse!” the staff captain cried, as though still understanding nothing.

<!-- B10-C07-P009 -->
“Syracuse is in Sicily,” Kolya suddenly interjected loudly, by way of explanation. The doctor looked at him.

<!-- B10-C07-P010 -->
“To Sicily! My dear sir, Your Excellency...” The staff captain was bewildered. “But you've seen for yourself!” He swept both hands round, indicating his surroundings. “And what about Mama, what about the family?”

<!-- B10-C07-P011 -->
“N-no, not Sicily for the family. Your family should go to the Caucasus, early in the spring... your daughter to the Caucasus, and your wife... after a course of the waters, also in the Cau-ca-sus, on account of her rheumatism... immediately afterward dis-patch her to Paris, to the clinic of the psy-chi-a-trist Dr. Le-pel-letier. I could give you a letter to him, and then... it might, perhaps, be possible...”

<!-- B10-C07-P012 -->
“Doctor, doctor! But you can see for yourself!” The staff captain suddenly flung out his hands again, pointing in despair to the bare log walls of the entryway.

<!-- B10-C07-P013 -->
“Ah, that's no concern of mine,” the doctor smiled. “I merely said what sci-ence could say in answer to your question about last resorts. As for the rest... to my regret...”

<!-- B10-C07-P014 -->
“Don't worry, medic, my dog won't bite you,” Kolya broke in loudly, noticing the doctor's somewhat anxious glance at Perezvon, who stood in the doorway. A note of anger rang in Kolya's voice. He had deliberately said “medic” instead of “doctor,” and, as he later declared himself, had “said it to insult him.”

<!-- B10-C07-P015 -->
“What's that?” The doctor jerked up his head and stared at Kolya in surprise. “Who is this?” He suddenly turned to Alyosha, as though demanding an account from him.

<!-- B10-C07-P016 -->
“This is Perezvon's owner, medic. Don't trouble yourself about my identity,” Kolya pronounced again.

<!-- B10-C07-P017 -->
“Zvon?” the doctor repeated, not understanding what Perezvon was.

<!-- B10-C07-P018 -->
“Why, he doesn't know where he is. Goodbye, medic. See you in Syracuse.”

<!-- B10-C07-P019 -->
“Who is this? Who, who?” the doctor suddenly burst out in a terrible temper.

<!-- B10-C07-P020 -->
“A local schoolboy, doctor, a mischievous boy. Pay no attention,” Alyosha said quickly, frowning. “Kolya, be quiet!” he shouted at Krasotkin. “Pay no attention, doctor,” he repeated, somewhat more impatiently.

<!-- B10-C07-P021 -->
“F-flog him! He needs f-flogging, f-flogging!” The doctor began stamping his feet, for some reason quite beside himself now.

<!-- B10-C07-P022 -->
“You know, medic, Perezvon might bite after all!” Kolya said in a trembling young voice, turning pale, his eyes flashing. “Ici, Perezvon!”

<!-- B10-C07-P023 -->
“Kolya, if you say one more word, I'll break with you forever!” Alyosha shouted commandingly.

<!-- B10-C07-P024 -->
“Medic, there's only one person in the whole world who can give Nikolai Krasotkin orders, and that's this man here”—Kolya pointed to Alyosha—“I obey him. Goodbye!”

<!-- B10-C07-P025 -->
He sprang away, opened the door and hurried into the room. Perezvon darted after him. The doctor stood for another five seconds or so as though petrified, looking at Alyosha; then suddenly spat and strode toward the carriage, repeating loudly: “This, this, this, I don't know what this is!” The staff captain rushed to help him in. Alyosha went back into the room after Kolya. He was already standing by Ilyusha's bed. Ilyusha was holding his hand and calling for Papa. A minute later the staff captain returned too.

<!-- B10-C07-P026 -->
“Papa, Papa, come here... we...” Ilyusha began in extreme agitation, but apparently unable to go on, he suddenly flung both wasted little arms forward and clasped them both at once, Kolya and Papa, as tightly as he could, joining them in a single embrace and pressing himself against them. The staff captain's whole body suddenly shook with silent sobs, and Kolya's lips and chin began trembling.

<!-- B10-C07-P027 -->
“Papa, Papa! I'm so sorry for you, Papa!” Ilyusha moaned bitterly.

<!-- B10-C07-P028 -->
“Ilyushechka... my darling... the doctor said... you'll get well... we'll be happy... the doctor...” the staff captain began.

<!-- B10-C07-P029 -->
“Oh, Papa! I know what the new doctor told you about me... I saw!” Ilyusha cried, and again pulled them both close with all his strength, hiding his face on his father's shoulder.

<!-- B10-C07-P030 -->
“Papa, don't cry... and when I die, take a good boy, another one... choose one yourself from all of them, a good one, call him Ilyusha and love him instead of me...”

<!-- B10-C07-P031 -->
“Be quiet, old fellow, you'll get well!” Krasotkin suddenly cried, as though angry.

<!-- B10-C07-P032 -->
“But never forget me, Papa, never,” Ilyusha went on. “Come to my grave... and listen, Papa, bury me by our big stone, the one we used to walk to together, and come to me there with Krasotkin, in the evening... And Perezvon... I'll be waiting for you... Papa, Papa!”

<!-- B10-C07-P033 -->
His voice broke. The three of them remained clasped together, silent now. Ninochka was crying softly in her armchair too, and suddenly, seeing everyone in tears, Mama burst into tears as well.

<!-- B10-C07-P034 -->
“Ilyushechka! Ilyushechka!” she cried. Krasotkin suddenly freed himself from Ilyusha's embrace.

<!-- B10-C07-P035 -->
“Goodbye, old fellow, my mother's expecting me for dinner,” he said rapidly. “What a pity I didn't let her know! She'll be very worried... But I'll come straight back after dinner, for the whole day, the whole evening, and I'll tell you so much, so much! I'll bring Perezvon too, but I'll take him with me now, because he'll start howling without me and disturb you. See you soon!”

<!-- B10-C07-P036 -->
And he ran out into the entryway. He did not want to burst into tears, but in the entryway he did cry. That was how Alyosha found him.

<!-- B10-C07-P037 -->
“Kolya, you absolutely must keep your word and come back, or he'll be terribly upset,” Alyosha said insistently.

<!-- B10-C07-P038 -->
“Of course! Oh, how I curse myself for not coming sooner,” Kolya murmured through his tears, no longer embarrassed to be crying. Just then the staff captain suddenly seemed to burst out of the room, shutting the door behind him at once. His face was distraught, his lips trembling. He stood before the two young men and flung both hands upward.

<!-- B10-C07-P039 -->
“I don't want a good boy! I don't want another boy!” he whispered fiercely, grinding his teeth. “If I forget thee, O Jerusalem, let it cleave...”[^b10-c07-n001]

<!-- B10-C07-P040 -->
He could not finish, as though choking, and sank helplessly to his knees before the wooden bench. Clenching his head between both fists, he began sobbing with strange, absurd little cries, struggling with all his might to keep his cries from being heard inside. Kolya rushed out into the street.

<!-- B10-C07-P041 -->
“Goodbye, Karamazov! Will you be coming yourself?” he shouted sharply and angrily to Alyosha.

<!-- B10-C07-P042 -->
“I'll certainly be here this evening.”

<!-- B10-C07-P043 -->
“What was that he said about Jerusalem... What does it mean?”

<!-- B10-C07-P044 -->
“It's from the Bible: ‘If I forget thee, O Jerusalem’—that is, if I forget what is most precious to me, if I exchange it for anything, then let me be struck...”

<!-- B10-C07-P045 -->
“I understand, that's enough! Make sure you come! Ici, Perezvon!” he shouted at the dog, quite fiercely now, and strode home with long, rapid steps.

[^b10-c07-n001]: Psalm 137:5–6. The father breaks off before the psalm's image of a tongue cleaving to the roof of the mouth.
