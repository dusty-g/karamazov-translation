# Book III: The Lustful
## IX. The Lustful

<!-- B03-C09-P001 -->
Grigory and Smerdyakov ran into the drawing room immediately after Dmitri Fyodorovich. They were the ones who had struggled with him in the passage, trying to keep him out, on instructions Fyodor Pavlovich himself had given several days earlier. Taking advantage of Dmitri Fyodorovich's pause to look around after bursting into the room, Grigory ran around the table and shut both leaves of the door opposite the entrance, which led to the inner rooms. He stood in front of the closed door with his arms spread wide, ready to defend it, so to speak, to the last drop of blood. At the sight, Dmitri gave not so much a shout as a shriek and hurled himself at Grigory.

<!-- B03-C09-P002 -->
“So she's in there! They've hidden her in there! Out of the way, scoundrel!” He tried to yank Grigory aside, but Grigory pushed him back. Beside himself with rage, Dmitri swung and struck Grigory with all his strength. The old man fell as though his legs had been cut from under him, and Dmitri leaped over him and forced his way through the door. Smerdyakov stayed at the far end of the drawing room, pale and trembling, pressed close against Fyodor Pavlovich.

<!-- B03-C09-P003 -->
“She's here!” Dmitri Fyodorovich shouted. “I just saw her turn toward the house myself, but I couldn't catch her. Where is she? Where is she?”

<!-- B03-C09-P004 -->
That cry, “She's here!” had an extraordinary effect on Fyodor Pavlovich: all his fear fell away.

<!-- B03-C09-P005 -->
“Hold him, hold him!” he yelled, and rushed after Dmitri Fyodorovich. Meanwhile Grigory had risen from the floor, though he still seemed dazed. Ivan Fyodorovich and Alyosha ran after their father. From the third room came the sudden sound of something falling to the floor, shattering and ringing out: a large glass vase—not an expensive one—on a marble pedestal, which Dmitri Fyodorovich had knocked against as he ran past.

<!-- B03-C09-P006 -->
“Get him!” the old man yelled. “Help!”

<!-- B03-C09-P007 -->
Ivan Fyodorovich and Alyosha finally caught up with the old man and forcibly brought him back to the drawing room.

<!-- B03-C09-P008 -->
“Why are you chasing him? He really will kill you in there!” Ivan Fyodorovich shouted angrily at his father.

<!-- B03-C09-P009 -->
“Vanechka, Leshechka, then she's here, Grushenka's here, he says he saw her run in himself . . .”

<!-- B03-C09-P010 -->
He could hardly get the words out. He had not been expecting Grushenka this time, and the sudden news that she was here had driven him out of his mind. He was trembling all over; he seemed crazed.

<!-- B03-C09-P011 -->
“But you saw for yourself that she didn't come!” Ivan shouted.

<!-- B03-C09-P012 -->
“Perhaps through the other entrance?”

<!-- B03-C09-P013 -->
“But that entrance is locked, and you've got the key . . .”

<!-- B03-C09-P014 -->
Dmitri suddenly reappeared in the drawing room. He had of course found the other entrance locked, and the key was indeed in Fyodor Pavlovich's pocket. All the windows in all the rooms were locked too. There was therefore no way Grushenka could have come in and no way she could have slipped out.

<!-- B03-C09-P015 -->
“Hold him!” Fyodor Pavlovich shrieked the moment he saw Dmitri again. “He's stolen money from my bedroom!” Breaking free of Ivan, he rushed at Dmitri once more. But Dmitri raised both hands and suddenly seized the old man by the last two wisps of hair left at his temples, yanked him, and hurled him crashing to the floor. Before they could stop him, he struck the fallen man's face two or three times with his heel. The old man let out a piercing groan. Ivan Fyodorovich, though not as strong as his brother Dmitri, threw his arms around him and tore him away from their father with all his strength. Alyosha helped with all the little strength he had, grasping his brother from the front.

<!-- B03-C09-P016 -->
“You madman, you've killed him!” Ivan cried.

<!-- B03-C09-P017 -->
“It serves him right!” Dmitri exclaimed, gasping for breath. “And if I haven't killed him, I'll come back and do it. You won't keep him safe!”

<!-- B03-C09-P018 -->
“Dmitri! Get out of here at once!” Alyosha cried commandingly.

<!-- B03-C09-P019 -->
“Alexei! You tell me, I'll believe only you: was she here just now or wasn't she? I saw her myself, slipping out of the lane past the fence in this direction. I called out, and she ran away . . .”

<!-- B03-C09-P020 -->
“I swear to you, she hasn't been here, and no one here was expecting her at all!”

<!-- B03-C09-P021 -->
“But I saw her . . . Then she . . . I'll find out where she is at once . . . Goodbye, Alexei! Not a word to Aesop about the money now, but go to Katerina Ivanovna at once, without fail: ‘He told me to pay you his respects, pay you his respects, pay you his respects! To bow and take his leave, exactly!’ Describe this scene to her.”

<!-- B03-C09-P022 -->
Meanwhile Ivan and Grigory had lifted the old man and seated him in an armchair. His face was covered in blood, but he was conscious and listening greedily to Dmitri's shouts. He still imagined that Grushenka really was somewhere in the house. As he left, Dmitri Fyodorovich gave him a look of hatred.

<!-- B03-C09-P023 -->
“I'm not sorry I shed your blood!” he exclaimed. “Watch out, old man, guard your dream, because I have a dream too! I curse you myself, and I disown you completely . . .”

<!-- B03-C09-P024 -->
He ran out of the room.

<!-- B03-C09-P025 -->
“She's here, she must be here! Smerdyakov, Smerdyakov,” the old man rasped almost inaudibly, beckoning to Smerdyakov with one finger.

<!-- B03-C09-P026 -->
“She's not here, she's not, you crazy old man!” Ivan shouted at him viciously. “Now he's fainting! Water, a towel! Get moving, Smerdyakov!”

<!-- B03-C09-P027 -->
Smerdyakov rushed for water. At last they undressed the old man, carried him to the bedroom, and laid him in bed. They wrapped a wet towel around his head. Weakened by the cognac, the violent emotions, and the beating, he rolled his eyes back and sank into oblivion the instant his head touched the pillow. Ivan Fyodorovich and Alyosha returned to the drawing room. Smerdyakov was carrying out the pieces of the broken vase, while Grigory stood at the table with his eyes lowered gloomily.

<!-- B03-C09-P028 -->
“Shouldn't you wet your head too and go to bed?” Alyosha asked Grigory. “We'll look after him here. My brother hit you terribly hard . . . on the head.”

<!-- B03-C09-P029 -->
“He dared strike me!” Grigory said darkly, pronouncing each word distinctly.

<!-- B03-C09-P030 -->
“He ‘dared strike’ his father too, never mind you!” Ivan Fyodorovich remarked, twisting his mouth.

<!-- B03-C09-P031 -->
“I washed him in a tub . . . he dared strike me!” Grigory repeated.

<!-- B03-C09-P032 -->
“Damn it, if I hadn't pulled him off, he might well have killed him. It wouldn't take much to finish Aesop, would it?” Ivan Fyodorovich whispered to Alyosha.

<!-- B03-C09-P033 -->
“God forbid!” Alyosha exclaimed.

<!-- B03-C09-P034 -->
“Why ‘forbid’?” Ivan continued in the same whisper, his face twisted with malice. “One reptile will eat the other, and good riddance to them both!”

<!-- B03-C09-P035 -->
Alyosha shuddered.

<!-- B03-C09-P036 -->
“Of course I won't let a murder happen, just as I didn't now. Stay here, Alyosha. I'll go for a walk in the yard; my head's beginning to ache.”

<!-- B03-C09-P037 -->
Alyosha went to his father's bedroom and sat by his pillow, behind the screens, for about an hour. The old man suddenly opened his eyes and looked at Alyosha for a long time without speaking, evidently trying to remember and make sense of things. Suddenly an extraordinary agitation showed in his face.

<!-- B03-C09-P038 -->
“Alyosha,” he whispered fearfully, “where's Ivan?”

<!-- B03-C09-P039 -->
“In the yard. He has a headache. He's keeping watch over us.”

<!-- B03-C09-P040 -->
“Give me the little mirror, it's over there, give it to me!”

<!-- B03-C09-P041 -->
Alyosha brought him the little round folding mirror that stood on the chest of drawers. The old man looked at himself: his nose was badly swollen, and on his forehead, above his left eyebrow, was a large purple bruise.

<!-- B03-C09-P042 -->
“What does Ivan say? Alyosha, my dear, my only son, I'm afraid of Ivan. I'm more afraid of Ivan than of the other one. You're the only one I'm not afraid of . . .”

<!-- B03-C09-P043 -->
“Don't be afraid of Ivan either. Ivan is angry, but he'll protect you.”

<!-- B03-C09-P044 -->
“Alyosha, what about the other one? He's run off to Grushenka! My dear angel, tell me the truth: was Grushenka here earlier or wasn't she?”

<!-- B03-C09-P045 -->
“No one saw her. It's not true, she wasn't here!”

<!-- B03-C09-P046 -->
“But Mitka wants to marry her, marry her!”

<!-- B03-C09-P047 -->
“She won't marry him.”

<!-- B03-C09-P048 -->
“She won't, she won't, she won't, she won't, she never will!” The old man came alive with joy, as though nothing could have delighted him more at that moment. In rapture he seized Alyosha's hand and pressed it hard against his heart. Tears even shone in his eyes. “That little icon, the Mother of God, the one I told you about earlier—take it for yourself, take it with you. And I give you leave to go back to the monastery . . . I was joking earlier, don't be angry. My head aches, Alyosha . . . Lesha, put my heart at ease, be an angel, tell me the truth!”

<!-- B03-C09-P049 -->
“Are you still asking whether she was here or not?” Alyosha said sorrowfully.

<!-- B03-C09-P050 -->
“No, no, no, I believe you. It's this: go to Grushenka yourself, or find some way to see her. Ask her quickly, as quickly as you can; judge for yourself, with your own eyes: which of us does she want, me or him? Eh? Well? Can you or can't you?”

<!-- B03-C09-P051 -->
“If I see her, I'll ask,” Alyosha began to mumble in embarrassment.

<!-- B03-C09-P052 -->
“No, she won't tell you,” the old man interrupted. “She's a minx. She'll start kissing you and say she wants to marry you. She's a liar, she's shameless. No, you mustn't go to her, you mustn't!”

<!-- B03-C09-P053 -->
“And it wouldn't be right, Father, it wouldn't be right at all.”

<!-- B03-C09-P054 -->
“Where was he sending you earlier, when he shouted ‘Go’ as he ran out?”

<!-- B03-C09-P055 -->
“He was sending me to Katerina Ivanovna.”

<!-- B03-C09-P056 -->
“For money? To ask for money?”

<!-- B03-C09-P057 -->
“No, not for money.”

<!-- B03-C09-P058 -->
“He has no money, not a drop. Listen, Alyosha, I'll lie here tonight and think it over. You go for now. Perhaps you'll meet her too . . . Only be sure to come and see me tomorrow morning, be sure. There's a little something I'll tell you tomorrow. Will you come?”

<!-- B03-C09-P059 -->
“I will.”

<!-- B03-C09-P060 -->
“When you come, make it seem you came of your own accord, just to visit. Don't tell anyone I sent for you. Don't say a word to Ivan.”

<!-- B03-C09-P061 -->
“All right.”

<!-- B03-C09-P062 -->
“Goodbye, angel. You stood up for me earlier, I'll never forget it as long as I live. There's a little something I'll tell you tomorrow . . . but I still need to think . . .”

<!-- B03-C09-P063 -->
“And how are you feeling now?”

<!-- B03-C09-P064 -->
“Tomorrow, I'll get up and walk about tomorrow. I'm perfectly well, perfectly well, perfectly well!”

<!-- B03-C09-P065 -->
Crossing the yard, Alyosha came upon his brother Ivan on a bench by the gate, writing something in his notebook in pencil. Alyosha told Ivan that the old man had woken and was in his senses, and had let him go to spend the night at the monastery.

<!-- B03-C09-P066 -->
“Alyosha, I'd be very glad to see you tomorrow morning,” Ivan said, half rising, with a warmth quite unexpected to Alyosha.

<!-- B03-C09-P067 -->
“I'll be at the Khokhlakovs' tomorrow,” Alyosha replied. “Perhaps I'll be at Katerina Ivanovna's tomorrow too, if I don't find her in now . . .”

<!-- B03-C09-P068 -->
“So you're going to Katerina Ivanovna now, after all! Is that to ‘take his leave, take his leave’?” Ivan suddenly smiled. Alyosha grew embarrassed.

<!-- B03-C09-P069 -->
“I think I understood it all from those shouts earlier, and a few things before that. Dmitri must have asked you to go to her and tell her that he . . . well . . . well, in short, ‘takes his leave’?”

<!-- B03-C09-P070 -->
“Brother! How will this whole dreadful business between Father and Dmitri end?” Alyosha exclaimed.

<!-- B03-C09-P071 -->
“There's no telling for sure. Perhaps in nothing: it may all come to nothing. That woman is a beast. In any case, we must keep the old man inside the house and Dmitri out of it.”

<!-- B03-C09-P072 -->
“Brother, let me ask you something else: does everyone really have the right to look at other people and decide which of them deserves to live and which is less worthy of it?”

<!-- B03-C09-P073 -->
“Why bring a judgment of merit into it? In people's hearts that question is usually decided on something quite other than merit, for other, much more natural reasons. And as for the right, who doesn't have the right to wish?”

<!-- B03-C09-P074 -->
“Surely not for someone else's death?”

<!-- B03-C09-P075 -->
“And what if it is for their death? Why lie to yourself when everyone lives like that, and perhaps can't live any other way? Is this about what I said earlier, that ‘two reptiles will eat each other’? In that case, let me ask you something too: do you think I'm capable, like Dmitri, of shedding Aesop's blood—well, of killing him, eh?”

<!-- B03-C09-P076 -->
“Ivan, what are you saying! The thought has never even entered my head! And I don't think Dmitri is either . . .”

<!-- B03-C09-P077 -->
“Thank you for that much, at least,” Ivan said with a wry smile. “Understand that I'll always protect Father. But in this case I reserve complete freedom for my wishes. Till tomorrow. Don't condemn me or look on me as a villain,” he added with a smile.

<!-- B03-C09-P078 -->
They shook hands firmly, as they never had before. Alyosha felt that his brother had taken the first step toward him of his own accord, and that he had done it for a reason, certainly with some intention.
