# Book IV: Strains
## IV. At the Khokhlakovs'

<!-- B04-C04-P001 -->
He soon reached Madame Khokhlakov's house, a handsome two-story stone house of her own, one of the finest in our little town. Although she lived mostly in another province, where she had an estate, or in Moscow, where she owned a house, she also had a house in our town, inherited from her forefathers. Moreover, the estate she owned in our district was the largest of her three estates, and yet until now she had very rarely visited our province. She came running to meet Alyosha while he was still in the entrance hall.

<!-- B04-C04-P002 -->
“Did you get it, did you get my letter about the new miracle?” she began, speaking quickly and nervously.

<!-- B04-C04-P003 -->
“Yes, I did.”

<!-- B04-C04-P004 -->
“Have you spread the news, shown everyone? He gave a mother back her son!”

<!-- B04-C04-P005 -->
“He'll die today,” said Alyosha.

<!-- B04-C04-P006 -->
“I've heard, I know. Oh, how I want to talk to you! To you or to someone about all of this. No, to you, to you! And what a pity that I can't possibly see him! The whole town is stirred up, everyone's waiting. But now… Do you know that Katerina Ivanovna is here with us?”

<!-- B04-C04-P007 -->
“Oh, how lucky!” Alyosha exclaimed. “Then I can see her here. Yesterday she told me I must come to see her today.”

<!-- B04-C04-P008 -->
“I know everything, everything. I've heard every detail of what happened at her house yesterday… and all those horrors with that… creature. *C'est tragique*[^b04-c04-n001], and if I were in her place—I don't know what I'd do in her place! But that brother of yours, that Dmitri Fyodorovich of yours—what a man! Oh, my God! Alexei Fyodorovich, I'm getting muddled. Just imagine: your brother is sitting in there now—I mean not that one, not yesterday's terrible one, but the other, Ivan Fyodorovich. He's sitting talking to her; it's a solemn conversation… And if you could only believe what's going on between them now—it's dreadful, I tell you; it's a terrible strain, a dreadful fairy tale that no one could possibly believe: they're both destroying themselves for no reason anyone can understand, they know it themselves, and they're enjoying it. I've been waiting for you! I've been longing for you! The thing is, I simply can't bear it. I'll tell you everything in a moment, but now there's something else, and this is the most important thing—oh, I'd even forgotten that it's the most important! Tell me, why is Lise having hysterics? The moment she heard you were coming, she went straight into hysterics!”

<!-- B04-C04-P009 -->
“*Maman*, it's you who's having hysterics now, not me,” Lise's small voice suddenly chirped through a crack in the door of a side room. The crack was very narrow, and her voice had a strained, breaking sound, exactly as if she desperately wanted to laugh and was fighting it with all her strength. Alyosha noticed the crack at once, and Lise was certainly peering at him through it from her wheeled chair, though he could not make her out.

<!-- B04-C04-P010 -->
“No wonder, Lise, no wonder… Your whims will give me hysterics too. But she is so ill, Alexei Fyodorovich. She was so ill all night, feverish, groaning! I could hardly wait for morning and Herzenstube. He says he can't make sense of anything and that we must wait. That Herzenstube always comes and says he can't make sense of anything. As soon as you came up to the house she cried out and had a fit, and ordered us to wheel her here into her old room…”

<!-- B04-C04-P011 -->
“Mama, I didn't know he was coming at all. It wasn't on his account that I wanted to come into this room.”

<!-- B04-C04-P012 -->
“That's not true, Lise. Yulia ran to tell you Alexei Fyodorovich was coming; you'd posted her as a lookout.”

<!-- B04-C04-P013 -->
“Mama, my darling, that wasn't very clever of you. If you'd like to make up for it and say something very clever now, tell our honored visitor, Mr. Alexei Fyodorovich, dear Mama, that he has proved his lack of wit simply by daring to visit us today after yesterday, even though everyone is laughing at him.”

<!-- B04-C04-P014 -->
“Lise, you take far too many liberties, and I assure you I'll be forced to be strict with you at last. Who's laughing at him? I'm so glad he's come. I need him, I absolutely need him. Oh, Alexei Fyodorovich, I'm terribly unhappy!”

<!-- B04-C04-P015 -->
“Whatever is the matter, Mama darling?”

<!-- B04-C04-P016 -->
“Oh, these whims of yours, Lise, your changeableness, your illness, that dreadful feverish night, that dreadful, everlasting Herzenstube—above all everlasting, everlasting, everlasting! And then everything, everything… And then even this miracle! Oh, how it struck me, how it shook me, dear Alexei Fyodorovich! And now that tragedy in the drawing room, which I cannot endure, cannot—I warn you now, I cannot. A comedy, perhaps, rather than a tragedy. Tell me, will Elder Zosima live until tomorrow? Will he? Oh, my God! What's happening to me? Every moment I close my eyes and see that it's all nonsense, all nonsense.”

<!-- B04-C04-P017 -->
“Could I please ask you,” Alyosha suddenly interrupted, “for a clean piece of cloth to wrap around my finger? I've hurt it badly, and it's hurting terribly now.”

<!-- B04-C04-P018 -->
Alyosha unwrapped his bitten finger. The handkerchief was thickly stained with blood. Madame Khokhlakov cried out and squeezed her eyes shut.

<!-- B04-C04-P019 -->
“God, what a wound! How horrible!”

<!-- B04-C04-P020 -->
But as soon as Lise saw Alyosha's finger through the crack, she flung the door wide open.

<!-- B04-C04-P021 -->
“Come in, come in here to me!” she cried insistently, commandingly. “No more nonsense now! Oh, Lord, why did you stand there all that time without saying anything? He could have bled to death, Mama! Where did you—how did you—? Water first, water! We must wash the wound. Just put it in cold water to stop the pain and keep it there, keep it there… Quickly, quickly, some water, Mama, in a rinsing bowl. Hurry!” she finished nervously. She was thoroughly frightened; Alyosha's wound had shocked her terribly.

<!-- B04-C04-P022 -->
“Shouldn't we send for Herzenstube?” Madame Khokhlakov started to exclaim.

<!-- B04-C04-P023 -->
“Mama, you'll be the death of me. Your Herzenstube will come and say he can't make sense of it! Water, water! Mama, for God's sake, go yourself and hurry Yulia up. She's got stuck somewhere, as usual. She never comes quickly! Hurry, Mama, or I'll die…”

<!-- B04-C04-P024 -->
“But it's nothing!” Alyosha cried, alarmed by their alarm.

<!-- B04-C04-P025 -->
Yulia came running with water. Alyosha put his finger in it.

<!-- B04-C04-P026 -->
“Mama, for God's sake, bring some lint. Lint and that stinging, cloudy water for cuts—what's it called? We have some, we have, we have… Mama, you know where the bottle is, in the little cupboard on the right in your bedroom. There's a big bottle there, and lint…”

<!-- B04-C04-P027 -->
“I'll bring everything at once, Lise, only don't shout and don't upset yourself. See how bravely Alexei Fyodorovich is bearing his misfortune. Where could you have hurt yourself so dreadfully, Alexei Fyodorovich?”

<!-- B04-C04-P028 -->
Madame Khokhlakov hurried out. Lise had only been waiting for this.

<!-- B04-C04-P029 -->
“First of all, answer my question,” she began rapidly. “Where did you have the goodness to injure yourself like that? Then I have something quite different to discuss with you. Well?”

<!-- B04-C04-P030 -->
Instinctively sensing that the time before her mother's return was precious to her, Alyosha hurriedly told her about his puzzling encounter with the schoolboys. He left out and shortened a great deal, but his account was accurate and clear. When she had heard it, Lise threw up her hands.

<!-- B04-C04-P031 -->
“How could you, how could you get mixed up with a lot of boys—and dressed like that!” she cried angrily, as though she had some claim over him. “Why, you're a boy yourself after that, the smallest little boy there could possibly be! But you must find out about that horrid boy for me somehow, and tell me everything, because there's some secret here. Now for the second thing, but first a question: Alexei Fyodorovich, in spite of the pain you're suffering, can you talk about absolute nonsense, but talk sensibly?”

<!-- B04-C04-P032 -->
“Of course I can. Besides, it doesn't hurt nearly so much now.”

<!-- B04-C04-P033 -->
“That's because your finger is in the water. We must change it at once; it'll get warm in no time. Yulia, run and fetch a piece of ice from the cellar, and a fresh rinsing bowl of water. There, now she's gone, I'll get to the point: dear Alexei Fyodorovich, kindly give me back the letter I sent you yesterday, this instant—this instant, because Mama may come back any moment, and I don't want…”

<!-- B04-C04-P034 -->
“I don't have the letter with me.”

<!-- B04-C04-P035 -->
“That's not true, you do. I knew you'd say that. It's in that pocket of yours. I regretted that silly joke so much all night. Give the letter back now, give it to me!”

<!-- B04-C04-P036 -->
“I left it there.”

<!-- B04-C04-P037 -->
“But you mustn't think I'm a little girl, a tiny little girl, because of that letter and its silly joke! I beg your pardon for the silly joke, but you must bring the letter back to me if you really don't have it with you. Bring it today, without fail, without fail!”

<!-- B04-C04-P038 -->
“Today is quite impossible, because I'm going back to the monastery, and I won't come here for two or three days, perhaps four, because Elder Zosima…”

<!-- B04-C04-P039 -->
“Four days! What nonsense! Listen, did you laugh at me very much?”

<!-- B04-C04-P040 -->
“I didn't laugh at all.”

<!-- B04-C04-P041 -->
“Why not?”

<!-- B04-C04-P042 -->
“Because I believed every word of it.”

<!-- B04-C04-P043 -->
“You're insulting me!”

<!-- B04-C04-P044 -->
“Not at all. As soon as I read it, I thought that was just how it would happen, because the moment Elder Zosima dies, I must leave the monastery. Then I'll go on with my studies and take my examination, and when the lawful time comes, we'll get married. I'll love you. Although I haven't had time to think yet, I did think I couldn't find a better wife than you, and the elder tells me to marry…”

<!-- B04-C04-P045 -->
“But I'm a cripple! They wheel me around in a chair!” Lise laughed, a flush spreading over her cheeks.

<!-- B04-C04-P046 -->
“I'll wheel you around myself, but I'm sure you'll be well by then.”

<!-- B04-C04-P047 -->
“But you're mad,” Lise said nervously. “To take a joke like that and suddenly turn it into such nonsense!… Oh, here's Mama, perhaps just in time. Mama, you're always so late. How could you take so long? Look, Yulia's bringing the ice already!”

<!-- B04-C04-P048 -->
“Oh, Lise, don't shout. Above all, don't shout. All this shouting makes me… What could I do, when you'd put the lint away somewhere else yourself?… I looked and looked… I suspect you did it on purpose.”

<!-- B04-C04-P049 -->
“But I couldn't have known he'd come here with a bitten finger, or perhaps I really would have done it on purpose. Mama, my angel, you're beginning to say extraordinarily witty things.”

<!-- B04-C04-P050 -->
“Witty they may be, but what feelings you're showing, Lise, about Alexei Fyodorovich's finger and all the rest of it! Oh, dear Alexei Fyodorovich, it's not the individual things that are killing me, not some Herzenstube or other, but everything together, the whole of it. That's what I can't bear.”

<!-- B04-C04-P051 -->
“Enough, Mama, enough about Herzenstube,” Lise laughed merrily. “Give me the lint and the water, quickly, Mama. It's only lead lotion, Alexei Fyodorovich. I've remembered the name now, but it's an excellent lotion. Mama, just imagine, he got into a fight with boys in the street on his way here, and one of the boys bit him. Isn't he a little boy himself, isn't he? And can he get married after that, Mama? Because, imagine it, he wants to get married, Mama. Just imagine him married—isn't it ridiculous, isn't it dreadful?”

<!-- B04-C04-P052 -->
Lise kept laughing her small, nervous laugh, looking slyly at Alyosha.

<!-- B04-C04-P053 -->
“Married, Lise? What for? And this is hardly the time for you to… when that boy might be rabid.”

<!-- B04-C04-P054 -->
“Oh, Mama! Can boys be rabid?”

<!-- B04-C04-P055 -->
“Why shouldn't they be, Lise? You act as if I'd said something stupid. Your boy was bitten by a mad dog, so he became a rabid boy, and then he goes and bites someone near him in his turn. How well she's bandaged it for you, Alexei Fyodorovich. I could never have done it so well. Does it hurt now?”

<!-- B04-C04-P056 -->
“Very little now.”

<!-- B04-C04-P057 -->
“And aren't you afraid of water?” Lise asked.

<!-- B04-C04-P058 -->
“That's enough, Lise. Perhaps I really did speak too hastily about a rabid boy, but you're so quick to draw conclusions. Katerina Ivanovna has just learned you're here, Alexei Fyodorovich, and came rushing to me. She's longing for you, longing for you.”

<!-- B04-C04-P059 -->
“Oh, Mama! Go to her on your own. He can't go now, he's in too much pain.”

<!-- B04-C04-P060 -->
“I'm not in any pain. I can perfectly well go,” said Alyosha.

<!-- B04-C04-P061 -->
“What! You're leaving? So that's how you are? That's how you are?”

<!-- B04-C04-P062 -->
“Well, what is it? When I'm finished there, I'll come back, and we can talk again for as long as you like. But I'd very much like to see Katerina Ivanovna soon, because in any case I want to get back to the monastery as early as possible today.”

<!-- B04-C04-P063 -->
“Mama, take him away, quickly. Alexei Fyodorovich, don't trouble to come and see me after Katerina Ivanovna. Go straight to your monastery. That's where you belong! I want to sleep. I didn't sleep all night.”

<!-- B04-C04-P064 -->
“Oh, Lise, you're only joking, but if only you really would fall asleep!” Madame Khokhlakov exclaimed.

<!-- B04-C04-P065 -->
“I don't know what I've… I'll stay another three minutes if you like, even five,” Alyosha murmured.

<!-- B04-C04-P066 -->
“Even five! Take him away at once, Mama. He's a monster!”

<!-- B04-C04-P067 -->
“Lise, you've lost your mind. Come along, Alexei Fyodorovich. She's so capricious today that I'm afraid to upset her. Oh, the trouble with a nervous woman, Alexei Fyodorovich! But perhaps your company really has made her sleepy. How did you make her sleepy so quickly? How fortunate!”

<!-- B04-C04-P068 -->
“Oh, Mama, you've started saying such lovely things. A kiss for you, dear Mama, for that.”

<!-- B04-C04-P069 -->
“And one for you too, Lise. Listen, Alexei Fyodorovich,” Madame Khokhlakov began in a rapid, mysterious, portentous whisper as she left with Alyosha. “I don't want to put any ideas into your head or lift the veil, but go in and see for yourself what's happening in there. It's dreadful, it's the most fantastic comedy: she loves your brother Ivan Fyodorovich and is trying with all her might to convince herself that she loves your brother Dmitri Fyodorovich. It's dreadful! I'll go in with you, and if they don't turn me out, I'll stay until the end.”

[^b04-c04-n001]: Madame Khokhlakov's French means “It's tragic.”
