# Book X: Boys
## VI. Precocity

<!-- B10-C06-P001 -->
“What do you think the doctor will tell him?” Kolya said rapidly. “What a disgusting mug he has, though, don't you think? I can't stand medicine!”

<!-- B10-C06-P002 -->
“Ilyusha is going to die. I think that's certain now,” Alyosha answered sadly.

<!-- B10-C06-P003 -->
“Rogues! Medicine's a rogue! Still, I'm glad to have met you, Karamazov. I've wanted to know you for a long time. It's only a pity we've met so sadly...”

<!-- B10-C06-P004 -->
Kolya very much wanted to say something warmer, more expansive, but something seemed to hold him back. Alyosha noticed it, smiled and shook his hand.

<!-- B10-C06-P005 -->
“I learned long ago to respect you as a rare being,” Kolya murmured again, stumbling and growing confused. “I've heard you're a mystic, and that you were in a monastery. I know you're a mystic, but... that didn't put me off. Contact with reality will cure you... It can't be otherwise with a nature like yours.”

<!-- B10-C06-P006 -->
“What do you mean by a mystic? Cure me of what?” Alyosha asked, a little surprised.

<!-- B10-C06-P007 -->
“Well, God and all that.”

<!-- B10-C06-P008 -->
“What, don't you believe in God?”

<!-- B10-C06-P009 -->
“On the contrary, I have nothing against God. Of course, God is only a hypothesis... but... I acknowledge he's necessary, for order... for the order of the universe and so forth... and if he didn't exist we'd have to invent him,” Kolya added, beginning to blush. He suddenly imagined that Alyosha would think he was trying to display his learning and show how “grown-up” he was. “But I don't want to display my learning to him at all,” Kolya thought indignantly. And he suddenly felt terribly annoyed.

<!-- B10-C06-P010 -->
“I confess, I can't stand getting into all these disputes,” he broke off sharply. “You can love humanity without believing in God, can't you? What do you think? Voltaire didn't believe in God, but he loved humanity, didn't he?” (“Again, again!” he thought to himself.)

<!-- B10-C06-P011 -->
“Voltaire did believe in God, though not very much, I think, and I think he didn't love humanity very much either,” Alyosha said quietly, with restraint and perfect naturalness, as though speaking to someone his own age or even older. What particularly struck Kolya was Alyosha's apparent uncertainty about his opinion of Voltaire, and the way he seemed to be leaving the question for him, little Kolya, to decide.

<!-- B10-C06-P012 -->
“But have you read Voltaire?” Alyosha concluded.

<!-- B10-C06-P013 -->
“No, not exactly read him... I have read *Candide*, though, in a Russian translation... an old, dreadful translation, a funny one...” (Again, again!)

<!-- B10-C06-P014 -->
“And understood it?”

<!-- B10-C06-P015 -->
“Oh yes, all of it... that is... why do you think I wouldn't understand it? There's a good deal of smut in it, of course... Naturally I'm capable of understanding that it's a philosophical novel, written to put across an idea...” Kolya was now hopelessly entangled. “I'm a socialist, Karamazov, an incorrigible socialist,” he suddenly broke out, apropos of nothing.

<!-- B10-C06-P016 -->
“A socialist?” Alyosha laughed. “When did you find the time? You're only thirteen, aren't you?”

<!-- B10-C06-P017 -->
Kolya writhed.

<!-- B10-C06-P018 -->
“In the first place, I'm not thirteen but fourteen—fourteen in two weeks,” he flared up. “And in the second, I fail to see what my age has to do with it. What matters is what my convictions are, not how old I am, isn't that right?”

<!-- B10-C06-P019 -->
“When you're older you'll see for yourself what age has to do with convictions. I also had the impression that the words you were saying weren't your own,” Alyosha answered modestly and calmly, but Kolya interrupted him heatedly.

<!-- B10-C06-P020 -->
“Come now, you want obedience and mysticism. You must admit, for instance, that the Christian faith has only served the rich and powerful to keep the lower class enslaved, isn't that so?”

<!-- B10-C06-P021 -->
“Oh, I know where you read that, and someone must have taught you!” Alyosha exclaimed.

<!-- B10-C06-P022 -->
“Come now, why must I have read it? Nobody taught me anything of the sort. I can think for myself... And if you want to know, I have nothing against Christ. He was an entirely humane individual, and if he lived in our time he'd join the revolutionaries at once and perhaps play a prominent role... In fact, he certainly would.”

<!-- B10-C06-P023 -->
“Where, where have you picked all this up? What fool have you got mixed up with?” Alyosha exclaimed.

<!-- B10-C06-P024 -->
“Come now, you can't hide the truth. Admittedly, for a particular reason, I often talk with Mr. Rakitin, but... They say old Belinsky used to say the same thing.”

<!-- B10-C06-P025 -->
“Belinsky? I don't remember. He never wrote that anywhere.”

<!-- B10-C06-P026 -->
“If he didn't write it, they say he said it. I heard it from someone... but damn...”

<!-- B10-C06-P027 -->
“And have you read Belinsky?”

<!-- B10-C06-P028 -->
“You see... no... I haven't exactly read him, but... I read the passage about Tatyana, why she didn't go away with Onegin.”

<!-- B10-C06-P029 -->
“What do you mean, didn't go away with Onegin? Do you already... understand that?”

<!-- B10-C06-P030 -->
“Come now, you seem to take me for that little boy Smurov,” Kolya said with an irritated grin. “Still, please don't think I'm such a revolutionary. I very often disagree with Mr. Rakitin. Just because I mentioned Tatyana doesn't mean I'm in favor of women's emancipation. I recognize that woman is a subordinate being and must obey. *Les femmes tricottent*[^b10-c06-n001], as Napoleon said”—Kolya smiled for some reason—“and on that point at least I entirely share the convictions of that supposedly great man. I also think, for example, that fleeing one's own country for America is vile—worse than vile, stupid. Why America, when we can do a great deal for humanity right here? Especially now. There are masses of opportunities for useful work. That's just what I said in reply.”

<!-- B10-C06-P031 -->
“What reply? To whom? Has someone already invited you to America?”

<!-- B10-C06-P032 -->
“I confess, someone tried to talk me into it, but I refused. This is between ourselves, of course, Karamazov, do you hear? Not a word to anyone. I'm only telling you. I have no wish to fall into the clutches of the Third Department and take lessons by the Chain Bridge.[^b10-c06-n002]

“Remember? Magnificent! What are you laughing at? Surely you don't think I've made it all up?” (“What if he finds out that I've only got that one issue of *The Bell* in my father's bookcase and haven't read anything else of the sort?” Kolya thought fleetingly, but with a shudder.)

<!-- B10-C06-P033 -->
“Oh no, I'm not laughing, and I don't think you've been telling me lies at all. That's just the trouble: I don't think so, because unfortunately it's all the absolute truth! But tell me, have you read Pushkin, *Onegin* itself?... You mentioned Tatyana just now.”

<!-- B10-C06-P034 -->
“No, I haven't read it yet, but I mean to. I'm free of prejudice, Karamazov. I want to hear both sides. Why did you ask?”

<!-- B10-C06-P035 -->
“No particular reason.”

<!-- B10-C06-P036 -->
“Tell me, Karamazov, do you utterly despise me?” Kolya suddenly demanded, drawing himself up before Alyosha as though taking a stand. “Please be good enough to answer plainly.”

<!-- B10-C06-P037 -->
“Despise you?” Alyosha looked at him in surprise. “But why? I'm only sad that a lovely nature like yours, which has hardly begun to live, has already been distorted by all this coarse nonsense.”

<!-- B10-C06-P038 -->
“Don't worry about my nature,” Kolya interrupted, not without complacency. “But it's true that I'm suspicious. Stupidly suspicious, crudely suspicious. You smiled just now, and I thought you were perhaps...”

<!-- B10-C06-P039 -->
“Oh, I smiled at something else entirely. This is what I was smiling at: recently I read a comment by a German from abroad who'd lived in Russia, about our students today. ‘Show a Russian schoolboy,’ he writes, ‘a map of the stars, something of which he previously had no idea, and tomorrow he'll return it to you corrected.’ No knowledge at all and boundless self-conceit—that's what the German meant about the Russian schoolboy.”

<!-- B10-C06-P040 -->
“Oh, but that's perfectly true!” Kolya suddenly burst out laughing. “Verissimo, exactly! Bravo, German! Still, the foreign clod didn't notice the good side, did he? What do you think? Self-conceit, yes, granted, that's youth, it'll mend—if it needs mending. But there's an independent spirit too, almost from childhood, courage in thought and conviction, instead of their sausage-makers' groveling before authority... All the same, the German put it well! Bravo, German! Though we ought to strangle the Germans all the same. Let them be strong in the sciences, we ought to strangle them anyway...”

<!-- B10-C06-P041 -->
“But why strangle them?” Alyosha smiled.

<!-- B10-C06-P042 -->
“Well, perhaps I said something untrue, I admit it. Sometimes I'm a terrible child, and when I'm happy about something I can't help myself, I'm ready to spout all sorts of nonsense. Listen, we're standing here chattering about trifles, and that doctor's been stuck in there for quite a while. Perhaps he's examining ‘Mama’ and Ninochka, who can't walk. You know, I liked Ninochka. As I was leaving, she suddenly whispered to me: ‘Why didn't you come earlier?’ In such a voice, so reproachful! I think she's terribly kind, and so pitiable.”

<!-- B10-C06-P043 -->
“Yes, yes! When you come to see them, you'll find out what kind of person she is. It's very good for you to get to know such people, to learn to value them, and much else besides that you'll discover precisely by knowing people like them,” Alyosha observed warmly. “That will change you more than anything.”

<!-- B10-C06-P044 -->
“Oh, how sorry I am, how I reproach myself for not coming sooner!” Kolya exclaimed bitterly.

<!-- B10-C06-P045 -->
“Yes, it's a great pity. You saw for yourself how happy you made the poor little boy! And how he wore himself out waiting for you!”

<!-- B10-C06-P046 -->
“Don't tell me! You're making it hurt worse. Still, I deserve it. I stayed away out of pride, out of selfish pride and a vile urge to dominate, which I've never been able to rid myself of all my life, although I've struggled against myself all my life. I see it now: in many ways I'm a scoundrel, Karamazov.”

<!-- B10-C06-P047 -->
“No, you have a lovely nature, though it's been distorted, and I understand only too well how you could have such influence over that noble, painfully sensitive boy!” Alyosha answered warmly.

<!-- B10-C06-P048 -->
“And you're saying that to me!” Kolya cried. “Imagine, I thought—several times already, since I've been here, I've thought you despised me! If only you knew how much your opinion matters to me!”

<!-- B10-C06-P049 -->
“But are you really so suspicious? At your age! Imagine, watching you in the room while you were telling your story, I actually thought you must be very suspicious.”

<!-- B10-C06-P050 -->
“You did think it? What an eye you have—you see, you see! I'll wager it was when I was telling the goose story. That was just when I imagined you deeply despised me for being so eager to make myself out a fine fellow, and I suddenly even hated you for it and began spouting rubbish. Then I imagined—this was just now, out here—when I said, ‘If God didn't exist we'd have to invent him,’ that I was too eager to show off my education, especially since I'd read that phrase in a book. But I swear I wasn't eager to show off out of vanity; it was just—I don't know why—out of joy, by God, I think it was out of joy... Though it's a deeply shameful trait, throwing your arms round everyone's neck out of joy. I know that. But now I'm convinced you don't despise me, that I invented it all myself. Oh, Karamazov, I'm deeply unhappy. Sometimes I imagine God knows what, that everyone's laughing at me, the whole world, and then I'm simply ready to destroy the whole order of things.”

<!-- B10-C06-P051 -->
“And you torment the people around you,” Alyosha smiled.

<!-- B10-C06-P052 -->
“And I torment the people around me, especially my mother. Karamazov, tell me, am I being very ridiculous now?”

<!-- B10-C06-P053 -->
“Don't think about that, don't think about it at all!” Alyosha exclaimed. “And what does ridiculous mean, anyway? How often is a person ridiculous, or seems ridiculous? Besides, nowadays nearly everyone with any ability is terribly afraid of looking ridiculous, and it makes them unhappy. I'm only surprised you began feeling it so early, though I've been noticing it for a long time now, and not only in you. Nowadays even people who are almost children have begun suffering from it. It's almost madness. The devil has taken flesh in this pride and crept into the whole generation—the devil himself,” Alyosha added, without the trace of a smile, though Kolya, who was staring at him, had expected one. “You're like everyone else,” Alyosha concluded, “that is, like a great many people. Only you mustn't be like everyone else, that's the thing.”

<!-- B10-C06-P054 -->
“Even though everyone is like that?”

<!-- B10-C06-P055 -->
“Yes, even though everyone is like that. You alone can be different. And you really aren't like everyone else: just now you weren't ashamed to admit what was bad in you, even what was ridiculous. Who admits to that nowadays? Nobody. They've even stopped feeling the need to judge themselves. So don't be like everyone else. Even if you're the only one left who's different, be different all the same.”

<!-- B10-C06-P056 -->
“Magnificent! I wasn't wrong about you. You know how to comfort people. Oh, how I've longed for you, Karamazov, how long I've been trying to meet you! Were you really thinking about me too? You said earlier that you were thinking about me too?”

<!-- B10-C06-P057 -->
“Yes, I'd heard about you, and I was thinking about you too... And if it was partly pride that made you ask that just now, it doesn't matter.”

<!-- B10-C06-P058 -->
“You know, Karamazov, this talk of ours is rather like a declaration of love,” Kolya said in a curiously softened, bashful voice. “Isn't it ridiculous, isn't it?”

<!-- B10-C06-P059 -->
“Not ridiculous at all. And even if it were, that wouldn't matter, because it's good,” Alyosha said with a radiant smile.

<!-- B10-C06-P060 -->
“You know, Karamazov, admit it, you're a little embarrassed with me yourself now... I can see it in your eyes,” Kolya smiled, somewhat slyly, but also with something very like happiness.

<!-- B10-C06-P061 -->
“What is there to be embarrassed about?”

<!-- B10-C06-P062 -->
“Then why have you blushed?”

<!-- B10-C06-P063 -->
“Because you made me blush!” Alyosha laughed, and indeed blushed all over. “Well yes, I'm a little embarrassed, God knows why, I don't know why...” he murmured, almost flustered.

<!-- B10-C06-P064 -->
“Oh, how I love and value you at this moment, precisely because you're embarrassed with me too, for some reason! Because you're just like me!” Kolya exclaimed in outright rapture. His cheeks were burning and his eyes shone.

<!-- B10-C06-P065 -->
“Listen, Kolya, you will also be a very unhappy man in life,” Alyosha suddenly said for some reason.

<!-- B10-C06-P066 -->
“I know, I know. How do you know it all beforehand!” Kolya immediately agreed.

<!-- B10-C06-P067 -->
“But all in all, you'll bless life just the same.”

<!-- B10-C06-P068 -->
“Exactly! Hurrah! You're a prophet! Oh, we'll be friends, Karamazov. You know, what delights me most is that you treat me entirely as an equal. But we're not equals, no, not equals—you're above me! But we'll be friends. You know, for the whole past month I've been saying to myself: ‘Either we'll become friends forever the moment we meet, or we'll part from that first meeting as enemies to the grave!’”

<!-- B10-C06-P069 -->
“And while you were saying that, of course you already loved me!” Alyosha laughed merrily.

<!-- B10-C06-P070 -->
“I loved you, loved you terribly, loved you and dreamed about you! How do you know everything beforehand? Ah, here's the doctor. Lord, what will he say? Look at his face!”

[^b10-c06-n001]: ‘Women knit.’ Kolya invokes it as a claim about women's place; the attribution to Napoleon is his.

[^b10-c06-n002]: The Third Department was the imperial political police, housed beside St. Petersburg's Chain Bridge. The quoted lines recall flogging there.
