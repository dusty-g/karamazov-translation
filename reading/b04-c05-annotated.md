# Book IV: Strains
## V. Strain in the Drawing Room

<!-- B04-C05-P001 -->
But the conversation in the drawing room was already coming to an end. Katerina Ivanovna was very agitated, though she looked determined. As Alyosha and Madame Khokhlakov came in, Ivan Fyodorovich was getting up to leave. His face was rather pale, and Alyosha looked at him anxiously. For one of his doubts, one troubling mystery that had tormented him for some time, was now being resolved here. As long as a month ago, several people, on different occasions, had hinted to him that his brother Ivan loved Katerina Ivanovna and, above all, really intended to “steal” her from Mitya. Until very recently Alyosha had thought the idea monstrous, though it worried him greatly. He loved both brothers and dreaded such a rivalry between them. Yet only yesterday Dmitri Fyodorovich himself had suddenly told him outright that he was actually glad of his brother Ivan's rivalry, and that it would help him, Dmitri, in many ways. Help him do what? Marry Grushenka? But Alyosha regarded that as a desperate, final step. Besides all this, until yesterday evening Alyosha had firmly believed that Katerina Ivanovna herself loved his brother Dmitri passionately and obstinately—but he had believed it only until yesterday evening. What was more, for some reason he had always had the impression that she could not love a man like Ivan, that she loved his brother Dmitri precisely as he was, monstrous though such a love might be. But yesterday, during the scene with Grushenka, he had suddenly seemed to glimpse something different. The word “strain,” just spoken by Madame Khokhlakov, had almost made him start, because that very night, half waking at dawn, he had suddenly said, probably in answer to his dream, “Strain, strain!” All night he had dreamed of yesterday's scene at Katerina Ivanovna's. Now Madame Khokhlakov's sudden, plain, persistent assertion that Katerina Ivanovna loved his brother Ivan, and was only deceiving herself deliberately, playing some sort of game, tormenting herself with her affected love for Dmitri out of some supposed gratitude, struck Alyosha forcibly. “Yes, perhaps those words really are the whole truth!” But then what position did that leave his brother Ivan in? Alyosha felt instinctively that a nature like Katerina Ivanovna's needed to dominate, and that she could dominate only a man like Dmitri, never one like Ivan. For Dmitri alone might eventually submit to her—though it might take a long time—“for his own happiness” (as Alyosha himself would even have wished); but Ivan would not, Ivan could never submit to her, nor would submission make him happy. For some reason this was the idea Alyosha had involuntarily formed of Ivan. And all these doubts and considerations flashed through his mind in the moment when he entered the drawing room. Another thought flashed through it too, suddenly and irresistibly: “What if she loves neither of them, neither one nor the other?” I should mention that Alyosha was almost ashamed of such thoughts and reproached himself for them whenever they had occurred to him during the past month. “What do I understand about love or women, and how can I pronounce judgments like these?” he would think reproachfully after every such thought or conjecture. And yet he could not help thinking about it. He understood instinctively that now, for instance, this rivalry was an immensely important question in his two brothers' lives, one on which far too much depended. “One reptile will eat the other,” his brother Ivan had said yesterday, speaking irritably of their father and Dmitri. So his brother Dmitri was a reptile in Ivan's eyes, and perhaps had been one for a long time? Ever since Ivan had met Katerina Ivanovna, perhaps? Of course those words had escaped Ivan involuntarily yesterday, but their very involuntariness made them all the more significant. If that was so, how could there be peace here? Wasn't this, on the contrary, another reason for hatred and hostility in their family? Above all, whom should he, Alyosha, pity? And what should he wish for each of them? He loved them both, but what could he wish for either amid such terrible contradictions? A person could lose his way entirely in this confusion, and Alyosha's heart could not bear uncertainty, because his love was always active by nature. He could not love passively: the moment he loved someone, he began helping them. But to do that he needed to set himself a goal, to know firmly what was good and necessary for each of them; once convinced that his goal was right, he could naturally help them both. Yet instead of a firm goal there was only obscurity and confusion everywhere. And now the word “strain” had been spoken! But what could he understand even of this strain? He did not understand the first word of this whole muddle!

<!-- B04-C05-P002 -->
Seeing Alyosha, Katerina Ivanovna spoke quickly and gladly to Ivan Fyodorovich, who had already risen to leave:

<!-- B04-C05-P003 -->
“One moment! Stay just one more moment. I want to hear the opinion of this man, whom I trust with my whole being. Katerina Osipovna, don't you go either,” she added, turning to Madame Khokhlakov. She seated Alyosha beside her, while Madame Khokhlakov sat opposite, next to Ivan Fyodorovich.

<!-- B04-C05-P004 -->
“All my friends are here, everyone I have in the world, my dear friends,” she began ardently, in a voice trembling with genuine tears of suffering, and Alyosha's heart swung back toward her at once. “You, Alexei Fyodorovich—you witnessed that... horror yesterday, and you saw how I was. You didn't see it, Ivan Fyodorovich; he did. What he thought of me yesterday I don't know. I know only one thing: if it all happened again today, this very moment, I would express the same feelings as yesterday—the same feelings, the same words, the same gestures. You remember my gestures, Alexei Fyodorovich; you yourself stopped me in the middle of one of them...” (As she said this, she blushed and her eyes flashed.) “I tell you, Alexei Fyodorovich, I cannot accept any of it. Listen, Alexei Fyodorovich, I don't even know whether I love him now. I've begun to pity him; that is poor evidence of love. If I loved him, if I still loved him, perhaps I wouldn't pity him now but would hate him instead...”

<!-- B04-C05-P005 -->
Her voice shook, and little tears glittered on her eyelashes. Alyosha shuddered inwardly. “This girl is truthful and sincere,” he thought, “and... and she no longer loves Dmitri!”

<!-- B04-C05-P006 -->
“That's true! True!” Madame Khokhlakov began to exclaim.

<!-- B04-C05-P007 -->
“Wait, dear Katerina Osipovna. I haven't said the main thing, I haven't told you my final decision, the decision I made last night. I feel that perhaps it is a terrible decision—for me—but I have a presentiment that nothing, nothing will ever make me change it, for as long as I live. That is how it will be. My dear, kind, constant and generous adviser, who understands the heart so deeply, my only friend in the world, Ivan Fyodorovich, approves of me in everything and praises my decision... He knows what it is.”

<!-- B04-C05-P008 -->
“Yes, I approve of it,” Ivan Fyodorovich said in a quiet but firm voice.

<!-- B04-C05-P009 -->
“But I want Alyosha too—oh, Alexei Fyodorovich, forgive me for calling you simply Alyosha—I want Alexei Fyodorovich to tell me now, before both my friends, whether I am right or not. I have an instinctive feeling that you, Alyosha, my dear brother—for you are my dear brother,” she said again with fervor, taking his cold hand in her hot one, “I feel that your judgment, your approval, will bring me peace despite all my suffering, because once you have spoken I will grow quiet and accept it—I feel it!”

<!-- B04-C05-P010 -->
“I don't know what you're going to ask me,” Alyosha said, his face flushed. “I only know that I love you, and at this moment I wish for your happiness more than for my own!.. But I know nothing about these things...” he suddenly hurried to add, for some reason.

<!-- B04-C05-P011 -->
“In these things, Alexei Fyodorovich, the main considerations now are honor and duty, and I don't know what else, but something higher, perhaps higher even than duty itself. My heart tells me of this irresistible feeling, and it draws me irresistibly. But it can all be said in two words; I have already decided. Even if he marries that... creature,” she began solemnly, “whom I can never, never forgive, I will still not abandon him! From this time forward I will never, never abandon him!” she said with a kind of self-torment, a pale, strained exaltation. “I don't mean I'll follow him about, keep appearing before him, torment him—oh no, I'll go to another town, anywhere you like, but all my life, my whole life, I will keep track of him without tiring. And when he is unhappy with that woman, which is bound to happen, and happen at once, let him come to me, and he will find a friend, a sister... Only a sister, of course, and that will be forever, but he will finally be convinced that this sister truly is his sister, who loves him and has sacrificed her whole life to him. I'll achieve it, I'll insist upon it, until at last he knows me and tells me everything without shame!” she exclaimed, almost beside herself. “I will be his god, to whom he will pray—and he owes me at least that for his betrayal and for what I went through because of him yesterday. Let him see all his life that I will be faithful to him all my life, faithful to the word I once gave him, even though he was unfaithful and betrayed me. I will... I will become merely a means to his happiness—or how should I put it?—an instrument, a machine for his happiness, for all my life, all my life, and let him see that for the rest of his! That is my whole decision! Ivan Fyodorovich approves of me most highly.”

<!-- B04-C05-P012 -->
She was breathless. Perhaps she had meant to express her thought with far more dignity, skill and naturalness, but it had come out too hurriedly and too nakedly. There was much youthful lack of restraint in it; much sounded like nothing more than yesterday's irritation, the need to parade her pride, and she sensed this herself. Her face suddenly darkened, and an ugly look came into her eyes. Alyosha noticed it all at once, and compassion stirred in his heart. Then his brother Ivan added his contribution.

<!-- B04-C05-P013 -->
“I only expressed my own thought,” he said. “With any other woman it would all be broken and forced, but with you it isn't. Another woman would be wrong, but you are right. I don't know how to justify that, but I see that you are supremely sincere, and therefore you are right...”

<!-- B04-C05-P014 -->
“But that's only at this moment... And what is this moment? Nothing but yesterday's insult—that's what this moment means!” Madame Khokhlakov suddenly burst out. She had evidently not intended to interfere, but had been unable to help herself, and had suddenly said something very true.

<!-- B04-C05-P015 -->
“Yes, yes,” Ivan interrupted with sudden vehemence, visibly angered at being interrupted himself. “Yes, but for another woman this moment would be only yesterday's impression, and only a moment, whereas with Katerina Ivanovna's character this moment will last her whole life. What for others is merely a promise is for her a lifelong duty, heavy, perhaps bleak, but unremitting. And she will nourish herself on the sense of that duty fulfilled! Your life, Katerina Ivanovna, will now be spent in the suffering contemplation of your own feelings, your own feat of devotion and your own grief; but in time that suffering will soften, and your life will become the sweet contemplation of a firm and proud intention carried out once and for all—proud in its own way, desperate in any case, but overcome by you. And that awareness will finally give you the fullest satisfaction and reconcile you to everything else...”

<!-- B04-C05-P016 -->
He said this with unmistakable malice, evidently on purpose, perhaps not even wishing to conceal his intention—that he was speaking deliberately and in mockery.

<!-- B04-C05-P017 -->
“Oh God, how wrong all this is!” Madame Khokhlakov exclaimed again.

<!-- B04-C05-P018 -->
“Alexei Fyodorovich, you tell me! I am desperate to know what you have to say!” Katerina Ivanovna cried, and suddenly burst into tears. Alyosha rose from the sofa.

<!-- B04-C05-P019 -->
“It's nothing, nothing!” she continued, weeping. “It's from being upset, from last night, but with two such friends beside me as you and your brother, I still feel strong... because I know... neither of you will ever abandon me...”

<!-- B04-C05-P020 -->
“Unfortunately, I may have to leave for Moscow tomorrow and be away from you for a long time... And unfortunately that cannot be changed...” Ivan Fyodorovich suddenly said.

<!-- B04-C05-P021 -->
“Tomorrow, to Moscow!” Katerina Ivanovna's whole face suddenly twisted. “But... but my God, how fortunate!” she cried in an entirely different voice, banishing her tears in an instant without leaving a trace. It was in that single instant that an astonishing change came over her, to Alyosha's immense surprise: in place of the poor, wounded girl who had just been weeping in a kind of emotional self-torment, there suddenly appeared a woman entirely in command of herself, and even extremely pleased about something, as though some sudden happiness had come to her.

<!-- B04-C05-P022 -->
“Oh, it isn't fortunate that I'm parting from you, of course not,” she suddenly seemed to correct herself, with a charming, worldly smile. “A friend like you couldn't think that. On the contrary, I'm terribly unhappy to be losing you.” (She suddenly rushed up to Ivan Fyodorovich, took both his hands and pressed them warmly.) “But what is fortunate is that now, in Moscow, you will be able to tell Auntie and Agasha yourself, in person, all about my situation, all the horror I'm in now, being entirely frank with Agasha and sparing dear Auntie, as you'll know how to do. You can't imagine how miserable I was yesterday and this morning, wondering how I was to write them that terrible letter... because it simply couldn't, couldn't be conveyed in a letter... Now it will be easy for me to write, because you'll be there with them and will explain everything. Oh, I'm so glad! But that is the only thing I'm glad about—again, believe me. You yourself, of course, are irreplaceable to me... I'll run and write the letter at once,” she suddenly concluded, and even took a step toward leaving the room.

<!-- B04-C05-P023 -->
“And Alyosha? What about Alexei Fyodorovich's opinion, which you were so determined to hear?” Madame Khokhlakov exclaimed. There was a sharp, angry note in her words.

<!-- B04-C05-P024 -->
“I haven't forgotten that,” Katerina Ivanovna said, suddenly stopping. “And why are you so hostile to me at a moment like this, Katerina Osipovna?” she asked in bitter, heated reproach. “I stand by what I said. I need his opinion; more than that, I need his decision! Whatever he says, that is what will happen—that, on the contrary, is how eagerly I long to hear you, Alexei Fyodorovich... But what's the matter with you?”

<!-- B04-C05-P025 -->
“I never thought—I can't imagine it!” Alyosha suddenly cried in distress.

<!-- B04-C05-P026 -->
“What? What?”

<!-- B04-C05-P027 -->
“He's going to Moscow, and you cried out that you were glad—you cried out on purpose! Then you immediately began explaining that you weren't glad about that, that on the contrary you were sorry to be... losing a friend—but that was an act too, on purpose... as though you were acting in a comedy on the stage!..”

<!-- B04-C05-P028 -->
“On the stage? How?.. What is this?” Katerina Ivanovna exclaimed in profound astonishment, flushing all over and drawing her brows together.

<!-- B04-C05-P029 -->
“No matter how much you assure him you're sorry to lose him as a friend, you still keep insisting to his face that his leaving is fortunate...” Alyosha said, now almost completely out of breath. He remained standing behind the table and did not sit down.

<!-- B04-C05-P030 -->
“What are you talking about? I don't understand...”

<!-- B04-C05-P031 -->
“I don't know myself... It's as if I've suddenly seen a light... I know this isn't a nice thing to say, but I'll say it all anyway,” Alyosha continued in the same shaking, faltering voice. “What I've suddenly seen is that perhaps you don't love my brother Dmitri at all... and never have... And perhaps Dmitri doesn't love you at all either... and never has... but only respects you... I really don't know how I dare say all this now, but someone has to tell the truth... because nobody here wants to tell the truth...”

<!-- B04-C05-P032 -->
“What truth?” Katerina Ivanovna cried, and something hysterical rang in her voice.

<!-- B04-C05-P033 -->
“This truth,” Alyosha stammered, as though he had flung himself off a roof. “Send for Dmitri now—I'll find him—and let him come here and take your hand, and then take my brother Ivan's hand, and join your hands together. Because you torment Ivan only because you love him... and you torment him because your love for Dmitri is self-torment... it isn't real love... you've persuaded yourself that it is...”

<!-- B04-C05-P034 -->
Alyosha broke off and fell silent.

<!-- B04-C05-P035 -->
“You... you... you're a little holy fool, that's what you are!” Katerina Ivanovna suddenly snapped, her face now pale and her lips twisted with spite. Ivan Fyodorovich suddenly laughed and rose. He held his hat in his hands.

<!-- B04-C05-P036 -->
“You're mistaken, my dear Alyosha,” he said with an expression Alyosha had never seen on his face before—an expression of youthful sincerity and strong, irrepressibly open feeling. “Katerina Ivanovna has never loved me! All along she knew I loved her, though I never said a word to her about my love—she knew, but she didn't love me. Nor was I ever her friend, not for a single day: a proud woman had no need of my friendship. She kept me beside her for continual revenge. She took her revenge on me, and used me to take revenge for all the insults she has constantly, at every moment, endured from Dmitri throughout this time, insults from their very first meeting... Because even their first meeting has remained an insult in her heart. That is the kind of heart she has! All this time I have done nothing but listen to her love for him. I'm leaving now, but know this, Katerina Ivanovna: in truth it is only him you love. And the more he insults you, the more you love him. That is your self-torment. You love him precisely as he is, you love him insulting you. If he reformed, you would drop him at once and stop loving him entirely. But you need him so that you can endlessly contemplate your own feat of faithfulness and reproach him for being unfaithful. And all of it comes from your pride. Oh, there is much abasement and humiliation in it, but all of it comes from pride... I'm too young, and I loved you too much. I know I shouldn't be saying this to you; it would show more dignity on my part simply to walk out, and it would be less insulting to you. But I'm going far away, and I will never come back. This is forever... I don't want to sit beside self-torment... But I no longer know how to speak; I've said everything... Goodbye, Katerina Ivanovna. You cannot be angry with me, because I am punished a hundred times more than you are: punished by the mere fact that I will never see you again. Goodbye. I don't want your hand. You have tormented me too consciously for me to forgive you at this moment. Later I will forgive you, but now don't offer me your hand.

*Den Dank, Dame, begehr ich nicht,*”[^b04-c05-n001]

he added with a twisted smile, demonstrating, quite unexpectedly, that he too could read Schiller until he knew him by heart—something Alyosha would not have believed before. He left the room without even saying goodbye to their hostess, Madame Khokhlakov. Alyosha threw up his hands.

<!-- B04-C05-P037 -->
“Ivan!” he called after him, bewildered. “Come back, Ivan! No, no, now he won't come back for anything!” he cried again, in a flash of sorrowful understanding. “But it was me, it was my fault, I started it! Ivan spoke spitefully, badly. Unfairly and spitefully...” Alyosha exclaimed like a man out of his mind.

<!-- B04-C05-P038 -->
Katerina Ivanovna suddenly went into the next room.

<!-- B04-C05-P039 -->
“You haven't done any harm, you acted beautifully, like an angel,” Madame Khokhlakov whispered quickly and enthusiastically to the grieving Alyosha. “I'll do everything I can to keep Ivan Fyodorovich from leaving...”

<!-- B04-C05-P040 -->
Her face shone with joy, to Alyosha's great distress; but Katerina Ivanovna suddenly returned. She held two rainbow-colored banknotes.

<!-- B04-C05-P041 -->
“I have a great favor to ask you, Alexei Fyodorovich,” she began, addressing Alyosha directly in an apparently calm, even voice, as though nothing at all had just happened. “A week ago—yes, I think it was a week ago—Dmitri Fyodorovich did something rash and unjust, something very ugly. There is a disreputable place here, a tavern. There he met that retired officer, that staff captain your father used for some business of his. For some reason Dmitri Fyodorovich grew angry with the staff captain, seized him by the beard, and led him out into the street like that, in front of everyone, humiliating him, and led him along the street for a long time. They say a boy, this staff captain's son, who goes to the local school—still a child—saw it and ran beside them the whole time, crying aloud and pleading for his father, rushing up to everyone and begging them to defend him, and everyone laughed. Forgive me, Alexei Fyodorovich, but I cannot remember this shameful act of his without indignation... one of those acts that only Dmitri Fyodorovich could bring himself to commit in his anger... and his passions! I can't even tell you about it, I can't. I get my words confused. I made inquiries about the man he insulted and learned that he is very poor. His name is Snegiryov. He committed some offense in the service and was dismissed—I don't know how to explain it—and now he and his family, an unfortunate family of sick children and a wife who I think is insane, have fallen into dreadful poverty. He has been here in town a long time. He does something, he worked somewhere as a copying clerk, but now suddenly they aren't paying him anything. I cast my eyes on you... that is, I thought—I don't know, I'm getting confused—you see, I wanted to ask you, Alexei Fyodorovich, my kindest Alexei Fyodorovich, to go to him, find some pretext, call on them, I mean on this staff captain—oh God, how muddled I am—and tactfully, carefully, in the way only you can manage”—Alyosha suddenly blushed—“contrive to give him this assistance, here, two hundred rubles. Surely he will accept... I mean persuade him to accept... Or no, how should it be? You see, this isn't a payment to settle things, to keep him from making a complaint—because I think he intended to complain—but simply sympathy, a wish to help, from me, from me, from Dmitri Fyodorovich's fiancée, not from him himself... In short, you'll know how... I'd go myself, but you'll manage much better than I would. He lives on Lake Street, in the house of a woman named Kalmykova, of the townspeople's estate... For God's sake, Alexei Fyodorovich, do this for me, and now... now I'm a little... tired. Goodbye...”

<!-- B04-C05-P042 -->
She turned and disappeared behind the curtain again so quickly that Alyosha had no time to say a word, though he wanted to. He wanted to ask forgiveness, to blame himself—well, to say something, because his heart was full, and he absolutely did not want to leave the room without doing so. But Madame Khokhlakov took him by the arm and led him out herself. In the entrance hall she stopped him again, as she had earlier.

<!-- B04-C05-P043 -->
“Proud, fighting herself, but kind, lovely, generous!” Madame Khokhlakov exclaimed in a half whisper. “Oh, how I love her, especially sometimes, and how glad I am about everything, everything, all over again! Dear Alexei Fyodorovich, you didn't know this: let me tell you that all of us, all of us—me, both her aunts—well, everyone, even Lise—for a whole month now all we've wished and prayed for is that she should break with your darling Dmitri Fyodorovich, who wants nothing to do with her and doesn't love her in the least, and marry Ivan Fyodorovich, an educated and excellent young man who loves her more than anything in the world. We've formed a whole conspiracy here, and perhaps that's the only reason I haven't gone away myself...”

<!-- B04-C05-P044 -->
“But she was crying, she'd been insulted again!” Alyosha exclaimed.

<!-- B04-C05-P045 -->
“Don't believe a woman's tears, Alexei Fyodorovich. I'm always against women in these matters, I'm on the men's side.”

<!-- B04-C05-P046 -->
“Mama, you're corrupting him and ruining him,” came Lise's thin voice from behind the door.

<!-- B04-C05-P047 -->
“No, I'm the cause of it all, I've done something terribly wrong!” the inconsolable Alyosha kept repeating, in a burst of painful shame at his outburst, even covering his face with his hands from shame.

<!-- B04-C05-P048 -->
“On the contrary, you behaved like an angel, like an angel, and I'm prepared to repeat it thousands upon thousands of times.”

<!-- B04-C05-P049 -->
“Mama, why did he behave like an angel?” came Lise's voice again.

<!-- B04-C05-P050 -->
“For some reason, watching all this, I suddenly imagined,” Alyosha continued, as though he had not heard Lise, “that she loved Ivan, and so I said that stupid thing... and now what will happen!”

<!-- B04-C05-P051 -->
“To whom, to whom?” Lise exclaimed. “Mama, you must be trying to kill me. I ask you questions and you don't answer.”

<!-- B04-C05-P052 -->
At that moment a maid came running in.

<!-- B04-C05-P053 -->
“Katerina Ivanovna is unwell... She's crying... hysterics, she's convulsing.”

<!-- B04-C05-P054 -->
“What's this?” Lise cried, her voice anxious now. “Mama, I'm the one who will have hysterics, not her!”

<!-- B04-C05-P055 -->
“Lise, for God's sake, don't shout, don't kill me. You're still at an age when you can't know everything grown-ups know. I'll run back and tell you everything you can be told. Oh my God! I'm coming, I'm coming... Hysterics are a good sign, Alexei Fyodorovich; it's excellent that she's having hysterics. That's exactly what ought to happen. I'm always against women in these matters, against all these hysterics and women's tears. Yulia, run and say I'm flying to her. And as for Ivan Fyodorovich walking out like that, she has only herself to blame. But he won't leave. Lise, for God's sake, don't shout! Oh yes, you're not shouting, I'm the one shouting; forgive your mama, but I'm delighted, delighted, delighted! And did you notice, Alexei Fyodorovich, what a young man Ivan Fyodorovich was just now when he left, when he said all that and left! I thought he was such a scholar, an academician, and suddenly he was so very ardent, open and young, inexperienced and young, and it was all so wonderful, wonderful, just like you... And he recited that little German verse, just like you! But I'm going, I'm going. Alexei Fyodorovich, hurry along on this errand and come back as soon as you can. Lise, do you need anything? For God's sake, don't keep Alexei Fyodorovich even a minute; he'll be right back to you...”

<!-- B04-C05-P056 -->
Madame Khokhlakov finally ran off. Before leaving, Alyosha was about to open Lise's door.

<!-- B04-C05-P057 -->
“Absolutely not!” Lise cried. “Absolutely not, now! Talk through the door. What did you do to become an angel? That's the only thing I want to know.”

<!-- B04-C05-P058 -->
“Something terribly stupid, Lise! Goodbye.”

<!-- B04-C05-P059 -->
“Don't you dare leave like that!” Lise began to cry.

<!-- B04-C05-P060 -->
“Lise, I'm in real distress! I'll be right back, but I'm in great, great distress!” And he ran out of the room.
