# Book VI: The Russian Monk

## III. From the Elder Zosima’s Conversations and Teachings

### e) A Few Words on the Russian Monk and His Possible Significance

<!-- B06-C03-P001 -->
Fathers and teachers, what is a monk? In the enlightened world these days, some already speak the word with mockery, others even as an insult. And increasingly so. It is true, oh, it is true: among monks, too, there are many parasites, gluttons, sensualists, and brazen vagabonds. Educated people in the world point to them: “You are lazy, useless members of society, living off other people’s labor, shameless beggars.” And yet how many humble and gentle men there are among monks, thirsting for solitude and fervent prayer in silence. These are less often pointed out, even passed over in silence altogether. And how astonished people would be if I said that from these gentle men, thirsting for solitary prayer, the salvation of the Russian land might yet come once again! For truly, in silence, they have been made ready “for the day and hour, and month and year.” For now, in their solitude, they preserve the image of Christ in its beauty, undistorted, in the purity of God’s truth, handed down from the earliest fathers, apostles, and martyrs; and when it is needed, they will reveal it to a world whose truth has faltered. This is a great thought. This star will shine from the East.

<!-- B06-C03-P002 -->
Such are my thoughts about the monk: can they be false, can they be arrogant? Look at those in the world, at the whole world that exalts itself above God’s people: have God’s countenance and his truth not been distorted there? They have science, and science deals only with what the senses can perceive. But the spiritual world, the higher half of human existence, has been rejected altogether, cast out with a kind of triumph, even with hatred. The world has proclaimed freedom, especially of late, and what do we see in this freedom of theirs? Nothing but slavery and suicide! For the world says: “You have needs, so satisfy them, for you have the same rights as the noblest and richest people. Do not be afraid to satisfy them; indeed, multiply them.” Such is the world’s teaching today. That is what they see as freedom. And what comes of this right to multiply one’s needs? For the rich, isolation and spiritual suicide; for the poor, envy and murder, for the rights have been granted, but the means of satisfying their needs have yet to be shown them.

We are assured that the world is growing ever more united, coming together in brotherly fellowship by shortening distances and transmitting thoughts through the air. Alas, do not believe in such a union of people. By understanding freedom as the multiplication and swift satisfaction of their needs, they distort their nature, for they engender in themselves many senseless, foolish desires, habits, and utterly absurd inventions. They live only to envy one another, to indulge the flesh, and to swagger. Dinner parties, fashionable outings, carriages, official rank, and slaves to wait on them are already considered such necessities that people sacrifice life itself, honor, and love of humanity to satisfy this need, and even kill themselves if they cannot satisfy it. We see the same among those who are not rich; among the poor, unsatisfied needs and envy are still being drowned in drink. But soon they will grow drunk on blood instead of wine: that is where they are being led.

I ask you: is such a person free? I knew one “fighter for a cause” who told me himself that when he was deprived of tobacco in prison, the deprivation tormented him so much that he nearly went and betrayed his “cause,” if only they would give him tobacco. And yet such a man says: “I am going to fight for humanity.” Well, where will he get, and what is he capable of? A sudden act, perhaps, but he will not hold out for long. Nor is it any wonder that instead of freedom they have fallen into slavery, and instead of serving brotherly love and human unity, they have fallen, on the contrary, into separation and isolation, as my mysterious visitor and teacher told me in my youth. And so the idea of serving humanity, of brotherhood and the wholeness of humankind, is fading more and more in the world; indeed, the very idea is already met with mockery. For how can one give up one’s habits? Where can this slave go, so accustomed as he is to satisfying the countless needs he has invented for himself? He is isolated, and what does he care about the whole? And the result is that they have accumulated more things, but have less joy.

<!-- B06-C03-P003 -->
The monastic path is another matter. They even laugh at obedience, fasting, and prayer, yet these alone contain the way to real, true freedom: I cut away my superfluous, unnecessary needs, I humble and scourge my selfish, proud will through obedience, and so, with God’s help, attain freedom of spirit, and with it spiritual gladness! Which of the two is better able to lift up a great idea and go forth to serve it—the isolated rich man, or this man freed from the tyranny of things and habits? They reproach the monk for his solitude: “You have shut yourself away to save yourself within the monastery walls, and forgotten your brotherly service to humanity.” But we shall yet see who will labor more zealously for brotherly love. For the isolation is theirs, not ours, though they do not see it. From ancient times, men who labored for the people have come from among us; why should there not be such men now? These same humble and gentle men who fast and keep silence will rise and go forth to a great work. Russia’s salvation will come from the people. And the Russian monastery has been with the people from the beginning. If the people are isolated, then we are isolated too. The people believe as we do, and in our Russia a man who works without faith will accomplish nothing, however sincere his heart, however brilliant his mind. Remember that. The people will confront the atheist and overcome him, and there will be one Orthodox Russia. Cherish the people, then, and guard their hearts. Nurture them in silence. This is your monastic labor, for this people bears God within it.

### f) A Few Words on Masters and Servants, and Whether Masters and Servants Can Become Brothers in Spirit

<!-- B06-C03-P004 -->
God, who would deny it: there is sin among the people too. And the flame of corruption is growing visibly, by the hour; it comes from above. Isolation is spreading among the people too: the grasping peasants and village bloodsuckers are appearing; the merchant is ever more eager for honors, strives to look educated without having any education at all, and to this end vilely scorns the old customs and is even ashamed of his fathers’ faith. He pays visits to princes, though he himself is no more than a spoiled peasant. The people are festering with drunkenness and can no longer give it up. And how much cruelty there is toward the family, toward wives, even toward children—all from drunkenness. In factories I have seen children even ten years old: frail, sickly, bent over, and already corrupted. A stifling room, a clattering machine, work all the livelong day, filthy talk, and drink, drink—is that what the soul of so young a child needs? He needs sunshine, children’s games, a good example everywhere, and at least a drop of love for him. Let this be no more, monks; let there be no more tormenting of children. Rise and preach this, quickly, quickly. But God will save Russia, for although the common man is corrupted and can no longer deny himself his foul sin, he still knows that his foul sin is cursed by God and that in sinning he does wrong. Thus our people still believe steadfastly in truth, acknowledge God, and weep with tender feeling.

It is different among the higher ranks. Following science, they want to establish justice by their own reason alone, but now without Christ, as in former times, and they have already proclaimed that there is no crime, there is no longer any sin. And by their reasoning they are right: for if you have no God, what crime can there be? In Europe the people are already rising against the rich by force, and everywhere the people’s leaders are leading them toward bloodshed and teaching them that their anger is just. But “cursed be their anger, for it is fierce.” Yet the Lord will save Russia, as he has saved her many times before. Salvation will come from the people, from their faith and humility.

Fathers and teachers, guard the people’s faith, and this is no dream: all my life I have been struck by the beautiful and true dignity of our great people. I have seen it myself, I can testify to it myself; I have seen it and marveled, seen it despite even the stench of sin and the beggarly appearance of our people. They are not servile, and this after two centuries of slavery. They are free in their bearing and manner, yet give no offense. Nor are they vengeful or envious. “You are noble, you are rich, you are intelligent and gifted—so be it, God bless you. I honor you, but I know that I too am a human being. By honoring you without envy, I show you my own human dignity.” Truly, if they do not say this—for they do not yet know how to say it—this is how they act. I have seen it myself, experienced it myself, and would you believe it: the poorer and humbler our Russian man, the more evident in him is this beautiful truth, for many of the rich among them, the grasping peasants and village bloodsuckers, are already corrupted; and much, so much of this has come from our negligence and neglect! But God will save his people, for Russia is great in her humility.

I dream of seeing our future, and seem already to see it clearly: for it will come to pass that even our most corrupted rich man will at last be ashamed of his wealth before the poor man, and the poor man, seeing this humility, will understand and gladly yield to him, answering his noble shame with affection. Believe that this is how it will end: this is where it is heading. Equality lies only in human spiritual dignity, and only among us will this be understood. Let there be brothers, and there will be brotherhood; until there is brotherhood, they will never divide things among themselves. We preserve the image of Christ, and it will shine upon the whole world like a precious diamond… So be it, so be it!

<!-- B06-C03-P005 -->
Fathers and teachers, something touching once happened to me. In my wanderings, in the provincial capital of K., I once met my former orderly, Afanasy; by then eight years had passed since I parted from him. He happened to see me in the marketplace, recognized me, ran up to me, and, God, how glad he was! He threw himself toward me: “Father, master, is it you? Can I really be seeing you?” He took me home with him. He had already left the service, married, and fathered two little children. He and his wife made their living selling small wares from a tray in the market. His room was poor but clean and cheerful. He sat me down, put on the samovar, sent for his wife, as though my appearance had given him a holiday. He brought the children to me: “Bless them, Father.” “Who am I to give a blessing?” I answered. “I am a simple, humble monk. I will pray to God for them, and for you, Afanasy Pavlovich, I have always prayed, every day since that very day, for it all began with you,” I told him. And I explained it to him as best I could. But what did the man do? He looked at me and still could not take it in that I, his former master, an officer, now stood before him looking like this, in these clothes; he even began to cry. “Why are you crying?” I said to him. “You unforgettable man, be glad for me in your heart instead, my dear fellow, for my path is joyful and bright.” He said little, only sighed and shook his head at me tenderly. “But where is your fortune?” he asked. I answered: “I gave it to the monastery, and we live in common.” After tea I began to say goodbye, and suddenly he brought me half a ruble, an offering for the monastery; and then I saw him hurriedly pressing another half ruble into my hand. “This is for you,” he said, “a wanderer on your travels. You may have a use for it, Father.” I accepted his half ruble, bowed to him and his wife, and left full of joy, thinking as I went: “Here we both are now, he at home and I on the road, probably sighing and smiling joyfully, in the gladness of our hearts, nodding our heads as we remember how God brought us together.” And I have never seen him again since. I had been his master and he my servant, but now, as we kissed each other lovingly, our spirits moved to tenderness, a great human union took place between us. I have thought about this often, and now I think: is it really so impossible to conceive that this great and simplehearted union might, in its time, come about everywhere among our Russian people? I believe it will, and the time is near.

<!-- B06-C03-P006 -->
And about servants I will add this: formerly, as a young man, I was often angry with servants: “The cook served the food too hot, the orderly hasn’t cleaned my clothes.” But then my dear brother’s thought, which I had heard from him in childhood, suddenly shone upon me: “Am I, taken all in all, worth another person’s serving me, while I order him about because he is poor and ignorant?” And even then I marveled at how late the simplest thoughts, plain to the eye, come into our minds. In the world one cannot do without servants, but see to it that your servant is freer in spirit with you than he would be if he were not a servant. And why can I not be a servant to my servant, in such a way that he even sees it, without any pride on my part or disbelief on his? Why should my servant not be like a kinsman to me, so that at last I receive him into my family and rejoice in it? Even now this is quite possible, and it will lay the foundation for that glorious future union of people, when a man will no longer seek servants for himself or wish to turn his fellow human beings into his servants, as he does now, but will, on the contrary, wish with all his strength to become everyone’s servant himself, according to the Gospel. And is it really a dream that in the end a man should find his joys only in the labors of enlightenment and mercy, instead of the cruel joys he has now—in gluttony, fornication, swagger, boasting, and the envious struggle to outdo one another? I firmly believe it is not, and that the time is near.

They laugh and ask: when will this time come, and does it look as though it will ever come? But I think that with Christ we shall accomplish this great work. And how many ideas have there been on earth, in human history, that were unthinkable even ten years before, and suddenly appeared when their mysterious appointed time arrived, and swept across the whole earth? So it will be with us, and our people will shine upon the world, and everyone will say: “The stone the builders rejected has become the cornerstone.” But the mockers themselves ought to be asked: if ours is a dream, then when will you raise your own building and establish justice by your reason alone, without Christ? Though they themselves maintain that, on the contrary, they are advancing toward unity, truly only the most simplehearted among them believe it, and one can only marvel at such simpleheartedness. Truly, there is more dreamy fantasy among them than among us.

They mean to establish justice, but, having rejected Christ, they will end by flooding the world with blood, for blood calls for blood, and he who has drawn the sword will perish by the sword. And were it not for Christ’s promise, they would indeed destroy one another down to the last two people on earth. Even those last two, in their pride, would be unable to hold on to each other, so that the last would destroy the second to last, and then himself. And this would come to pass were it not for Christ’s promise that for the sake of the gentle and humble these events will be cut short. Back then, after my duel, while still in an officer’s uniform, I began speaking about servants in society, and I remember how astonished everyone was at me. “What are we to do, then,” they said, “sit the servant on the sofa and bring him tea?” And I answered them: “Why not, if only now and then?” Everyone laughed then. Their question was frivolous and my answer unclear, but I think there was a certain truth in it.

### g) On Prayer, on Love, and on Contact with Other Worlds

<!-- B06-C03-P007 -->
Young man, do not forget prayer. Each time you pray, if your prayer is sincere, a new feeling will flash through it, and in that feeling a new thought, one you did not know before, which will give you fresh courage; and you will understand that prayer is an education. Remember also: every day, whenever you can, repeat to yourself: “Lord, have mercy on all who have appeared before you today.” For every hour and every moment, thousands of people leave their life on this earth, and their souls stand before the Lord—and how many of them have parted from the earth alone, unknown to anyone, in grief and anguish because no one will mourn them or even knows of them at all: whether they ever lived or not. And now, perhaps from the other end of the earth, your prayer too will rise to the Lord for the repose of such a soul, though you did not know him at all, nor he you. How moving it will be for his soul, standing in fear before the Lord, to feel at that moment that someone is praying for him too, that a human being remains on earth who loves him too. And God will look more mercifully upon you both, for if even you have felt such pity for him, how much more will he, infinitely more merciful and loving than you, feel pity. And he will forgive him for your sake.

<!-- B06-C03-P008 -->
Brothers, do not be afraid of people’s sin; love a person even in his sin, for this is already a likeness of divine love and is the highest love on earth. Love all God’s creation, the whole of it and every grain of sand. Love every leaf, every ray of God’s light. Love the animals, love the plants, love every thing. If you love every thing, you will grasp God’s mystery in things. Once you have grasped it, you will begin tirelessly to understand it further and more deeply, every day. And at last you will love the whole world with an all-embracing, universal love. Love the animals: God has given them the beginnings of thought and untroubled joy. Do not disturb that joy, do not torment them, do not take their joy from them, do not oppose God’s intention. Man, do not exalt yourself above the animals: they are without sin, while you, with all your greatness, bring corruption to the earth by your appearance on it, and leave your festering trace behind you—alas, almost every one of us does! Love children especially, for they too are without sin, like angels, and live to move us to tenderness, to cleanse our hearts, and as a kind of sign to us. Woe to him who harms a child. Father Anfim taught me to love children: dear man, so silent on our travels, he would buy them gingerbread and candy with the small coins given us in charity, and hand it out. He could not pass children without being shaken to his soul: such was the man.

<!-- B06-C03-P009 -->
Some thoughts will leave you at a loss, especially when you see people’s sin, and you will ask yourself: “Shall I prevail by force, or by humble love?” Always decide: “I will prevail by humble love.” Once you resolve upon this forever, you will be able to conquer the whole world. Loving humility is a tremendous force, stronger than any other, with nothing else like it. Every day and hour, every minute, keep watch over yourself, observe yourself, so that your presence may be beautiful. You have just passed a little child, passed him in malice, with a foul word and an angry soul; perhaps you did not even notice the child, but he saw you, and your ugly, godless image may have remained in his defenseless little heart. You did not know it, but perhaps by that alone you have already cast a bad seed into him, and perhaps it will grow, all because you did not guard yourself before the child, because you had not cultivated watchful, active love within yourself. Brothers, love is a teacher, but one must know how to acquire it, for it is hard to acquire, dearly bought, through long labor and over a long time; for one must love not merely for a chance moment, but for the whole span of life. Anyone can love by chance; even a villain can love. My brother, as a young man, asked the birds for forgiveness. It seems senseless, yet it is true, for everything is like an ocean, everything flows and touches everything else: touch it in one place, and it echoes at the other end of the world. Let it be madness to ask the birds for forgiveness, yet things would be easier for the birds, and for the child, and for every animal around you, if you yourself were more gracious than you are now, even by a single drop. Everything is like an ocean, I tell you. Then you would begin praying even to the birds, tormented by an all-embracing love, as though in a kind of rapture, and begging them too to forgive you your sin. Cherish this rapture, however senseless it may seem to people.

<!-- B06-C03-P010 -->
My friends, ask God for gladness. Be glad as children, as the birds of heaven. And let people’s sin not trouble you in your work; do not fear that it will crush your work and keep it from being accomplished. Do not say: “Sin is strong, godlessness is strong, the corrupt world around us is strong, and we are alone and powerless; that corrupt world will crush us and keep our good work from being accomplished.” Flee this despair, children! There is only one way to save yourself here: take yourself in hand and make yourself answerable for all human sin. My friend, this really is so, for the moment you sincerely make yourself answerable for everything and everyone, you will see at once that it truly is so, and that you yourself are guilty for everyone and everything. But by blaming other people for your own laziness and powerlessness, you will end by sharing in Satan’s pride and murmuring against God. About Satan’s pride I think this: it is hard for us on earth even to comprehend it, and therefore how easy it is to fall into error and share in it, while even supposing we are doing something great and beautiful. There are many of the strongest feelings and impulses of our nature that we cannot comprehend while still on earth. Do not let this lead you astray either, or suppose it can in any way serve as your excuse, for the eternal Judge will hold you to account for what you could comprehend, not for what you could not. You will see this for yourself, for then you will see everything rightly and will no longer argue. On earth, truly, we seem to wander, and without the precious image of Christ before us we would perish and lose our way altogether, like the human race before the Flood. Much on earth is hidden from us, but in return we have been given a secret, inward sense of our living connection with another world, a higher, heavenly world; indeed, the roots of our thoughts and feelings are not here, but in other worlds. That is why philosophers say that the essence of things cannot be comprehended on earth. God took seeds from other worlds and sowed them on this earth and grew his garden, and everything that could grow came up, but what has grown lives, and is alive, only through the sense of its contact with mysterious other worlds. If this sense weakens or is destroyed within you, then what has grown within you dies too. Then you will become indifferent to life and even come to hate it. Such is my thought.

### h) Can One Judge One’s Fellow Human Beings? On Faith to the End

<!-- B06-C03-P012 -->
Remember above all that you cannot be anyone’s judge. For no one on earth can judge a criminal before that judge himself recognizes that he is just as much a criminal as the man standing before him, and that he himself may be more to blame than anyone for the crime of the man before him. Only when he has understood this will he be able to become a judge. However mad it may appear, this is true. For had I myself been righteous, perhaps the criminal standing before me would never have existed. If you can take upon yourself the crime of the criminal standing before you, whom your heart is judging, then take it upon yourself at once and suffer for him yourself, and let him go without reproach. Even if the law itself has appointed you his judge, do as much as you possibly can in this same spirit, for he will go away and judge himself more harshly still than you have judged him. But if he leaves with your kiss, unmoved and laughing at you, do not let this lead you astray either: it means his time has not yet come, but it will come in due course; and if it does not, it is all the same: if not he, then another will come to understand in his place, and suffer, and judge and accuse himself, and truth will be fulfilled. Believe this, believe it without doubt, for herein lies all the hope and all the faith of the saints.

<!-- B06-C03-P013 -->
Work tirelessly. If at night, as you are going to sleep, you remember: “I have not done what needed doing,” then rise at once and do it. If the people around you are malicious and unfeeling and will not listen to you, then fall before them and ask their forgiveness, for truly you too are to blame for their unwillingness to listen to you. And if you can no longer speak with those embittered people, serve them in silence and humility, never losing hope. If everyone abandons you and even drives you away by force, then, left alone, fall to the ground and kiss it, wet it with your tears, and the earth will bear fruit from your tears, though no one should see or hear you in your solitude. Believe to the end, even if it should come to pass that everyone on earth goes astray and you alone remain faithful: even then, offer your sacrifice and praise God, you who alone remain. And if two such as you come together, then there is already a whole world, a world of living love. Embrace each other with tender hearts and praise the Lord: for even if only in the two of you, his truth has been fulfilled.

<!-- B06-C03-P014 -->
If you yourself sin and grieve even to death over your sins or your sudden sin, rejoice for another, rejoice for the righteous man, rejoice that though you have sinned, he is righteous and has not sinned.

<!-- B06-C03-P015 -->
But if people’s wickedness stirs you to indignation and a grief you can no longer overcome, even to a desire to take revenge on the wicked, fear this feeling above all; go at once and seek suffering for yourself, as though you yourself were guilty of this human wickedness. Accept this suffering and endure it, and your heart will be soothed, and you will understand that you too are guilty, for you could have shone upon the wicked, even as the one sinless man, and did not shine. Had you shone, you would have lit the way for others too, and the man who committed the wicked act might not have committed it in your light. And even if you did shine, but see that people are not saved even in your light, remain firm and do not doubt the power of the light of heaven; believe that if they have not been saved now, they will be saved later. And if they are not saved later either, their sons will be saved, for your light will not die, though you yourself may have died. The righteous man departs, but his light remains. People are always being saved after the death of the one who saves them. The human race does not accept its prophets and kills them, but people love their martyrs and honor those whom they have tortured to death. You, however, are working for the whole, laboring for what is to come. Never seek a reward, for even without one your reward on this earth is already great: your spiritual joy, which only the righteous find. Fear neither the noble nor the powerful, but be wise and always gracious. Know the measure, know the proper time; learn this. And when you are alone, pray. Love to throw yourself upon the earth and kiss it. Kiss the earth and love it tirelessly, insatiably; love everyone, love everything, seek this rapture and ecstasy. Wet the earth with the tears of your joy and love these tears of yours. Do not be ashamed of this ecstasy; cherish it, for it is a gift of God, a great one, given not to many but to the chosen.

### i) On Hell and Hellfire: A Mystical Reflection

<!-- B06-C03-P017 -->
Fathers and teachers, I ask myself: “What is hell?” I reason thus: “The suffering of being unable to love any longer.” Once, in infinite existence, measurable by neither time nor space, a certain spiritual being was given, through its appearance on earth, the ability to say to itself: “I am, and I love.” Once, only once, it was given a moment of active, living love, and for that purpose earthly life was given to it, and with that life its seasons and allotted times. And what happened? This fortunate being rejected the priceless gift, failed to value it, did not love it, looked upon it with mockery, and remained unfeeling. Such a being, having now departed from the earth, sees Abraham’s bosom[^b06-c03-n001] and speaks with Abraham, as we are shown in the parable of the rich man and Lazarus; it gazes upon paradise and can ascend to the Lord, but is tormented precisely because it will ascend to the Lord without having loved, will come into contact with those who loved after having scorned their love. For it sees clearly and now says to itself: “Now I have knowledge, and though I thirst to love, there will no longer be any labor in my love, nor any sacrifice, for earthly life is over, and Abraham will not come with so much as a drop of living water—that is, the renewed gift of earthly life, the former life of action—to cool the flame of my thirst for spiritual love, with which I now burn, having scorned it on earth. Life is no more, and there shall be time no longer! Gladly as I would give my life for others, I can no longer do so, for the life that could have been offered as a sacrifice of love has passed, and now there is a gulf between that life and this existence.”

They speak of a material flame in hell: I do not examine this mystery, and I fear it, but I think that if there were a material flame, they would truly rejoice in it, for I imagine that in physical torment they would forget, if only for a moment, the more terrible torment of the spirit. Nor can this spiritual torment be taken from them, for it is not outside them but within. And even if it could be taken away, I think they would become still more unhappy. For even if the righteous in paradise, beholding their torments, forgave them and called them to themselves in infinite love, by that very act they would only increase their torments still further, for they would kindle in them still more fiercely the flame of thirst for a love in return, an active, grateful love, which is no longer possible.

Yet in the timidity of my heart I think that the very awareness of this impossibility would at last serve to ease their suffering: for, accepting the love of the righteous without being able to return it, in this submission and in this act of humility they will at last find something like a form of that active love they scorned on earth, and something like an act akin to it… I regret, my brothers and friends, that I do not know how to say this clearly.

But woe to those who have destroyed themselves on earth, woe to suicides! I think there can be no one more unhappy than they. We are told it is a sin to pray to God for them, and outwardly the Church seems even to reject them, but in the secrecy of my soul I think one might pray for these too. Surely Christ will not be angry at love. I have prayed for them inwardly all my life, I confess this to you, fathers and teachers, and still pray for them every day.

<!-- B06-C03-P018 -->
Oh, there are some who have remained proud and fierce even in hell, despite their indisputable knowledge and their contemplation of irrefutable truth; there are terrible beings who have joined themselves wholly to Satan and his proud spirit. For them hell is now voluntary and insatiable; they are willing sufferers. For they have cursed themselves by cursing God and life. They feed upon their malicious pride as though a starving man in the desert began to suck his own blood from his own body. But they are insatiable forever and ever, and reject forgiveness; they curse God, who calls to them. They cannot behold the living God without hatred, and demand that the God of life cease to be, that God destroy himself and all his creation. And they will burn in the fire of their anger eternally, thirsting for death and nonexistence. But they will not receive death.

<!-- B06-C03-P019 -->
Here ends the manuscript of Alexei Fyodorovich Karamazov. I repeat: it is incomplete and fragmentary. The biographical material, for example, covers only the elder’s early youth. As for his teachings and opinions, things evidently said at different times and for different reasons have been brought together as though into a single whole. Exactly what the elder said in these last hours of his life has not been established; we are given only a sense of the spirit and character of this conversation too, when it is set beside the earlier teachings included in Alexei Fyodorovich’s manuscript.

The elder’s death, however, did indeed come quite unexpectedly. For although everyone who had gathered in his room that last evening fully understood that his death was near, they still could not have imagined it would come so suddenly. On the contrary, as I have already noted above, seeing him that night apparently so vigorous and talkative, his friends were even convinced that his health had improved noticeably, if only for a little while. Even five minutes before his death, as they afterward related with astonishment, nothing could yet have been foreseen. He suddenly felt what seemed to be a very sharp pain in his chest, turned pale, and pressed his hands tightly to his heart. Everyone then rose and hurried toward him; but, though suffering, he still looked at them with a smile, quietly lowered himself from his armchair to the floor, and knelt down. Then he bowed face down to the earth, stretched out his arms, and, as though in joyful rapture, kissing the earth and praying—as he himself had taught—quietly and joyfully gave his soul to God.

News of his death immediately spread through the hermitage and reached the monastery. Those closest to the newly departed, and those whose office required it, began preparing his body according to the ancient rite, while all the brethren gathered in the monastery’s main church. And even before dawn, according to what was later rumored, news of the newly departed had reached the town. By morning almost the whole town was talking about the event, and a great many townspeople began streaming toward the monastery. But we shall speak of this in the next book, and for now will only add in advance that before even a day had passed, something happened so unexpected to everyone, and in the impression it made upon the monastery and the town seemingly so strange, alarming, and bewildering, that even now, after so many years, our town retains the liveliest memory of that day, so alarming to so many…
